# NASA Mars Reference Mission Pressurized Rover and Habitat Rendering (`cameron-mars-reference-mission.jpg`)

## Overview
A color 3D conceptual rendering from a Mars Reference Mission design study (noted in the blog as funded by James Cameron, posted February 1, 2004), depicting a multi-module, wheeled Martian surface vehicle and habitat train parked in profile on the rusty-orange surface of Mars.

## Vehicle & Scene Description
The vehicle assembly is shown in a side-profile view facing right, resting on a flat, bright orange-ochre Martian plain beneath a dusty peach-and-tan sky:
- **Rear Spherical Module (Left)**: A large, spherical pressurized module or inflatable airlock/storage pod painted black on its upper hemisphere and white with a grid-like structural pattern and small dark rectangular ports on its lower hemisphere. It connects to the central module via a cylindrical docking collar with a blue ring.
- **Central Cylindrical Habitat/Trailer Module (Center)**: An elongated horizontal cylinder with rounded, hemispherical end caps.
  - The lower half is white/light gray with a textured grid of panels and small dark vents or windows.
  - The upper half is dark black with a vertical white stripe in the middle.
  - Along the side of the black upper section is a NASA meatball logo followed by a horizontal row of colorful international flags (including the United States, Japan, European nations, and others).
- **Front Tractor / Cab Unit (Right)**: Connected to the front docking adapter of the central cylinder is a four-wheeled tractor or rover cab unit.
  - Painted in dark blue, black, and metallic gray with white fenders over its two visible pairs of large, dark, rugged wheels.
  - Topped by an angled, flat gray solar array or thermal radiator roof panel and a tall, thin vertical communications whip antenna extending straight up to the top edge of the frame.
