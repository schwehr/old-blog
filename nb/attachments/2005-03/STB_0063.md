# Coastal Cliff Landslide and Talus Cone at Black's Beach (`STB_0063.JPG`)

## Overview
A wide-angle color photograph taken by Becca on March 12, 2005, showing a large, fresh coastal bluff landslide and conical talus apron at **Black's Beach** in La Jolla / San Diego, California, cordoned off by yellow caution tape along the sandy beach at the base of the cliff.

## Geological & Scene Description
- **Stratified Sea Cliff**: A tall, steep coastal bluff composed of horizontally bedded, tan and light-gray sedimentary rock layers (Eocene Ardath Shale / Scripps Formation and overlying units).
  - The upper rim of the cliff shows patches of green coastal scrub vegetation against a bright, overcast white sky.
  - In the upper-center of the cliff face, a recessed alcoved scar marks the source zone where rock and sediment detached and funneled down a narrow chute.
- **Conical Talus Fan (Center)**:
  - Beneath the central chute, a massive, symmetrical cone of loose, tan-brown sand, silt, and angular rock debris fans out across the lower half of the cliff face.
  - Coarser cobbles and small boulders have rolled to the bottom apron of the cone, forming a gravelly toe along the back of the beach, while finer-grained sediment forms the smooth upper apex of the cone.
- **Beach & Safety Perimeter (Foreground)**:
  - The flat, damp gray-tan sand of Black's Beach occupies the bottom third of the frame, with faint tire tracks or footprints in the immediate foreground and scattered angular rock fragments resting on the sand in front of the slide toe.
  - A continuous line of **bright yellow caution tape** supported by thin wooden stakes runs horizontally across the beach in front of the landslide debris fan to warn beachgoers away from the unstable cliff base.
