# Cropping the Assembled Core X-Ray Composite in Photoshop (`Picture02.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of **Adobe Photoshop CS** on Mac OS X showing the full vertical extent of the assembled sediment core X-ray composite (`bp04-5g...`) selected with the Rectangular Marquee Tool and about to be cropped via the `Image -> Crop` menu command to reduce memory usage while preserving black scratch space to the left of the core.

## Interface & Menu Details
- **Menu Bar & Dropdown**: At the top of the screen, the Mac OS X application bar displays the Apple icon, `Photoshop`, `File`, `Edit`, `Image` (highlighted), `Layer`, `Select`, and `Filter`. The **Image** menu is open with **Crop** highlighted:
  - `Mode ▸`
  - `Adjustments ▸`
  - `Duplicate...`
  - `Apply Image...`
  - `Calculations...`
  - `Image Size...`
  - `Canvas Size...`
  - `Pixel Aspect Ratio ▸`
  - `Rotate Canvas ▸`
  - `Crop` (selected)
  - `Trim...`
  - `Reveal All`
  - `Trap...` (grayed out)
- **Options Bar & Tool Palette**: Below the menu bar, the Rectangular Marquee Tool options show `Feather: 0 px`. On the left, the full two-column Photoshop tool palette is visible with the **Rectangular Marquee Tool** selected at the top left.

## Document Window & Core Composite
- **Rulers**: The document window `bp04-5g...` shows a horizontal ruler from `0` to `1000`+ pixels and a vertical ruler extending from `0` down past `9000` pixels (`0`, `1000`, `2000`, `3000`, `4000`, `5000`, `6000`, `7000`, `8000`, `9000`).
- **X-Ray Strip & Marquee Selection**:
  - Nine overlapping vertical grayscale X-ray plate segments are aligned into a continuous vertical column on the right side of the black canvas, with tiny white depth and section labels along the left side of the core.
  - Horizontal guide lines cross the image at roughly `y = 3450`, `y = 5500`, and `y = 7550`.
  - A dashed marching-ants rectangular marquee selection encloses the full vertical length of the core (from `y = 0` to `y ≈ 9450`) and extends from `x ≈ 500` on the left to the right edge of the core liner (`x ≈ 1600`), leaving black workspace to the left of the X-ray strip.
