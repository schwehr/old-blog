# Duplicating the Flattened Background Layer in Photoshop (`Picture04.png`)

## Overview
A close-up screenshot of the **Layers** palette in **Adobe Photoshop CS** on Mac OS X showing the user dragging the flattened `Background` layer onto the **Create a new layer** icon at the bottom of the palette to create a working duplicate layer while keeping the original `Background` layer as a backup.

## Interface & Layer Details
- **Palette Header**: Shows the `Layers` tab selected alongside `Channels` and `Paths`, with blending mode `Normal`, `Opacity: 100%`, `Lock` toggles, and `Fill: 100%` (all grayed out while `Background` is locked).
- **Layer Entry**: A single layer named `Background` (in italics) is visible, with its eye visibility icon and paintbrush active-editing icon on the left, a vertical thumbnail preview of the tall core X-ray strip, and a padlock icon on the right.
- **Drag-to-Duplicate Action**: Near the bottom center of the palette, a dotted rectangular drag outline represents the `Background` layer being dragged onto the middle button at the bottom bar—the **Create a new layer** icon (a square with a folded page corner), flanked by the **Create new fill or adjustment layer** icon on the left and the **Delete layer** (trash can) icon on the right.
