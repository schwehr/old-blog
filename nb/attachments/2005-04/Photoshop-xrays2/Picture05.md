# Selecting Auto Levels on a Core X-Ray Region in Photoshop (`Picture05.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of **Adobe Photoshop CS** on Mac OS X showing an interior rectangular selection of a sediment core X-ray plate (excluding the outer core liner and alignment pegs) on the `Background copy` working layer, with the user selecting `Image -> Adjustments -> Auto Levels` (`⇧⌘L`).

## Menu & Submenu Details
- **Menu Bar**: `Image` is selected in the top application bar, opening the main dropdown and the **Adjustments** submenu:
  - `Levels... ⌘L`
  - `Auto Levels ⇧⌘L` (highlighted in blue)
  - `Auto Contrast ⌥⇧⌘L`
  - `Auto Color ⇧⌘B` (grayed out)
  - `Curves... ⌘M`
  - `Color Balance... ⌘B` (grayed out)
  - `Brightness/Contrast...`
  - `Hue/Saturation... ⌘U` (grayed out)
  - `Desaturate ⇧⌘U` (grayed out)
  - `Match Color...` (grayed out)
  - `Replace Color...`
  - `Selective Color...` (grayed out)
  - `Channel Mixer...` (grayed out)
  - `Gradient Map...`
  - `Photo Filter...` (grayed out)
  - `Shadow/Highlight...`
  - `Invert ⌘I`
  - `Equalize`
  - `Threshold...`
  - `Posterize...`
  - `Variations...` (grayed out)

## Canvas & Layers Panel
- **Document Canvas (Left)**: Shows the grayscale X-ray of core section `bp04-5ga-s1` above `bp04-5ga-s2`, with white labels `20cm` and `bp04-5ga-s2` visible on the black background to the left of the core. A vertical rectangular marching-ants marquee selection is drawn inside the core X-ray, avoiding the darker liner walls and circular peg shadows.
- **Layers Palette (Right)**: Shows two layers:
  1. `Background copy` (highlighted in blue as the active layer, `Normal`, `Opacity: 100%`, `Fill: 100%`)
  2. `Background` (locked original layer underneath)
