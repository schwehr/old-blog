# Researchers Using the CoreWall Visualization System at EVL, UIC (`evl-corewall.jpg`)

## Overview
A color photograph showing two researchers seated in front of the **CoreWall** high-resolution multi-monitor stratigraphic and sediment core visualization workstation at the **Electronic Visualization Laboratory (EVL)** at the University of Illinois at Chicago, referenced in the August 24, 2005 blog post *"CoreWall"*.

## Scene Description
- **People (Left Foreground)**:
  - **Far Left**: A man with dark hair and glasses wearing a dark navy/black jacket, seen in right-side profile smiling toward the high-resolution display wall.
  - **Center-Left Foreground**: A man with short light-brown hair wearing a maroon/burgundy collared shirt, seen from behind and to the right, resting his left hand (with a wristwatch) on the edge of the workstation table while holding a printed handout containing two small color photographs of display systems.
- **Desktop Monitors (Lower Row)**:
  - Three side-by-side black flat-panel LCD monitors sit on the desk directly in front of the researchers.
  - They display synchronized horizontal sediment core imagery strips along the bottom, bounded by bright orange horizontal guide lines, with orange line-graph plots of physical properties (such as magnetic susceptibility or gamma-ray attenuation density) plotted on a horizontal grid across the middle of the screens.
- **Tiled Display Wall (Background)**:
  - Behind the desk monitors rises a large tiled matrix of LCD panels (4 columns visible across by 3+ rows high) with thin black bezels.
  - The leftmost three columns show high-resolution horizontal core-section photographs and data tracks on a dark background, while the rightmost column displays high-contrast grayscale aerial/satellite or microscopic imagery of icy/rocky terrain.
