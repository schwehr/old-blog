# Lightning Striking Imperial Beach Pier Captured by ROADNet Camera (`SCC_IBpier_cam.10_17_09-small.png`)

## Overview
A dramatic nighttime webcam photograph (600×450 pixels, downsampled from a 2048×1536 original) captured by an automated **ROADNet / HPWREN** camera at 10:17:09 UTC on September 20, 2005 (day 263), showing a twin cloud-to-ground lightning strike hitting the **Imperial Beach Pier** in San Diego County, California (shared by Rob Newman at Scripps Institution of Oceanography in the September 20, 2005 blog post *"lightning in SD hits a pier"*).

## Scene Description
- **Lightning Bolts (Upper Center)**:
  - Two brilliant, blindingly white main lightning channels descend vertically from a glowing storm cloud at the top center, surrounded by an intricate web of jagged purple-white branching leaders and streamers across the night sky.
  - The left bolt terminates directly on the flagpole/roof peak of the restaurant building at the far western end of the Imperial Beach Pier, while the thicker right bolt strikes the ocean surface just beyond the pier just to the right of the end structure.
- **Imperial Beach Pier (Middle)**:
  - The long wooden pier extends diagonally from the middle-right edge out into the Pacific Ocean toward the center-left, supported by rows of dark pilings.
  - A line of warm yellow-orange sodium-vapor pier lamps illuminates the deck and casts vertical golden reflections across the wet sand and shallow surf below.
- **Ocean Surf & Beach (Lower Half)**:
  - Dark parallel wave crests and whitewater surf bands roll onto the wet foreshore, reflecting both the brilliant blue-white flash of the lightning in the center and the warm orange glow of the pier lights on the right.
- **Sensor & Lens Artifacts**:
  - The image exhibits noticeable salt-spray graininess and scattered red, blue, and white hot-pixel speckles across the dark sky and beach foreground from the long nighttime exposure and coastal salt accumulation on the camera lens.
