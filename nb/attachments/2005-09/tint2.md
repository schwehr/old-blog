# Cutout Layer of Sub-Bottom Seismic Profile Before Color Overlay (`tint2.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot from Adobe Photoshop showing an isolated cutout layer of a black-and-white seismic reflection profile over a transparent checkerboard canvas. This image is the second step in a three-part tutorial demonstrating how to use the magnetic lasso tool and layer styles in Photoshop to selectively tint and highlight a sub-bottom geological region of a seismic section.

## Scene & Layer Details
- **Transparent Background**: The upper-left diagonal half of the frame displays Photoshop's standard gray-and-white checkerboard pattern, indicating that the overlying seismic strata and water column have been removed from this duplicated layer while the background layer's visibility is turned off.
- **Isolated Seismic Wedge**: The lower-right diagonal half contains the remaining high-contrast black-and-white seismic reflection data, rising steeply from the bottom-left corner toward the upper-right margin.
- **Internal Structure**: Within the cutout wedge, steeply dipping, sub-parallel reflectors trace the upper slope and an internal fault or unconformity boundary on the right, transitioning into more chaotic, mottled acoustic backscatter in the deeper core and lower-left toe.
- **Canvas Borders**: Thin gray-and-white checkerboard strips are also visible along the very bottom and right edges of the image canvas.
