# SEM Backscattered Electron Micrograph of Pyrite Framboids (`69_BSE_10kx_4-small.png`)

## Overview
A high-magnification (5000x) Backscattered Electron (BSE) scanning electron microscope (SEM) image of dried marine/estuarine mud (sample `69`) captured on the FEI Quanta 600 E-SEM in low-vacuum mode. The image showcases several bright, spherical pyrite framboids—raspberry-like aggregates of densely packed microcrystals typical of reducing sediments—nestled within darker clay and silt grains.

## Microstructural Features
- **Pyrite Framboids**: Seven distinct bright white/light-gray spheroidal aggregates ranging from roughly `2 µm` to `9 µm` in diameter stand out with high atomic-number (`Z`) contrast against the darker aluminosilicate matrix:
  - **Lower-Left Cluster**: Three adjacent framboids tucked into a cavity—a large (~8 µm) framboid on the upper left with clearly resolved equant microcrystals, a ~5 µm sphere below it, and a slightly darker ~4 µm sphere to its right.
  - **Upper-Center Group**: A prominent, well-formed ~8 µm framboid near the top center flanked by a small ~2.5 µm bright sphere on its left, and another ~7 µm partially disaggregated framboid further to the upper right.
  - **Right-Edge Pair**: Two smaller rounded framboids (~3.5 µm and ~5 µm) resting in a recess near the upper-right margin.
- **Surrounding Matrix**: A large, rough-textured mineral grain occupies the lower-right quadrant, bounded on its left by a thin, dark, vertically oriented platy sheet (likely a phyllosilicate/mica flake seen edge-on) that separates it from the lower-left framboid pocket.
