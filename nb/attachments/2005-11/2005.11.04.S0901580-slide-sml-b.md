# Side-by-Side Comparison of Martian Landslide in Ophir Chasma (`2005.11.04.S0901580-slide-sml-b.png`)

## Overview
A side-by-side grayscale comparison image based on Mars Global Surveyor (MGS) Mars Orbiter Camera (MOC) image `S0901580` (MOC Release No. `MOC2-1271`, 4 November 2005), showing a landslide deposit on a valley wall in Ophir Chasma on Mars. The left panel shows the original unannotated satellite image, while the right panel overlays a white outline and directional arrow interpreting the landslide lobe and transport direction.

## Geological & Visual Details
- **Left Panel (Original MOC Image)**:
  - **Source Alcove & Scarp (Upper Center)**: A semi-circular, amphitheater-shaped alcove eroded into layered bedrock along the upper slope, surrounded by fields of fine light-toned aeolian ripples/dunes across the top plateau.
  - **Landslide Apron / Talus Slope (Center to Bottom)**: Below the alcove, mass-wasting debris fans downward across a smooth, intermediate-toned slope that narrows toward the bottom-left into a V-shaped chute bordered by darker, rough-textured rocky outcrops.
- **Right Panel (Annotated Interpretation)**:
  - Identical grayscale satellite image overlaid with hand-drawn white vector lines:
    - **White Perimeter Outline**: Traces the boundary of the interpreted landslide feature, encompassing the upper cirque-like headwall scarp and extending down-slope around the main debris apron to its lobate toe just above the dark rocky exposures.
    - **White Directional Arrow**: Drawn near the center of the outlined apron, pointing upslope (`NNE`) toward the base of the headwall alcove (or indicating the axis of the slide).
