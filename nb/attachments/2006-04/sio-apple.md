# Scripps Institution of Oceanography Visualization Wall (`sio-apple.png`)

## Overview
A promotional photograph from an Apple Science profile article (*"Scripps Institution of Oceanography - Seeing the Big Picture"*) showing two researchers at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO) examining geophysical Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR) / fault deformation imagery on both an Apple PowerBook G4 laptop and a large 12-panel tiled LCD display wall (the "HiPerWall" / visualization wall).

## Scene Description
- **People**:
  - In the left foreground, a man (**Atul Nayak**) sits at a wooden conference/work table wearing a dark navy polo shirt over a white t-shirt, typing on or operating a silver 15-inch or 17-inch **Apple PowerBook G4** laptop.
  - Standing next to him and leaning forward slightly is a woman (**Debi Kilb**) with long sandy-blonde hair wearing a light gray long-sleeved shirt, looking intently toward the large tiled display wall on the right.
- **Display Wall & Imagery**:
  - Mounted on the wood-paneled wall to the right is a **4 × 3 grid (12 panels total)** of thin-bezel flat-panel LCD monitors framed in dark gray/black metal.
  - Across the 12 screens—and mirrored on the PowerBook's screen on the desk—is a vibrant false-color geophysical visualization (from Bridget Smith's thesis work on San Andreas / Southern California fault deformation and InSAR fringes).
  - The visualization shows a shaded-relief topographic map overlaid with rainbow-colored interference fringes (concentric bands of magenta, blue, cyan, green, yellow, and red/orange) radiating around a fault trace running horizontally/diagonally across the scene.
- **Room & Background Details**:
  - The room features warm vertical wood-plank paneling below the display wall and along the back wall.
  - On the back left wall hangs a large framed world physical/bathymetric map showing the continents and ocean basins.
  - To the right of the world map is a dark vertical scientific poster with yellow and white header text and small figures.
  - Next to the poster is a wooden door with a small black-and-white photograph taped or mounted near the top.
