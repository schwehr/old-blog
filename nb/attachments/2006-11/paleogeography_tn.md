# Middle Triassic (240 Ma) Paleogeography Globes Thumbnail (`paleogeography_tn.jpg`)

## Overview
A thumbnail image showing two side-by-side 3D paleogeographic globe reconstructions of Earth during the **Middle Triassic (240 Ma)**, linked from San Diego State University's (SDSU) Google Earth KMZ collection (`paleogeography.kmz`, based on Ron Blakey's paleogeographic reconstructions).

## Visual Details
- **Background**: Solid muted slate-teal background.
- **Left Globe (`Equatorial West view`)**:
  - Displays the western margin of the supercontinent **Pangaea** and the eastern **Panthalassic Ocean**.
  - Vast tan and light-brown arid continental interiors span across the northern and southern hemispheres, bisected near the equator by a green and brown vegetated/mountainous belt.
  - Light cyan shallow epicontinental seas and continental shelves border the western coastline and form an embayment near the northern polar region, transitioning outward into dark blue deep ocean.
- **Right Globe (`Equatorial Tethys view`)**:
  - Displays the crescent-shaped **Tethys Ocean** opening to the east between the northern (Laurasia) and southern (Gondwana) arms of Pangaea.
  - Extensive bright cyan shallow marine shelves, carbonate platforms, and island arcs wind through the western and central Tethys, surrounding microcontinents and peninsulas rendered in tan and olive-green.
  - Deep ocean basins are colored rich dark blue.
- **Labels**:
  - Bottom center (in bold black sans-serif text): `240 Ma Middle Triassic`.
  - Bottom left: `Equatorial West view`.
  - Bottom right-center: `Equatorial Tethys view`.
