# High-Altitude Perspective of GPS Satellite Coverage Cones in Google Earth (`ge-gps-3.png`)

## Overview
A far-field **Google Earth** space perspective showing the Earth in the lower right and multiple high-altitude **GPS satellites** in medium Earth orbit (~20,200 km altitude), each projecting a 3D cone of grey line-of-sight rays and a central red nadir vector down to the globe.

## Visualization Details
- **Space & Earth:** Against a black starfield background, the Earth globe is positioned in the lower right quadrant, displaying North, Central, and South America outlined in yellow.
- **GPS Satellites & Signal Cones:**
  - Three distinct GPS satellite vertices are visible high above the Earth in the upper-left and upper-center portions of the frame, with a fourth red vector extending to the middle left.
  - From each satellite vertex, dozens of thin grey-blue rays fan out in a 3D conical bundle that tangentially encompasses the visible hemisphere of the Earth, illustrating the broad terrestrial footprint of each GPS satellite.
  - A thick bright-red line runs down the center of each cone from the satellite directly to its sub-satellite point (nadir) on the Earth's surface, while additional red nadir lines radiate outward from the right and bottom sides of the globe toward other satellites in the constellation.
