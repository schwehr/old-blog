# Low-Earth-Orbit Iridium Communications Satellites in Google Earth (`ge-iridium.png`)

## Overview
An oblique horizon screenshot from **Google Earth** visualizing low-Earth-orbit (LEO) **Iridium** communications satellites (`IRIDIUM 23 [+]`) and their 3D coverage cones over eastern North America and the North Atlantic Ocean.

## Scene & Visualization Details
- **Oblique Earth Horizon:** Looking northward across the curved horizon of the Earth against a black star-speckled sky. Below are Hudson Bay (left), Quebec and Labrador (center), the Gulf of St. Lawrence and Newfoundland (right), and the North Atlantic Ocean, outlined in bright yellow coastline vectors.
- **Satellites & Labels:**
  - Four low-altitude satellites are visible just above the Earth's limb:
    - Left: A satellite icon (grey cylindrical bus with red-and-white checkered solar panels) over Hudson Bay.
    - Upper left-center: A distant satellite cone vertex near the horizon.
    - Upper right: A satellite icon high over the Labrador Sea / Greenland.
    - Middle right (foreground): A larger satellite icon over the North Atlantic off Newfoundland, explicitly labeled in bold white text: `IRIDIUM 23 [+]`.
- **Coverage Cones & Nadir Lines:**
  - Each satellite projects a wide, tent-like 3D fan of thin grey lines down to the Earth's surface, forming circular/elliptical coverage footprints on the globe.
  - A thick bright-red vertical line drops straight down from each satellite icon to its sub-satellite ground point (in Hudson Bay, the Labrador Sea, and east of Newfoundland).
