# GeoMapApp Seafloor Photographs in Google Earth (`geomapapp-photos.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of Google Earth showing the Southern California Bight (from Los Angeles to San Diego) with the GeoMapApp bathymetry overlay and georeferenced historical seafloor photographs (`LDEO_Seafloor_photos.kmz`), featuring an open pop-up balloon with a black-and-white underwater photograph of a scuba diver in La Jolla Submarine Canyon.

## Map & Placemark Details
- **Geographic Features**:
  - Onshore Southern California is visible with yellow highway lines and highway shields for US-101 (`101`) and Interstates `210`, `605`, `105`, `110`, `405` (around Los Angeles) and `8` and `15` (around San Diego).
  - Offshore shaded-relief bathymetry shows the continental borderland basins, ridges, and submarine canyons, with yellow coastline outlines and cyan labels for `Santa Catalina Island` and `San Clemente Island`.
- **Placemarks**: Three yellow pushpin icons are clustered along the La Jolla / San Diego coastline and submarine canyon system at the bottom right, labeled in white text:
  - `Chapter 2, p. 112`
  - `Chapter 7, p. 236`
  - `Chapter 8, p. 288`

## Information Balloon (`Chapter 8, p. 288`)
The pop-up balloon anchored to the `Chapter 8, p. 288` pushpin off La Jolla contains:
- **Title**: `Chapter 8, p. 288` (from Robert F. Dill's 1964 dissertation on submarine erosion in Scripps and La Jolla Submarine Canyons).
- **Link**: `click to view full size` (blue underlined hyperlink).
- **Embedded Photograph**: A vertical black-and-white underwater photograph showing a steep, overhanging rock and cohesive mud/silt wall on the right side of the submarine canyon. A scuba diver wearing a single tank and wetsuit swims in the open water at the lower left, looking toward the undercut canyon ledge, while a larger fish swims near the bottom right and smaller fish are silhouetted in the sunlit water above.
