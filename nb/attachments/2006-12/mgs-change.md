# Mars Global Surveyor Crater Gully Change Comparison (`mgs-change.png`)

## Overview
A side-by-side before-and-after satellite image comparison from NASA's **Mars Global Surveyor (MGS)** Mars Orbiter Camera (NASA image `PIA09027`), showing a newly formed light-toned deposit inside a Martian impact crater gully between **August 1999** and **September 2005**.

## Left Panel (`August 1999`)
- **Label**: `August 1999` in white sans-serif text at the upper left.
- **Scale Bar**: A white horizontal scale bar at the bottom left labeled `300 m`.
- **Geological Features**: Shows the steep, rocky upper rim and alcove wall of an impact crater in the Terra Sirenum region of Mars running diagonally from the middle-left to the upper right. Numerous narrow, braided erosional gullies and debris chutes descend southeastward down the crater wall toward the lower right, all exhibiting uniform medium-gray surface tones.

## Right Panel (`September 2005`)
- **Labels & Indicators**:
  - `September 2005` in white text at the upper left.
  - `North` with a downward-pointing white arrow in the upper right corner (indicating that south is toward the top-left and north is toward the bottom-right).
  - `new deposit` in white text near the bottom center-right, with a white arrow pointing to a bright streak.
- **Surface Change**: Along one of the channels descending from the central rocky alcove, a distinct, bright, light-toned finger-like deposit with digitate terminal lobes has appeared on the lower apron slope—interpreted by NASA scientists as evidence of recent liquid water flow or a dry granular slope failure exposing lighter underlying material between 1999 and 2005.
