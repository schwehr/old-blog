# Paul Haeberli Multifocus Depth-of-Field Composite Comparison (`haeberli-multifocus.png`)

## Overview
A three-panel horizontal grayscale comparison image from Paul Haeberli's *"A Multifocus Method for Controlling Depth of Field"* (`graficaobscura.com`), demonstrating how images captured at different focus distances can be combined into a single all-in-focus composite image.

## Panel-by-Panel Description
Each panel shows the same tabletop still-life arrangement: a small glass bottle with a cork stopper and dark wax seal lying on its side in the foreground (with handwritten-style German label text including `gelb-rot...`, `Orangenblüten...`, `mit Farbpigment...`, `ABRAXAS CH-4204...`), an upright corked ink/pigment bottle in the background on the left, and a large machined metal helical gear resting at an angle in the background on the right.

1. **Left Panel (Foreground Focus)**:
   - The camera is focused on the foreground bottle lying on its side. The ornate cursive lettering on its label, the texture of the cork stopper, and the dark wax seal are sharp and legible.
   - Both the upright bottle in the left background and the metal gear on the right are noticeably blurred and out of focus.
2. **Center Panel (Background Focus)**:
   - The camera is focused on the background objects. The upright bottle on the left (including its cork and label) and the individual teeth and metallic sheen of the gear on the right are crisp and sharply defined.
   - The foreground bottle lying in the front is blurred and out of focus.
3. **Right Panel (Multifocus Composite)**:
   - The combined result produced by merging the sharp regions from the multi-focus source images.
   - Both the foreground bottle (cork, wax seal, and cursive label text) and the background objects (the upright bottle and the teeth of the metal gear) are simultaneously in sharp focus, achieving an extended depth of field.
