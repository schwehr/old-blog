# Frost and Ice Crystals Growing on a Window Pane (`ice-crystals.png`)

## Overview
A close-up macro photograph of feathery, dendritic ice crystals (window frost) growing across a glass window pane on a -2°F morning on January 26, 2007.

## Visual Details
- **Crystal Morphology**:
  - Interlocking fern-like and star-shaped crystalline branches radiate outward from multiple nucleation points across the glass surface.
  - Individual crystal plumes exhibit delicate, parallel needle-like side branches (dendrites) at sharp angles, forming triangular fans, curved plumes, and multi-pointed stars.
  - In the lower-right corner, the frost transitions into a denser, more granular cluster of smaller overlapping ice rosettes.
- **Color & Lighting**:
  - The translucent ice crystals are illuminated in cool shades of pale blue, periwinkle, and silvery white against a dark navy-black background outside the window.
  - A small warm-toned reflection or speck of light is visible near the middle-right portion of the frame.
