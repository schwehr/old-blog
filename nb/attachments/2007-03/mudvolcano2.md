# Close-Up of Submarine Mud Volcano Flow Channel and Ridges (`mudvolcano2.png`)

## Overview
A close-up underwater ROV photograph (`mudvolcano2.png`) looking directly down at the active mud flow channel on the flank of a submarine mud volcano in the Gulf of Mexico, highlighting its ropy, pahoehoe-like surface texture and marginal levees.

## Scene Description
- **Central Mud Channel**: Running diagonally from the upper left to the lower center-right is a distinct channel (approximately 50–150 cm wide) filled with flowing mud. The surface of the mud exhibits curved, festooned, and corrugated ridges (resembling ropy pahoehoe lava flows) formed by the viscous movement of the mud downslope.
- **Channel Margins (Levees)**: Smooth, lighter-colored raised levee rims sharply bound both the left and right edges of the active flow channel, separating the moving mud from the surrounding seabed.
- **Surrounding Seafloor**: Outside the levees on the left and right, the seafloor consists of finer, bioturbated or pitted bluish-gray sediment with several small dark rocks, cavities, or organisms visible near the far left edge and along the right margin.
