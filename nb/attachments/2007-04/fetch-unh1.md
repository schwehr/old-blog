# Roland and Robert Working on the Fetch 3.5 AUV After Tank Tests (`fetch-unh1.jpg`)

## Overview
A high-angle indoor photograph of researchers Roland Arsenault and Robert working on the **Fetch 3.5** Autonomous Underwater Vehicle (AUV) resting in its aluminum cradle on the wet concrete deck of the engineering tank facility at the University of New Hampshire (CCOM/JHC), taken on April 13, 2007.

## Scene Description
- **Fetch 3.5 AUV (Center)**:
  - The torpedo-shaped AUV sits inside a square-tube aluminum frame cradle. Its hull is white with a bright yellow dorsal section, a blue trim band behind the nose cone, and bright lime-green cylindrical side thrusters/floats mounted along the upper frame rails, flanked by white cylindrical buoyancy fenders.
  - The nose cone is removed or open at the front end, exposing black cables, connectors, and internal electronics, with a small rectangular status window atop the forward hull ring and a coiled beige rope tied around the nose.
- **Researchers**:
  - **Left**: A researcher (Roland) wearing a red zip-up hoodie over a gray sweatshirt and khaki trousers kneels beside the port side of the AUV. With his left hand, he holds a small clamshell handheld computer/PDA with a QWerty thumb keyboard resting atop the AUV's yellow hull, while his right hand adjusts wiring at the open nose cone.
  - **Right**: A second researcher (Robert) wearing glasses, a brown t-shirt, and blue jeans leans in from the starboard side. He holds a silver-and-black convertible tablet PC in his left arm while using a black stylus in his right hand to point at or adjust a component near the AUV's nose cone.
