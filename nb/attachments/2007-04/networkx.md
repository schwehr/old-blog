# NetworkX Graph Visualization of Connected Components (`networkx.png`)

## Overview
A network graph visualization illustrating the capabilities of **NetworkX**, a Python package for studying complex networks and graphs developed at Los Alamos National Laboratory, posted on April 20, 2007.

## Visual Layout & Structure
The rectangular graphic (bordered in gray on a white background) is divided into two distinct regions showing the connected components of a complex network:
- **Giant Component (Left Half)**:
  - A large, densely interconnected central core of bright red circular nodes linked by black edges.
  - Surrounding the dark-edged core is a sprawling halo of semi-transparent pink/light-red peripheral nodes and faint gray edges fanning outward in tree-like radial clusters, star bursts, and dense fan structures.
- **Small Disconnected Components (Right Half)**:
  - Packed neatly across the right half of the canvas are over a hundred small, isolated subgraphs (connected components of roughly 3 to 15 nodes each) representing rings, chains, trees, stars, and small cliques.
  - Each small component is color-coded by size or cluster type in vibrant rainbow hues—including dark blue, royal blue, cyan, teal, lime green, yellow, orange, bright red, and maroon—with circular nodes connected by thin gray-black edges.
