# Exposed Wooden Shipwreck Hull on Short Sands Beach, York, Maine (`shipwreck.jpg`)

## Overview
This image is a photograph (credited to Amy Phalon in the blog post) showing the weathered wooden ribs and hull framing of a historic **shipwreck** uncovered by an April 2007 nor'easter storm on **Short Sands Beach** in **York, Maine**.

## Scene Description
- **The Shipwreck (Foreground & Midground)**:
  - Viewed from a low angle near the bow/stern end, the curved outline of a wooden vessel's bottom hull and closely spaced futtocks/ribs protrudes from wet, pebble-strewn sand at low tide.
  - The dark brown, waterlogged timbers form an elongated boat-shaped oval stretching from the lower-right foreground toward the middle-left distance.
  - Shallow tidal water pools inside the center of the hull frame, reflecting the overcast sky, while coarse gravel, cobbles, and dark seaweed surround the timbers.
- **People on the Beach (Right Midground)**:
  - Three people are inspecting the beach to the right of the wreck: an adult in a dark blue jacket and light blue jeans bending slightly forward with an orange cap, a young child in a pink/purple jacket crouching near the pebbles, and another person in a bright blue jacket and dark pants standing further back toward the water.
  - Several smaller figures can be seen walking along the shoreline in the far distance.
- **Coastline & Background**:
  - Behind the flat intertidal beach, white surf breaks gently along the Atlantic shoreline on the right.
  - Along the upper-left and center horizon, coastal buildings and multi-story white and gray Victorian-style beachfront hotels and houses line the rocky seawall and headland of York Beach under an overcast white sky.
