# EarthNC Nautical Chart Quality Control Comparison in Google Earth (`earthnc-qc.png`)

## Overview
A close-up Google Earth screenshot showing EarthNC Electronic Navigational Chart (ENC) vector overlays superimposed on high-resolution satellite imagery of a waterfront pier in Norfolk, Virginia (`Lamberts Point Terminal Marine Hydraulics Pier LTS`), taken on May 8, 2007. The image illustrates a quality-control (QC) discrepancy between the charted pier geometry and the satellite photograph.

## Map & Chart Overlay Details
- **Central Feature & Orange Arrow**:
  - A long concrete pier extends diagonally from the upper-right shoreline toward the lower left, with a large gray naval/merchant vessel (featuring a helipad on its stern) moored along the northwest side of the pier.
  - An orange arrow near the center points directly to the red vector outline of the charted pier (`Lamberts Point Terminal Marine Hydraulics Pier LTS`), which terminates roughly halfway down the length of the actual pier visible in the satellite imagery—raising the question of whether the pier was extended after the chart survey or shortened after the aerial photograph was taken.
- **Adjacent Piers & Shoreline**:
  - In the upper left, a wide industrial pier (Lamberts Point coal pier) is outlined in bright yellow and green chart lines.
  - In the upper right, a paved terminal parking lot with parked cars connects to the root of the pier, with a small house icon in a yellow box in the top-right corner and another house icon on a bulkhead pier to the lower right.
- **Soundings & Navigational Aids**:
  - Depth soundings in meters/feet appear as colored numbers next to small crosshairs: white `30`, red `2`, `1`, `0`, `1`, and yellow `5`, `4`.
  - In the lower left: a yellow `4s` label, a white square caution icon with a red exclamation mark (`!`), the white text label `Lamberts Point Terminal Marine Hydraulics Pier LTS`, and a green can buoy icon labeled `"33"` and `4s` in green text along a cyan depth contour line.
  - Small white circles markcharted pilings/dolphins along the water between the piers, accompanied by blue double-wave seabed/tidal symbols.
