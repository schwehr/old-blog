# Matt Configuring the 11-Panel Tiled Display Wall at CCOM (`matt-tiled-display.png`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken at the Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping (CCOM) at the University of New Hampshire in May 2007, showing consultant **Matt** seated at a workstation desk next to a large mobile tiled LCD display wall being configured with the Rocks Clusters ("Viz Roll") Linux distribution.

## Scene Description
- **Tiled Display Wall (Left & Center)**:
  - A custom freestanding aluminum extrusion frame (80/20 T-slot framing) mounted on heavy-duty locking caster wheels holds a 3-row by 4-column grid of large widescreen flat-panel LCD monitors with silver bezels.
  - Eleven of the twelve monitor slots are populated (all four displays in the bottom and middle rows, and three displays in the top row—top-left, top-center-right, and top-right), while the second slot from the left on the top row is empty, revealing the aluminum mounting rails, power outlets, and cabling behind the array.
  - All screens are currently powered off or dark, reflecting the room lighting across their matte black panels, and a white power cord lies coiled on the dark shiny floor beneath the frame.
- **Person & Workstation (Right)**:
  - Matt is seated in a rolling office chair facing a gray desk on the far right, with his right hand resting on a computer keyboard. He is wearing a dark patterned crew-neck sweater and dark green/olive trousers.
  - On the wall behind him hangs a large dry-erase whiteboard covered in orange/red marker diagrams, flowcharts, and handwritten notes.
