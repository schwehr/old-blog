# Underwater Photograph of the Schooner *Paul Palmer* Shipwreck (`paulpalmer3c.jpg`)

## Overview
An underwater photograph showing fish swimming around the encrusted steam windlass / winch machinery on the wreck of the five-masted coal schooner *Paul Palmer* in the NOAA Gerry E. Studds Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary.

## Scene Description
- **Shipwreck Machinery**: Running diagonally across the center of the frame is a massive, heavily corroded and bio-fouled iron cylindrical mechanism with large geared wheels and drums (the vessel's steam windlass/winch). The metal surfaces are covered in orange, rust-red, yellow, and white encrusting sponges, tunicates, and colonial marine organisms.
- **Marine Life**:
  - Four silver-white fish with dark vertical bars (scup or cunner/tautog) swim in a loose horizontal group just above the top edge of the central gear wheel against the blue-green water column.
  - Several reddish-orange and brownish wrasse-like fish (cunner, *Tautogolabrus adspersus*) swim in the foreground among the dense beds of tan and olive-green branching bryozoans/hydroids growing around the base of the machinery.
- **Background Diver**: In the murky green background at upper left, a scuba diver is faintly visible with a yellow regulator hose loop and a bright orange piece of equipment near the left edge of the frame.
