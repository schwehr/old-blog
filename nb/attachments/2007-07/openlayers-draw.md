# OpenLayers Draw Feature Example Screenshot (`openlayers-draw.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of a web browser displaying the "OpenLayers Draw Feature Example" interactive web map demo, illustrating client-side vector drawing (points, lines, and polygons) over a coastal map of New Hampshire and southern Maine (Great Bay and Piscataqua River region).

## Interface & Controls
- **Header**: Large bold black serif text at the top reads `OpenLayers Draw Feature Example`.
- **Map Navigation Controls (Top Left)**:
  - A dark blue circular directional pan pad with four white arrows (up, down, left, right).
  - Below the pan pad is a vertical dark blue pill-shaped zoom control with a `+` button at the top, a wireframe globe icon in the middle (zoom to max extent), and a `-` button at the bottom.
- **Layer Switcher (Top Right)**: A small dark blue tab with a white `+` sign anchored to the right edge of the map frame.
- **Coordinates Readout (Bottom Right)**: Black sans-serif text in the lower-right corner of the map canvas displays `-70.68878, 42.89200`.
- **Drawing Mode Radio Buttons (Below Map)**:
  - `navigate` (unselected)
  - `draw point` (unselected)
  - `draw line` (unselected)
  - `draw polygon` (selected with a black dot inside the radio button)

## Map & Vector Details
- **Base Map**: Shows the New Hampshire and southern Maine seacoast in pale off-white land and slate-blue water, prominently featuring the branching estuary of Great Bay, Little Bay, and the Piscataqua River leading out to the Atlantic Ocean on the right, with small pale yellow polygons indicating urban/populated areas.
- **User-Drawn Vector Features**:
  - **Orange Line**: An unclosed, roughly circular orange freehand polyline loop drawn around the upper branches of Little Bay / Bellamy River near Dover and Portsmouth.
  - **Orange Polygon**: A semi-transparent light orange filled polygon with a solid orange outline drawn over the western side of Great Bay.
  - **Second Orange Line/Polygon**: A narrow, elongated horizontal orange shape south of Great Bay.
