# Researcher Attaching Acoustic DTAG to a Humpback Whale (`humpback1.jpg`)

## Overview
A NOAA photograph showing a marine mammal researcher leaning from the bow of a small rigid-hulled inflatable boat (RHIB) to attach a suction-cup acoustic recording tag (DTAG) onto the back of a surfacing humpback whale (*Megaptera novaeangliae*) in NOAA's Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary.

## Scene Description
- **Researcher & Inflatable Boat (Left)**:
  - In the lower-left foreground is the gray inflatable pontoon bow of a small workboat with a red rub-strake stripe and black registration lettering reading `MS 1808 CC`. White water spray splashes beneath the bow.
  - A researcher with shoulder-length brown hair tied back, wearing a dark visor/sunglasses, a navy blue T-shirt, a blue life vest/harness, and olive-green trousers, leans forward over the bow tube.
  - With both hands, he extends a long, slightly curved black carbon-fiber pole out over the water toward the whale.
- **Humpback Whale & Tag (Center to Right)**:
  - The broad, dark gray-black dorsal surface and prominent dorsal fin/hump of a humpback whale rise above the rippled gray-blue ocean surface across the center and right of the frame.
  - At the tip of the long black pole, a light-colored suction-cup multi-sensor acoustic tag (DTAG) is being pressed directly onto the whale's flank just forward/side of the dorsal fin. Water streams down the whale's skin beneath the tag.
