# Artist's Rendering of the Phoenix Mars Lander Digging (`lander_digging.jpg`)

## Overview
A computer-generated artist's rendering (by Corby Waste / NASA / JPL) of the *Phoenix* Mars Lander sitting on the Martian arctic plains at low sun, with its robotic arm extended to dig trenches in the reddish-brown soil.

## Scene Description
- **Phoenix Lander**:
  - Positioned on the left-center of the frame, the lander stands on three angled, padded landing legs with diagonal cross-struts that cast long, dark shadows toward the left foreground.
  - Its flat deck supports dark instrument boxes, a tall vertical meteorological mast on the left, a lattice-style Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) camera mast in the center-left, and gold multi-layer insulation foil glinting in the sunlight beneath the deck. Circular solar array wings extend horizontally to the left and right, viewed nearly edge-on.
  - Extending up and to the right from the deck is the long, articulated black Robotic Arm (RA), bent at its elbow joint with its scoop/camera assembly poised in mid-air above the Martian surface on the right.
- **Martian Terrain & Sky**:
  - The ground is covered in reddish-brown soil, small rocks, and polygonal trough patterns typical of Martian high-latitude permafrost terrain. Directly beneath the robotic arm on the right are two shallow excavated trenches revealing darker subsurface material.
  - In the background, low rugged hills rise on the far left, while a small, pale disk of the setting/rising Sun glows just above the flat horizon to the right of the lander under a dusty pinkish-gray and pale blue sky with two faint pinpoints of light above.
