# Phoenix Mars Lander Solid Rocket Booster Separation (`phx-solid-separation.png`)

## Overview
A still frame captured from a live broadcast or tracking camera feed showing the solid rocket booster (SRB) separation during the night launch of NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander aboard a Delta II rocket on August 4, 2007.

## Scene Description
- **Main Rocket & Exhaust Plume**: Entering from the lower-left corner and angled diagonally upward toward the center-right is a brilliant, overexposed white-orange exhaust plume from the rocket's main stage. Forward of the bright plume, the cylindrical upper body/fairing of the Delta II rocket is faintly visible as a semi-translucent, smoky grey-brown silhouette against the dark sky.
- **Separating Solid Rocket Boosters**: Flanking the central exhaust plume are two thin, glowing orange-tan streaks with a distinct bent or angled trajectory, representing spent solid rocket motors peeling away from the core vehicle:
  - The upper booster trail extends outward from the top edge of the main plume and bends sharply upward toward the top-center of the frame.
  - The lower booster trail extends along the bottom edge of the plume and angles outward toward the right-center.
- **Background & Framing**: The background is a dark, hazy, warm brownish-black night sky illuminated by the intense glare of the rocket exhaust. The entire image is enclosed within a thin, two-tone grey rectangular border.
