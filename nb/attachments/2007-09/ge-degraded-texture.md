# Degraded Raster Nautical Chart Texture in Google Earth (`ge-degraded-texture.png`)

## Overview

A screenshot from Google Earth on an older PowerBook G4 laptop (equipped with an ATI Mobility Radeon 9700 with 64 MB VRAM) taken on September 18, 2007, illustrating severe texture downsampling and vertical streak artifacts on a 4096×4096 Raster Nautical Chart (RNC) overlay along the New England coast.

## Map / Overlay Details

- **Degraded Chart Overlay**: Occupying the lower two-thirds of the viewport is a NOAA raster nautical chart ground overlay that has been heavily downsampled and corrupted by graphics memory/driver limitations:
  - **Vertical Streaking at Top Edge**: Along the upper margin of the chart overlay, pixels are stretched into vertical black, gray, blue, and magenta barcode-like stripes against a white/cream background.
  - **Pixelated Chart Content**: The chart body (showing pale tan land on the left, light blue shallow water along the coast, white deeper water, a purple traffic separation lane extending toward the right, magenta compass roses/lines, and black depth soundings and text labels) is blocky and completely illegible.
- **Satellite Base Map & Track Line**:
  - Above and to the left of the chart overlay, the Google Earth satellite base map shows the coastline of New Hampshire and southern Maine meeting the dark blue Atlantic Ocean.
  - A thin red polyline (the Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary boundary or a vessel track) enters from the top right over the ocean and cuts diagonally down to the bottom right across the pixelated chart.
- **Overview Inset Map**: In the upper-left corner, a rectangular world map inset shows a red crosshair over the northeastern coast of North America.
