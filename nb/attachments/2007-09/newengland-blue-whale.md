# Blue Whale Surfacing Off the Coast of New Hampshire (`newengland-blue-whale.jpg`)

## Overview
A wildlife photograph released by the Blue Ocean Society for Marine Conservation showing a rare giant blue whale (*Balaenoptera musculus*) surfacing in the Atlantic Ocean approximately 12 miles off the New Hampshire coast in mid-September 2007—the first sighting of a blue whale in the area since 2002.

## Scene Description
- **The Whale**:
  - The broad, mottled slate-blue and light-gray back of the blue whale arches above the ocean surface in the lower center-right of the frame, facing left.
  - Near the rear of the exposed back on the right sits the prominent, fleshy splashguard and dual nostrils of the whale's blowhole (or dorsal ridge area), with a tall, faint column of white mist from its spout rising vertically against the dark blue water in the left-center of the image.
  - Below and ahead of the surfacing body on the left, the submerged portion of the whale casts a pale turquoise/aqua glow through the water, bordered by white foam and breaking ripples along its flank.
- **Ocean Environment**:
  - Deep navy-blue ocean swells and small wind ripples fill the rest of the frame from edge to edge.
