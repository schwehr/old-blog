# Mars HiRISE False-Color Detail of Fractured Bedrock and Sand Dunes (`hirise-color.png`)

## Overview
A high-resolution crop from a Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) HiRISE (High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment) image posted on October 15, 2007, showcasing false-color and monochrome/cyan-filtered views of fractured Martian bedrock, craters, and dark windblown sand ripples.

## Image Details
- **Left Three-Quarters (Enhanced Color Swath)**:
  - Shows a complex geological terrain where darker brownish-grey, smoother caprock or regolith ridges surround depressed basins of exposed, brightly colored light-blue/turquoise fractured bedrock (breccia or clay/sulfate-bearing rock).
  - The turquoise exposures are crisscrossed by an intricate polygonal network of dark cracks, joints, and faults.
  - Two small circular dark-floored impact craters are visible in the upper-middle brown terrain.
  - Along the right side of the color swath, dark brown windblown sand dunes and linear ripples drape across the fractured turquoise rock.
- **Right Quarter (Single-Channel Cyan/Blue-Green Strip)**:
  - Separated by a sharp, slightly angled vertical boundary on the right is the adjacent region outside the 3-color central swath, rendered in a monochromatic cyan/teal palette.
  - A large field of dark, parallel, north-south trending sand dunes and megaripples fills the center of this right-hand strip, resting atop bright cyan-toned fractured bedrock.
