# Horse Lubber Grasshopper Next to Finger in Sabino Canyon (`sabino-yellow-green-grasshopper.jpg`)

## Overview
A macro photograph taken in Sabino Canyon near Tucson, Arizona, on October 6, 2007 (posted October 7), showing a large black, yellow, and green grasshopper (*Taeniopoda eques*, the western horse lubber grasshopper) resting on coarse gravel next to a human index finger for scale.

## Subject & Scene Description
- **Grasshopper**:
  - Oriented horizontally facing left, spanning roughly the width of the frame.
  - **Head & Pronotum**: Glossy jet-black exoskeleton boldly outlined with bright yellow-orange margins along the flared pronotum (thorax shield) and a central orange-yellow dorsal stripe running down the crest of the head and pronotum.
  - **Antennae**: Segmented bright orange-and-black antennae extending forward to the left.
  - **Wings**: Long folded forewings (tegmina) featuring a striking reticulated (net-like) pattern of bright lime-green/yellow veins over a black background, tapering to solid dark tips at the rear.
  - **Legs & Abdomen**: Shiny black legs accented with yellow stripes along the femora and tibiae, with sharp black spines on the hind tibiae; the segmented black abdomen tips upward slightly at the rear with a small yellow-orange mass visible at the tip.
- **Scale & Background**:
  - A person's bare index finger enters from the top-left edge, resting its fingertip on the ground just above the grasshopper's head and left foreleg, demonstrating that the insect is significantly wider and longer than a human finger.
  - The ground consists of coarse, multi-colored desert gravel and sand grains in shades of gray, tan, pinkish-brown, and white quartz.
