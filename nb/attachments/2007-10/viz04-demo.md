# SIO Visualization Contest 2004 Web Presentation Screenshot (`viz04-demo.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of the opening slide (`pop1.shtml`) of Kurt Schwehr's 2004 Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO) Visualization Contest web presentation titled *"How to stop worrying and love the blob"*, demonstrating Server Side Includes (SSI) navigation for a 21-foot-wide display wall.

## Slide & Layout Details
- **Top Widescreen Banner Image**:
  - Features a faded background photograph looking straight down at a circular oceanographic CTD rosette water-sampling cage being lowered on a cable into dark blue ocean water, surrounded by a white ring of splashing foam.
  - Centered over the rosette in bold yellow-orange sans-serif text is the title:
    - **`How to stop worrying`**
    - **`and love the blob`**
  - Below the center of the rosette in smaller yellow-orange text is the author credit:
    - **`Kurt Schwehr`**
    - **`Oct 2004`**
  - **Four Inset Images** (two on the left, two on the right, each framed in a thin white border):
    - **Top Left**: Coastal cliff photograph showing golden-orange sedimentary layers of the Ardath Shale slump in La Jolla, California, with a house perched on top and seabirds on the wet beach below.
    - **Bottom Left**: 3D perspective shaded-relief bathymetric mesh of the Gaviota Slide submarine slope failure in the Santa Barbara Channel, colored from green (shallow) to blue (deep) with small red/orange marker dots.
    - **Top Right**: Close-up photograph of deformed, slumped rock strata on the beach at the Ardath Shale locality.
    - **Bottom Right**: 3D visualization on a black background showing blue, green, yellow, and red probability density clouds ("blobs") representing Anisotropy of Magnetic Susceptibility (AMS) fabric directions.
- **Explanatory Text Section (Middle)**:
  - Set in light grey sans-serif text against a dark charcoal-brown background (`#2b2523`):
    > This presentation will guide you through the background and application of Anisotropy of Magnetic Susceptibility 3D probability density estimations to marine slope failures.
    >
    > This is the opening slide showing a Rosette cage with CTD sensors being dropped into the ocean on one of my many cruises. The top left image is of the actual location of the Ardath Shale slump where the sample locality for this research project. The bottom left image is a picture of the Gaviota Slide, a modern slope failure in the Santa Barbara Channel. The top right image is a closer look at the Ardath Shale slump being studied. The bottom right image is a capture from the technology that I have created used to visualize the material researched.
- **Bottom SSI Navigation Thumbnail Grid**:
  - Headed by the grey link text **`Next Image`**.
  - Displays 13 small rectangular slide thumbnails arranged in two rows (8 thumbnails in the top row, 5 in the bottom row), depicting coastal cliff outcrops, multi-panel wall display layouts, 3D scatter plots, coordinate axes, paleomagnetic core diagrams, 3D spherical AMS density plots, bathymetric maps, and a horseshoe magnet illustration.
