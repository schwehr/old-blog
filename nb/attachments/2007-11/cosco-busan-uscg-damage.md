# Close-Up Photograph of M/V Cosco Busan Hull Damage (`cosco-busan-uscg-damage.jpg`)

## Overview
This photograph, taken by the U.S. Coast Guard (USCG) and distributed via NOAA, provides a close-up view of the severe port-side hull gash sustained by the 810-foot container ship *M/V Cosco Busan* after striking the protective fender of the Delta tower on the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge in heavy fog on November 7, 2007.

## Scene Description
- **Hull & Lettering**:
  - The upper half of the frame shows the dark navy-blue/black painted steel hull of the *Cosco Busan*, with the bottom portion of the giant white block letters **`H A N`** (the beginning of the charterer name **`HANJIN`**) painted on the side.
  - Below the dark upper hull is the reddish-brown anti-fouling paint along the vessel's lower waterline, sitting just above the choppy greenish-grey surface of San Francisco Bay.
- **Collision Gash**:
  - Directly beneath and forward (left) of the `"HAN"` lettering is a massive, jagged horizontal tear and indentation ripped into the port side of the hull above the red waterline.
  - The steel hull plating is crumpled inward in vertical accordion-like folds (exposing the internal framing ribs), with torn, ragged metal edges and a dark cavernous opening in the middle where fuel tanks were breached, spilling approximately 58,000 gallons of bunker fuel oil.
  - White and pale grey scrape marks and discolored stains trail down onto the reddish-brown lower hull plating beneath the gash.
  - A thin vertical rope/Jacob's ladder hangs down the hull directly over the letter `"A"`.
