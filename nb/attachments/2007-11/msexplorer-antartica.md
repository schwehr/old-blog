# Sinking of Cruise Ship M/S Explorer in Antarctica — Close-Up Bow View (`msexplorer-antartica.png`)

## Overview
An aerial photograph taken by the Chilean Navy on November 23, 2007, showing the expedition cruise ship *M/S Explorer* listing heavily onto its starboard side in pack ice in the Bransfield Strait, Antarctica, shortly before sinking.

## Scene Description
- **Vessel Orientation & Hull**:
  - Viewed from off the port bow, the *M/S Explorer* is heeled over at an extreme angle (nearly 45–60 degrees) to starboard into icy water.
  - The vessel's bright red hull with a white sheer stripe curves prominently across the right side of the frame, with the black ship name **"EXPLORER"** painted along the white band on the port bow above a rectangular hull opening.
- **Superstructure & Mast**:
  - The white multi-deck superstructure, bridge windows, and open deck railings tilt sharply to the left (starboard).
  - Above the bridge, the white radar mast with multiple crossbar antennas, a white radome, and a red-and-blue funnel topped by a black mast extension angle out over the water and ice floes.
  - All orange lifeboats have been launched from their davits following the evacuation of the 154 passengers and crew.
- **Surrounding Sea Ice**:
  - The ship is surrounded by dense, broken white sea-ice floes and brash ice, with a patch of dark open water visible directly beneath the submerged starboard bridge wing on the lower left.
