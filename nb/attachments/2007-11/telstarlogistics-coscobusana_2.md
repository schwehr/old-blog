# USCG Patrol Boat Inspecting Hull Gash on Container Ship Cosco Busan (`telstarlogistics-coscobusana_2.jpg`)

## Overview
A news photograph (featured via *Telstar Logistics* and the *San Francisco Chronicle*) showing a U.S. Coast Guard utility boat alongside the port bow of the container ship *M/V Cosco Busan* in San Francisco Bay after the vessel struck the fender of the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge on November 7, 2007.

## Scene Description
- **Container Ship (*M/V Cosco Busan*)**:
  - The massive dark-gray port hull and stacked shipping containers of the *Cosco Busan* dominate the right two-thirds of the frame against a hazy gray sky.
  - Large white block letters painted vertically along the gray hull side spell **`HANJIN`** (the ship was operating under charter to Hanjin Shipping).
  - Directly beneath the letters **`H A N`**, just above the reddish-brown waterline boot-topping near the flaring port bow, a ragged, cavernous horizontal gash and crumpled indentation is torn into the steel hull plating—the breach that punctured the ship's fuel tanks and released approximately 58,000 gallons of bunker fuel oil.
  - A smaller round puncture hole is also visible high up on one of the blue shipping containers in the forward stack on the left.
  - Above the deck, rows of red, blue, brown, and gray intermodal containers (bearing logos such as `HANJIN`, `SENATOR`, and `COSCO`) are stacked six tiers high, with an orange free-fall lifeboat and white superstructure visible behind the stacks.
- **U.S. Coast Guard Vessel**:
  - In the lower-left foreground, a white U.S. Coast Guard utility patrol boat (with the orange-and-blue USCG racing stripe on its bow) floats on the calm gray water just off the damaged bow of the container ship, providing a stark sense of scale against the towering hull breach.
