# AGU 2007 Poster: Google Earth Visualizations of Marine AIS (`schwehr-agu2007-sml.jpg`)

## Overview
A thumbnail image of Kurt Schwehr's scientific poster presented at the December 2007 American Geophysical Union (AGU) Fall Meeting (Session IN43A-0906), titled *"Google Earth Visualizations of Marine Automatic Identification System (AIS): Monitoring Ship Traffic in National Marine Sanctuaries"* by Kurt Schwehr, Leila Hatch, Michael Thompson, and David Wiley.

## Header Banner
- **Title & Authors (Center Box)**:
  - Title: `Google Earth Visualizations of Marine Automatic Identification System (AIS): Monitoring Ship Traffic in National Marine Sanctuaries`
  - Authors: `Kurt Schwehr, Leila Hatch, Michael Thompson, David Wiley`
- **Logos & Icons (Left & Right)**:
  - Top Left: University of New Hampshire Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping / Joint Hydrographic Center (`CCOM / JHC`) circular logo above a gray radio tower icon.
  - Top Right: `NOAA` circular logo above a matching gray radio tower icon.

## Upper Half Content
- **Abstract Box (Upper Left)**: A bordered text box titled `Abstract` containing three paragraphs describing the installation of three AIS receivers around Massachusetts Bay to monitor ship traffic in NOAA's Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary (SBNMS), the July 1, 2007 shifting of the Boston Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) to reduce right whale ship strikes, and the `noaadata-py` software package used to generate KML visualizations and density histograms in Google Earth.
- **Conceptual Diagram & Whale Photos (Upper Right)**:
  - **3D Schematic Diagram**: Illustrates how coastal AIS base stations (`USCG` and `NOAA/UNH/CCOM`), ships, aircraft, acoustic buoys, and the `IRIDIUM` satellite network track vessels and whales across Massachusetts Bay. Two inset maps at the top show a nautical chart with circular detection zones (`Whale Buoy TX`) and a 3D green-to-yellow traffic density surface (`Traffic Analysis`).
  - **Whale Photographs**: Three side-by-side color photographs below the diagram showing North Atlantic right whales swimming, breaching, and surfacing at sea.

## Lower Half Content
- **Left Column**:
  - Top left: Two Google Earth maps comparing `Two months of recorded AIS ship traffic` (red density tracks across Massachusetts Bay and Stellwagen Bank) and `Analysis of a single vessel` (a multi-colored vessel track across the sanctuary boundary).
  - Middle left: A 2x2 grid of small maps titled `Ship traffic with time` next to a large pie chart titled `Traffic composition based on AIS` breaking down vessel types (e.g., `tug/tow 28%`, `pleasure craft 15%`, `oil and/or chemical product tanker 14%`, `general cargo/full container 12%`, etc.).
  - Bottom left: Two nautical chart overlays titled `Picking new traffic lanes based on whale sightings`, showing hot-spot density contours of whale sightings inside the old and shifted Traffic Separation Scheme lanes.
- **Right Column**:
  - Top center-right: Three stacked Google Earth views of the Stellwagen Bank sanctuary boundary and shipping lanes labeled `June 2007 AIS traffic`, `July 2007 AIS traffic`, and `Traffic Separation before and after`, clearly demonstrating the northward shift of red vessel tracks into the new TSS lanes.
  - Top far-right: Two 3D extruded bar/column histograms in Google Earth labeled `Tanker` and `Tug Non-TSS traffic`.
  - Middle right: A dark time-depth dive profile plot titled `Tagged right whale feeding behavior` with underwater whale photos, alongside a screenshot of the Google Earth client interface displaying sanctuary layers and a pop-up balloon.
  - Bottom right: A bordered box titled `Acknowledgements` thanking NOAA (grant NA0N0S4001153), L-3 Klein Associates, ICAN, USCG RDC & VTS groups, US Army Corps of Engineers, and the DOT Volpe Lab.
