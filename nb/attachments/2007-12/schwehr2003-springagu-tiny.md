# Spring AGU 2003 Poster: Deciphering the Origin of the Humboldt Slide (`schwehr2003-springagu-tiny.jpg`)

## Overview
A low-resolution thumbnail image of a scientific poster from the 2003 Spring AGU Meeting titled *"Deciphering the Origin of the Humboldt Slide using Anisotropy of Magnetic Susceptibility"* by Kurt Schwehr, Neal Driscoll, and Lisa Tauxe from the Scripps Institution of Oceanography.

## Header Banner
- A blue-bordered title box at the top center reads:
  - `Deciphering the Origin of the Humboldt Slide using Anisotropy of Magnetic Susceptibility`
  - `Kurt Schwehr, Neal Driscoll, Lisa Tauxe`
  - `Scripps Institution of Oceanography`
- Flanked by the circular Scripps Institution of Oceanography logo on the left and a magnetic field/spin graphic on the right.

## Poster Layout & Sections
- **Top Row (Three Gray Boxes)**:
  - **Left (`Slide or Sediment Waves?`)**: Introductory text explaining the controversy over whether the Humboldt Slide off northern California represents a retrogressive slope-failure slide deposit or upslope-migrating sediment waves formed by down-slope gravity flows, and how Anisotropy of Magnetic Susceptibility (AMS) detects minor post-depositional deformation ("crypto-slumps").
  - **Center**: A schematic diagram showing expected AMS stereonet and eigenvalue histogram results for three different sediment deformation states (undeformed oblate fabric, moderate deformation, and slumped fabric).
  - **Right (`AMS of a known slump`)**: Field outcrop photographs of a known slump deposit on the left paired with a 3x4 grid of stereonets and bootstrap eigenvalue histograms on the right (`Schwehr and Tauxe (2003)`).
- **Middle Section (Seismic Profile & Core AMS Data)**:
  - **Chirp Seismic Profile**: A wide black-and-white high-resolution sub-bottom seismic profile across the undulating morphological steps of the Humboldt Slide (`~460 Meters Water Depth`, `150 Meters` wavelength scale), showing the locations of three piston cores (`01PC`, `02PC`, and `07PC`) penetrating a single sediment wave/step.
  - **Core Columns (`01PC`, `02PC`, `07PC`)**: Three tall vertical boxes (outlined in purple, cyan, and maroon) linked to the seismic profile, displaying stereonets and bootstrap susceptibility histograms for `Section 1` through `Section 7` (with `Flow-in` noted at the bottom of `01PC`), above grayscale photographs of the split sediment core sections at the base.
- **Right Column (Regional Maps & Comparative Imagery)**:
  - **Regional Map**: Shaded-relief bathymetric and topographic map of the Eel River basin and northern California continental margin showing the location of the Humboldt Slide.
  - **3D Perspective Bathymetry**: A grayscale 3D multibeam shaded-relief block diagram of the Humboldt Slide amphitheater and undulating waves.
  - **Aerial Photograph**: An oblique color aerial photo of the muddy brown sediment plume discharging from the Eel River mouth into the Pacific Ocean during flood stage.
  - **Comparative Seismic Examples**: Side-by-side acoustic profiles comparing sediment waves (`Lee et al., 2002`) and retrogressive slumps (`Gardner et al., 1999`).
- **Bottom Row**:
  - **Left (`References`)**: Gray box listing key citations (Gardner, Prior, and Field, 1999; Lee et al., 2002; Schwehr and Tauxe, 2003) and URL `http://schwehr.org/TTN136B/`.
  - **Center**: A 3D visualization of bulk magnetic susceptibility on a dark background (`X-Core visualization of bulk susceptibility of 02PC`).
  - **Right (`Conclusion`)**: Gray summary box stating that cores `01PC`, `02PC`, and `07PC` exhibit oblate sedimentary fabric throughout the 150-meter structures, supporting the interpretation that the features are depositional sediment waves (`Lee et al., 2002`) rather than retrogressive slumps.
