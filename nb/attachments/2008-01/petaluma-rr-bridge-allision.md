# Tug and Barge Allision Damage to the Petaluma Railroad Bridge (`petaluma-rr-bridge-allision.jpg`)

## Overview
A U.S. Coast Guard photograph taken on January 17, 2008, showing structural timber damage to the fender and pier protection system of the Northwestern Pacific Railroad swing bridge over the Petaluma River at Black Point near Novato, California, following an early-morning allision by the tug *Trig Lind* and a 206-foot sand barge.

## Scene Description
- **Bridge Superstructure (Upper Half)**:
  - The lower steel chord and deck stringers of the historic railroad swing span run horizontally across the upper portion of the frame, painted light grey/white with riveted steel plates, vertical and diagonal lattice truss members extending upward, and dark wooden railroad ties resting along the top deck.
  - At the far right, the bridge span rests on a cluster of tall cylindrical support pilings and a pivot/abutment structure.
- **Damaged Timber Fender & Piling Structure (Center & Left)**:
  - Below the steel span, a wooden fender wall constructed of vertical timber pilings and heavy horizontal timber wales stands in the water.
  - On the left and center-left, a roughly 15-foot-by-10-foot section of the timber fender is splintered and wrenched out of alignment: a thick, light-grey horizontal timber beam is snapped and angled sharply downward from left to right, with jagged, freshly splintered yellow-orange wood fibers exposed where it broke away from the central pilings.
  - Beneath the angled beam, dark weathered planks are broken and tilted downward into the river.
  - To the right of the impact zone, the intact portion of the timber fender wall is coated in bright green marine algae along the intertidal zone, supported by a row of dark vertical pilings rising from the calm, greenish-brown rippled water of the Petaluma River.
- **Background**:
  - Green trees, a hillside on the far right, and the roof of a building on the far left are visible along the distant riverbank beneath a bright, overexposed sky.
