# Multibeam Bathymetry of Arctic Seafloor Pockmarks (`arctic-pock-marks.jpg`)

## Overview

A 3D shaded-relief multibeam bathymetry visualization from the UNH-NOAA Joint Hydrographic Center (CCOM/JHC) Arctic mapping expedition to the Chukchi Cap, showing circular pockmark craters and parallel linear ridges/furrows on the seafloor at approximately 1,600 feet depth.

## Visualization Details

- **Seafloor Morphology:**
  - The seafloor surface is rendered in warm orange and golden-yellow tones using directional hillshading.
  - Numerous sub-parallel, corrugated linear ridges and troughs (likely iceberg scour marks or sediment waves) run diagonally from the upper-left toward the lower-right across the entire terrain.
  - Eight prominent circular to oval depression craters (**pockmarks**, formed by fluid or gas escape from the seabed) punctuate the surface:
    - Two large pockmarks on the left-center.
    - A cluster of four large pockmarks in the upper-right quadrant (with a fifth partial depression at the far upper-right edge).
    - Two pockmarks in the lower-right quadrant (a smaller one above a larger, deeper one at the bottom).
- **Depth Color Coding & Profile Line:**
  - The surrounding seabed is colored reddish-orange, while the deeper interiors of the pockmark craters transition to brighter golden-yellow at their centers.
  - A bright cyan/blue cross-sectional profile line is draped across the largest pockmark in the lower-right foreground, tracing the flat rim on either side and dipping down into the V-shaped/U-shaped floor of the crater.
  - A few tiny black data-holiday pixels appear along a swath seam near the left-center.
