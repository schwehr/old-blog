# Shachak Demonstrating a Carbonate Content Measuring Device (`carbonate-1.jpg`)

## Overview

A color indoor photograph of researcher Shachak Pe'eri holding and demonstrating a custom-built laboratory pressure-gauge apparatus ("carbonate bomb") designed by Jim Gardner to measure the calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) content of sediment samples.

## Scene Description

- **Subject:** Shachak stands on the left side of the frame wearing a dark navy-blue V-neck pullover sweater with white and red trim along the collar and shoulders, looking down attentively at the instrument in his hands.
- **Carbonate Measuring Device:**
  - **Lower Cylinder:** In his right hand (at the bottom of the frame), he holds a thick-walled, transparent acrylic reaction cylinder with tiny water droplets visible on its inner wall and an O-ring seal near the top rim.
  - **Top Cap & Pressure Gauge:** In his raised left hand (at the upper right), he holds the removable top assembly consisting of a brass-rimmed analog dial gauge (`% CaCO3`) mounted atop a machined clear acrylic cap with a white knurled bleed/release valve on the left side.
  - **Suspended Acid Cup:** Hanging below the cap by two bright red flexible plastic/rubber rods is a small, cylindrical clear plastic cup (used to hold hydrochloric acid before sealing the cylinder and tipping it to react with the carbonate sediment).
- **Background:** Behind him is a dark gray metal filing cabinet with two white business cards (one with a blue logo and phone number) stuck to the front, coiled white cables on top, and a framed poster on the wall in the upper background.
