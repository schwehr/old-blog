# Coastal Marine Lab Tide Gauge Stilling Tubes Underwater (`cml-tide-sensor.jpg`)

## Overview
A high-angle photograph looking down into the greenish-murky water beneath the UNH Coastal Marine Lab (CML) pier in New Castle, New Hampshire, showing the submerged tops of two PVC stilling tubes attached to a wooden pier piling for tide gauge water-level sensors.

## Scene Description
- **Wooden Pier Piling & Timbers**:
  - A square vertical wooden timber piling runs down the center of the frame, bolted at the top between heavy horizontal reddish-brown structural beams with recessed metal bolts and washers.
  - The upper portion of the vertical piling is dry, light tan wood with visible grain. Moving downward toward the water surface, the wood transitions into a bright green band of marine algae and moss, followed by a darker brown/olive intertidal zone with barnacles and seaweed just at and below the waterline.
  - Diagonal wooden cross-bracing beams are faintly visible submerged in the murky green water behind the main piling.
- **Tide Gauge Stilling Tubes & Cabling**:
  - Two vertical cylindrical white/light-colored PVC stilling tubes are mounted on opposite sides (left and right) of the lower submerged section of the central piling, with their open circular tops visible just beneath the water surface.
  - Black sensor data cables run down the sides of the vertical timber into the stilling tubes, secured to the wood with white zip-ties or clips and a horizontal tie wrap just above the water surface.
  - Along the bottom edge of the frame, a sunlit wooden ledge or walkway timber with small metal eye-hooks and round white bolt caps sits in the foreground above the water.
