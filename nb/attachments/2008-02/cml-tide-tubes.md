# Coastal Marine Lab Tide Station Stilling Tubes at Low Tide (`cml-tide-tubes.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken at low tide beneath the UNH Coastal Marine Lab (CML) pier in New Castle, New Hampshire, showing the exposed upper sections of two vertical PVC tide-gauge stilling tubes mounted to a barnacle- and seaweed-encrusted wooden pier piling.

## Scene Description
- **Stilling Tubes**:
  - Two long, vertical white PVC pipes (stained tan and brownish from marine exposure) flank the left and right sides of a square vertical wooden pier piling.
  - The left tube extends slightly higher than the right tube and is fastened against the side of the piling with several horizontal metal hose clamps/straps spaced along its length. A bit of green algae is visible at its top rim.
  - The right tube is mounted in the foreground face of the piling. A black sensor cable and a tan rope with a tied loop and a small hanging weight/bundle enter the open top of the right tube.
  - Both tubes extend straight down into the dark green water at the bottom of the frame.
- **Pier Structure & Intertidal Growth**:
  - The central wooden piling and the horizontal and diagonal timber cross-braces behind it are heavily covered in white barnacles, dark green/brown rockweed (*Fucus*/*Ascophyllum*), and patches of orange-brown rust or bryozoan growth.
  - In the dark background under the pier to the right, rocky riprap boulders and hanging clumps of wet seaweed are visible in the shadows.
