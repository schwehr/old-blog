# Shipboard LiDAR Point Cloud and Photograph of a Norfolk Aid to Navigation (`norfolk-aton-lidar.png`)

## Overview
A composite image from a US Hydro 2007 presentation (*Surface Imaging Capabilities on Marine Hydrographic Vessels* by P. Canter, R. Brennan, A. Silva, and J. Van Rens) showing a green lighted channel buoy (Aid to Navigation, or AtoN) in Norfolk Harbor in the foreground, a large blue-and-red bulk carrier ship (*Federal Kivalina* / Fednav vessel) moored at a pier in the background, and an inset 3D shipboard laser scanner (LiDAR) point cloud rendering of the same buoy in the lower-left corner.

## Visual Elements
- **Main Photograph**:
  - **Foreground Buoy**: A green cylindrical steel channel buoy with a lattice tower structure, dayboard numbering (`33` / green panel), solar panel, and top lantern floats on dark rippled harbor water in the lower center.
  - **Background Cargo Vessel**: Directly behind the buoy is the bow and starboard quarter of a large ocean-going bulk carrier moored alongside a concrete pier. The ship has a dark blue upper hull with a large white circled `L` bow logo and white vessel name lettering (`FEDERAL ...`) on the port and starboard bows, a dull red lower hull/boot-topping above the waterline, and tall gray deck cranes and masts rising against a hazy gray sky.
- **LiDAR Point Cloud Inset (Lower Left)**:
  - Overlaid in the lower-left corner is a vertical rectangular black-background inset showing the 3D Riegl laser scanner point cloud of the green buoy collected from the moving NOAA survey launch *Bay Hydrographer*.
  - White and light-gray laser return points clearly trace the concentric horizontal rings of the buoy's cylindrical float base, the vertical legs and cross-braces of its lattice tower, a bright white band with a red center dot at the daymark/reflector level, the top light assembly, and faint specular reflections on the water directly beneath the buoy.
