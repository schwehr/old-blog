# Propagating Waves and Energy Dissipating Screens in the Wave Tank (`wave-tank-waves.jpg`)

## Overview
An eye-level photograph looking down the water channel of the wave tank in the Chase Ocean Engineering Laboratory high bay at UNH, showing green water waves propagating toward energy-dissipating screens while a real-time waveform plot is projected onto the upper wall.

## Scene Description
- **Water & Waves**:
  - The lower half of the image looks directly into the wave tank channel filled with deep emerald-green water.
  -Captured with a slow shutter speed without a flash, the moving wave crests and troughs show a smooth, slightly motion-blurred texture with bright specular highlights reflecting the overhead high-bay lights.
- **Tank Walls & Dissipating Screens**:
  - On the left, a wide wooden cap plank runs along the top of the concrete tank wall into the background, with a black sensor cable draped across the foreground.
  - On the right, the inner concrete wall of the tank is topped with metal guide rails for the tow carriage.
  - At the far end of the visible tank section, dark mesh/screen baffles sit in the water to absorb and dissipate wave energy, topped by a block of white rectangular foam/plastic floats.
- **Background & Projected Display**:
  - In the upper right, projected high onto the two-tone brown wall above a double door and storage shelving, is the wireless projector display showing a computer window with a bright yellow sinusoidal wave-staff trace on a black plot area.
  - A bright overhead light fixture illuminates the pale yellow back wall of the high bay on the upper left.
