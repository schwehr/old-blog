# AMVER Global Ship Reporting Density Map (`amver.png`)

## Overview
A global equirectangular map plot illustrating vessel position report density from the United States Coast Guard's **AMVER** (Automated Mutual-Assistance Vessel Rescue) voluntary global ship reporting system.

## Map & Data Details
- **Coordinate Grid & Axes:**
  - **Horizontal Axis (Longitude):** Labeled along the bottom from `180` (West) through `0` (Prime Meridian) to `180` (East) in 10-degree increments.
  - **Vertical Axis (Latitude):** Labeled along the left edge from `80` (South) through `0` (Equator) to `90` (North) in 10-degree increments.
- **Continents:** World coastlines and landmasses (North and South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, and Antarctica) are outlined in dark gray and filled in white.
- **Vessel Density Coloring:** Ocean grid cells containing AMVER position reports are color-coded from low to high density:
  - **Purple:** Low density / scattered vessel reports across broad expanses of the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, and Southern Oceans, including distinct vertical and diagonal ship tracks extending toward Antarctica and around southern capes.
  - **Green:** Moderate-to-high density covering major transoceanic shipping basins, particularly across the North Atlantic, North Pacific great-circle routes, and Indian Ocean lanes.
  - **Yellow / Orange / Red:** Highest shipping density concentrated along major maritime choke points and coastal corridors:
    - The English Channel, Strait of Gibraltar, Mediterranean Sea, Suez Canal, Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, across the northern Indian Ocean past Sri Lanka to the Strait of Malacca, and northward through the South and East China Seas to Japan.
    - Trans-Atlantic corridors between the US East Coast / Gulf of Mexico / Panama Canal and Western Europe.
    - Routes rounding the Cape of Good Hope (South Africa) and eastern South America.
    - Noticeable absence of reports in the high Arctic Ocean north of Alaska, Canada, and Siberia (except in the Barents/Norwegian Sea north of Scandinavia).
