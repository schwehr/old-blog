# NGDC World Magnetic Anomaly Map in Google Earth (`ge-mag-1.png`)

## Overview
A full-window screenshot of **Google Earth** on Mac OS X displaying a global overlay of the **NGDC World Magnetic Anomaly Map** (created by Stefan Maus of NOAA), centered on North America, Central America, and the eastern Pacific and western Atlantic oceans.

## Interface & Controls
- **Left Sidebar**:
  - **Search Panel**: `Fly To` tab active (`Fly to e.g., 1600 Pennsylvania Ave, 2000`).
  - **Places Panel**: Under `Temporary Places`, the checked folder `NGDC World Magnetic Anomaly Map` and its checked child layer `NGDC World Magnetic Anomaly Map` are visible above a transparency slider.
  - **Layers Panel**: `View: Core` dropdown, showing `Primary Database` with `Terrain`, `roads`, `3D Buildings`, and `Borders and Labels` checked.
- **3D Viewport Controls & Status Bar**:
  - Upper right: Navigation compass ring, pan buttons, and zoom slider.
  - Bottom center: `Image NASA`, `© 2008 Europa Technologies`, `Map data © 2008 DMapas/El Mercurio`, `© 2008 MapLink/TeleAtlas`.
  - Bottom bar: `Streaming 100%`, `©2007 Google™` logo, and `Eye alt 8467.73 km`.

## Map & Geophysical Features
- **Magnetic Anomaly Overlay**:
  - The globe is covered in high-contrast red (positive magnetic anomalies) and blue (negative magnetic anomalies) patterns, with grey areas in the southern Pacific where satellite/marine track coverage is sparser or masked.
  - **Oceanic Seafloor Spreading Stripes**: Distinct linear north-south alternating red and blue magnetic lineations (stripes) parallel the East Pacific Rise and Juan de Fuca / Gorda ridges in the eastern Pacific Ocean off the western coast of North and South America, intersected by east-west fracture zones (such as the Mendocino, Murray, Molokai, and Clarion fracture zones).
  - **Continental Anomalies**: Complex mottled and curvilinear red-and-blue crustal magnetic anomalies cover the North American craton, with yellow national border and coastline outlines clearly delineating the United States, Canada, Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean islands, and northwestern South America.
