# Foraging Hawkmoth (*Manduca sexta*) and *Datura wrightii* Flower on PNAS Cover (`riffell-pnas-image.jpg`)

## Overview
- **Image Type:** Close-up high-speed nature photograph
- **Subject / Context:** Cover image from the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* (PNAS, Vol. 105, No. 9, March 2008) accompanying the paper *"Behavioral consequences of innate preferences and olfactory learning in hawkmoth-flower interactions"* by Jeffrey A. Riffell, Ruben Alarcon, Leif Abrell, Goggy Davidowitz, Judith L. Bronstein, and John G. Hildebrand.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Left (Flower):** A large, trumpet-shaped white sacred datura / jimsonweed flower (*Datura wrightii*) rises diagonally from the lower-left corner against a pitch-black background. Its pleated, funnel-like corolla is brightly lit, showing delicate veining, a pale greenish tinge near the base of the tube, and five recurved, pointed lobes/tendrils around the flared rim.
- **Upper Right (Hawkmoth):** A Carolina sphinx / tobacco hawkmoth (*Manduca sexta*) hovers in mid-air to the upper right of the blossom, facing toward the flower:
  - Its mottled gray-and-brown wings are spread mid-beat, with dark wavy banding and a narrow white fringe along the wing margins.
  - Its large dark compound eyes, feathery antennae, and tucked segmented legs are clearly visible under studio strobe lighting.
  - Extending downward from its head is its long, slender, amber-brown proboscis ("tongue"), which forms a graceful circular loop in mid-air as it uncoils (or recoils) while foraging for nectar.
