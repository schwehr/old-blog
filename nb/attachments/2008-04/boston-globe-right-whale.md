# Right Whale Listening Network Underwater Buoy and Satellite Diagram (`boston-globe-right-whale.png`)

## Overview
A two-panel side-by-side computer-generated illustration (featured in *The Boston Globe* and produced for the Cornell Bioacoustics Research Program's Right Whale Listening Network) depicting how moored acoustic buoys detect North Atlantic right whales underwater and transmit alerts via satellite to nearby ships.

## Panel Descriptions
- **Left Panel (Underwater Acoustic Detection):**
  - Depicts a bright turquoise-blue underwater scene lit by sunbeams slanting down from the surface.
  - Two vertical buoy mooring lines with cylindrical sensors and spherical floats descend through the water column in the foreground and background.
  - A North Atlantic right whale swims upward and away toward the upper center-right between the mooring lines, while a small school of fish swims near the bottom center and upper left.
- **Right Panel (Surface Buoy & Satellite Relay to Ship):**
  - Depicts the ocean surface under a pale overcast sky.
  - In the foreground sits a yellow-and-blue cylindrical monitoring buoy topped with a vertical mast and a red warning light; a second buoy is visible in the distance on the left.
  - To the right, an orange-hulled commercial cargo ship or LNG tanker approaches across the water.
  - In the upper left sky, a satellite icon is labeled `Satellite Network` in blue sans-serif text.
  - Two dotted light-blue arrows illustrate the communication path: one arrow points upward from the buoy's mast to the satellite, and a second longer arrow points downward from the satellite to the approaching ship.
