# Right Whale Listening Network "4. Notify" Step Diagram (`brp-notify.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology Bioacoustics Research Program's *Right Whale Listening Network* website (`listenforwhales.org`), showing the fourth step (**4. Notify – Alerting ship traffic**) of its five-step right whale acoustic detection and ship-alerting process.

## Interface & Process Tabs
- **Top Heading:** Blue bold italic text reading `Learn about the five-step detection process:`.
- **Five Chevron Step Tabs:**
  - `1. Detect`
  - `2. Transmit`
  - `3. Analyze`
  - `4. Notify` (highlighted as the active tab with a white background and orange bottom border)
  - `5. Avoid`

## Content & Illustration
- **Section Heading:** `Alerting ship traffic` in dark blue bold italic sans-serif text.
- **Left Illustration:**
  - A stylized vector graphic looking out through the bridge windows of a ship at wavy blue ocean water and a pale yellow sky with three flying seagulls.
  - Across the top of the window is semi-transparent white bold italic text reading `Whales Detected!` next to a circular wall clock icon showing 4 o'clock.
  - On the ship's bridge console in the foreground are a pair of black binoculars on the left, a radar/navigation display showing a green circle and bearing line, a brown engine throttle lever, and a spoked ship's helm wheel on the right.
- **Explanatory Text (Right & Bottom):**
  - Paragraph 1: *"The main recipients of whale alerts are ships headed for a liquefied natural gas terminal located at the western end of the buoy array and operated by Northeast Gateway Deepwater Port."*
  - Paragraph 2: *"When ships bound for the terminal are in Massachusetts Bay (roughly once per week when the terminal is operating at full capacity), Cornell sound analysts monitor buoy reports round the clock. Analysts stay on duty from before the ships enter the buoy array until the ship is safely anchored at the terminal. They continuously issue updates to inform the ships which of the 10 buoys have heard a right whale in the last 24 hours."*
  - Paragraph 3: *"When no natural-gas ships are in the shipping lanes, analysts provide updates every 12 hours for the benefit of other traffic. These summaries are made available to any ship or boat via the Right Whale Sighting Advisory System (SAS) operated by NOAA. The reports are available online and are also distributed by e-mail. The information is also incorporated into marine safety bulletins."*
- **Bottom Navigation Link:** Orange underlined link reading `Step 5 > Ships may slow and avoid whales`.
