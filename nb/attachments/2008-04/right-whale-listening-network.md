# Cornell Lab of Ornithology — Right Whale Listening Network Homepage (`right-whale-listening-network.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of the homepage for the **Right Whale Listening Network** (`http://listenforwhales.org/`), operated by the **Cornell Lab of Ornithology Bioacoustics Research Program**, captured on April 7, 2008 upon the public launch of the real-time acoustic right whale monitoring website for Massachusetts Bay and Cape Cod Bay.

## Top Banner & Navigation
- **Header Graphic**:
  - Deep ocean-blue banner displaying the **`CORNELL LAB of ORNITHOLOGY`** bird logo at the top left, the large white title **`Right Whale Listening Network`**, and subtitle **`Bioacoustics Research Program`**.
  - On the right side of the banner is an underwater illustration of an adult North Atlantic right whale (with white callosities on its head) swimming alongside a calf.
  - Top navigation links along the bottom right of the dark blue banner: `About Right Whales`, `Threats`, `Sounds`, `Solutions`, `Partnerships`.
- **Main Tab Bar & Hero Strip**:
  - Three large folder-style tabs: **`Home`** (active, highlighted in bright cyan with a downward pointer triangle), **`Threats to Right Whales`**, and **`Explore Whale Sounds`**.
  - Light-blue hero strip below the tabs featuring a white silhouette of a whale emitting two curved white acoustic sound waves to the left, paired with the text:
    - **`Helping Ships Avoid Endangered Whales`**
    - *"The world's last 350 North Atlantic right whales live along the East Coast. Collisions with ships are a deadly hazard, but new listening buoys are helping. Here's how."*

## Main Content Columns
- **Left Column — Embedded Videos**:
  - **Primary Video Player**: Shows an aerial photograph/video frame of a North Atlantic right whale surfacing in green-blue water, with playback controls below (Play button, Stop button, timeline scrubber bar, mute/volume icons).
  - **Caption**:
    - ***`A Right Whale's Life`***
    - *"Find out the basics: Where they live, what they eat, why they're endangered. And why there's still hope. (Duration: 2:13)"*
  - **Secondary Video Teaser (Bottom Left)**:
    - Thumbnail photograph of an acoustic buoy on the deck of a vessel, accompanied by the text:
      - ***`Mission: Listen`***
      - *"Take a boat trip into Massachusetts Bay to install the new listening buoys. Dr. Christopher Clark is your guide. (Duration: 3:16)"*
- **Right Column — Live Detection Map**:
  - Heading: ***`Right whale detections: last 24 hours`***
  - Description: *"New smart buoys listen for whale calls all day, every day. Frequent alerts let ship captains know where and when to slow down—and save a whale."*
  - **Map of Massachusetts Bay & Cape Cod Bay**:
    - Shows **Cape Ann / Gloucester**, **Boston**, **Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary**, **Provincetown**, **Cape Cod Bay**, and **Cape Cod**, with a `0–20 Nautical Miles` scale bar at the top right, a compass rose (`N`) at the bottom right, and the **`Map Legends`** box at the bottom left.
    - Along the **Boston Shipping Lane**, 9 of the 10 buoys show red right-whale detection icons inside red rings, while the 2nd buoy from the west shows a green circle (`Online Buoy` with no detection).
    - Inside **Cape Cod Bay**, 2 buoys on the west side show red right-whale detection icons, and 1 buoy south of Provincetown shows a gray circle (`Offline Buoy`).
