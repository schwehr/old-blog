# Potted Carnivorous Plants at the UNH Greenhouse (`unh-greenhouse-2.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up high-angle photograph of a terracotta-colored plastic pot containing a miniature bog garden of carnivorous plants—including Venus flytraps, pitcher plants, and sundews—at the University of New Hampshire (UNH) Greenhouse.

## Scene Description
- **Venus Flytraps (*Dionaea muscipula*)**: Positioned in the center and left of the pot, several rosettes of hinged, toothed jaw-like traps with reddish-pink interiors sit below tall, upright green flower stalks topped with tight clusters of round green flower buds.
- **Pitcher Plants (*Sarracenia*)**: Rising from the upper-right portion of the pot are tall, tubular green and reddish-veined upright pitchers alongside long strap-like leaves and dried brown bases from previous growth.
- **Sundews (*Drosera*) & Sphagnum Moss**: Across the bottom foreground, bright green live sphagnum moss on the left transitions into a dense patch of reddish-orange sundew rosettes on the right, glistening with tiny sticky mucilage-tipped tentacles in the bright greenhouse sunlight.
