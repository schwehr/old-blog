# NASA Phoenix Mars Lander Entry, Descent, and Landing Sequence (`L-3.png`)

## Overview
A composite promotional artist's rendering from NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) illustrating the five sequential stages of Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) for the Phoenix Mars Lander, arranged diagonally from upper left to lower right over a black background with glossy mirror reflections beneath each panel.

## Sequential Panels (Left to Right)
1. **Cruise Stage Separation (Top Left)**: Shows the white conical aeroshell/backshell separating in deep space from the cruise stage with its rectangular solar panel wings deployed.
2. **Atmospheric Entry / Peak Heating (Second from Left)**: Depicts the aeroshell streaking diagonally downward through the upper Martian atmosphere, enveloped in a glowing orange-yellow plasma fireball and trailing shock plume above the curved horizon of Mars.
3. **Parachute Deployment (Center)**: Shows the lander suspended inside its dark heatshield/backshell beneath a circular orange-and-white striped supersonic parachute descending through dusty tan Martian skies.
4. **Powered Descent (Fourth from Left)**: Illustrates the three-legged Phoenix lander firing its pulsed hydrazine descent thrusters—visible as bright white vertical exhaust plumes beneath the deck—as it slows down meters above the rocky brown Martian plains.
5. **Touchdown on the Martian Surface (Bottom Right)**: Shows the Phoenix lander resting stationary on its three footpads on the dark, rock-strewn northern plains of Mars prior to unfolding its circular solar arrays.

## Visual Styling
- Each of the five square-ish panels overlaps slightly with the next, stepping downward from left to right.
- A soft gray outer glow borders the upper stair-step edge of the panels against the black background, while faded vertical reflections extend downward beneath the first four panels.
