# Phoenix Mars Lander Robotic Arm Camera View Underneath the Spacecraft (`RS005EFF896663219_11730MDM1.jpg`)

## Overview
A raw grayscale photograph (Experimental Data Record `RS005EFF896663219_11730MDM1`) captured on Sol 5 of the mission by the Robotic Arm Camera (RAC) on NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander, looking underneath the lander deck from the north side toward the southern landing leg.

## Scene Description
- **Southern Landing Leg & Footpad (Upper Center)**: Three tubular struts converge downward onto a circular footpad resting securely on the fine-grained Martian regolith. The central and right struts cast sharp, dark diagonal shadows across the ground toward the lower left.
- **Descent Thruster Nozzles (Top Edge)**: Four metallic conical rocket nozzles of the lander's pulsed hydrazine descent thrusters hang down along the top center and upper-right edge of the frame.
- **Exposed Subsurface Ice Table ("Holy Cow")**: Directly in front of the landing leg (center), rocket exhaust from the descent thrusters has blown away the overlying loose dirt, exposing a bright, smooth, slightly recessed patch of hard subsurface material (water ice) alongside a second smaller bright patch to the right.
- **Foreground Soil Mound & Lander Hardware**: A dark, shadowed ridge of displaced soil runs horizontally across the lower third of the image. In the bottom foreground, a rectangular metallic bracket with small fastener markings protrudes at lower left-center, while dark cylindrical structural tubing frames the lower left and lower right corners.
