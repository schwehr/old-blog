# Phoenix Mars Lander First Full 360-Degree Grayscale Horizon Panorama (`S_003EDN_CYLTSR10D28_R111M1_byte.png`)

## Overview
A wide cylindrical-projection grayscale mosaic (`PIA10726: Sweeping Martian Plains`) assembled from images taken by the Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) on NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander across Sols 1 and 3, showing the full 360-degree view of the polygonal patterned ground of the Vastitas Borealis landing site and the lander's upper deck.

## Scene Description
- **Martian Terrain & Horizon**: Flat, expansive Arctic plains stretch seamlessly across the entire width of the panorama to a crisp, level horizon beneath a pale gray sky strip (with a black missing-data band across the top sky region). The ground exhibits a network of shallow troughs and raised polygon interiors scattered with small pebbles and cobbles, illuminated from different solar azimuths across the vertical seams of the mosaic.
- **Decagonal Solar Arrays**:
  - **Left Foreground**: The fully deployed, dark, 10-sided (decagonal) fan-like Ultraflex solar panel array extends out from the left side of the lander deck, showing radial ribs and white calibration/fiducial markings around its perimeter.
  - **Right Midground**: The second circular/decagonal solar panel is visible to the right of the lander deck, catching brighter sunlight on its surface segments.
- **Lander Deck & Robotic Arm**: In the lower right foreground, the spacecraft deck is packed with scientific instruments, calibration targets, a thin vertical meteorology mast rising into the sky gap at upper right-center, and the stowed/partially raised Robotic Arm with its scoop and camera assembly angled upward to the right.
