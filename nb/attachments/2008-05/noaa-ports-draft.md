# NOAA PORTS Diagram of Vessel Draft Over Years (`noaa-ports-draft.jpg`)

## Overview
An informational graphic from NOAA's Physical Oceanographic Real-Time System (PORTS) titled **VESSEL DRAFT OVER YEARS**, illustrating how the underwater hull draft of commercial cargo ships has dramatically increased from 1855 to 1955 to the present relative to channel depth.

## Visual Elements & Layout
- **Title**: Bold dark-blue all-caps text `VESSEL DRAFT OVER YEARS` centered at the top against a white banner.
- **3D Perspective Channel Grid**: A dark blue/indigo perspective grid with light blue lines forms a trough or shipping channel surrounding the central illustration.
- **Central Underwater Illustration**: A black-bordered square monochrome underwater view looking beneath the water's surface at three ship hulls side by side, with rippled surface reflections above and dark shadows cast on the seabed below:
  - **Left (`Present`)**: A massive modern deep-draft hull extending nearly to the bottom of the channel (approaching 45–50 feet of draft), leaving minimal under-keel clearance above its seabed shadow.
  - **Center (`1955`)**: A medium-sized mid-20th-century ship hull reaching a moderate depth (roughly 25–30 feet).
  - **Right (`1855`)**: A small 19th-century vessel hull barely dipping below the surface (around 10–15 feet).
- **Depth Scales & Labels**:
  - **Left Scale Bar**: Vertical yellow bar from `0` to `40` transitioning diagonally to red at `50 ft.` in black bold text.
  - **Right Scale Bar**: Vertical bar that is yellow from `0` to `10` and red from `10` down to `50 ft.`, visually emphasizing the increased grounding hazard in shallow channels.
  - **Bottom Time Labels**: Bold red text along the bottom grid beneath the three hulls reading `Present`, `1955`, and `1855`.
