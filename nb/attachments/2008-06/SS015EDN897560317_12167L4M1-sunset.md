# Phoenix Mars Lander Arctic Martian Sun (`SS015EDN897560317_12167L4M1-sunset.jpg`)

## Overview
This grayscale image captured by the Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) on NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander shows the glow of the Arctic Martian sun near the bottom of the frame on Sol 15 (June 9–10, 2008). As noted in the accompanying blog post (*"Arctic Martian sun"*, June 10, 2008), the image was acquired at 17:20:23 local solar time (elevation 37.7223°, azimuth 275.848°); because Phoenix landed during the Martian northern summer with continuous daylight, this is not a sunset, and the scattered bright specks are dark-current noise on the CCD sensor rather than stars.

## Visual Details & Sensor Artifacts
- **Solar Glow**: At the bottom-left and bottom-center edge of the square frame, an intense, saturated white glow radiates upward and outward, creating a smooth radial gradient that transitions from bright white at the lower margin through mid-gray tones to a nearly black sky in the upper right.
- **CCD Dark-Current Dots**: Dozens of tiny, sharp white and light-gray single-pixel and multi-pixel dots are scattered randomly across the dark sky field, resembling a starfield. Several brighter pixel clusters appear in the upper-right, upper-left, and central regions.
- **Framing**: The square image is bordered by a thin black margin along the left, top, and right edges.
