# Shadow-Leveled Dodo-Goldilocks Trench on Mars (`SS022EFF898161657_12CAEL1M1_sml-leveled.jpg`)

## Overview
This grayscale photograph taken by the Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) on NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander on Sol 22 shows the excavated "Dodo-Goldilocks" trench in the Martian arctic soil after digital shadow-leveling processing in Adobe Photoshop by Joel Hagen (`jhagen`). Featured in the June 17, 2008 blog post (*"Reducing the impact of shadows"*), the leveling adjustment lifts the dark shadow cast across the trench wall and floor so that both the illuminated and shaded portions of the white subsurface ice layer are clearly visible.

## Scene & Geological Details
- **Excavated Trench**: An elongated, rectangular trench runs diagonally from the lower-center toward the upper-right of the frame, cut into fine-grained, clumpy gray-toned Martian regolith.
- **Exposed Bright Ice Layer**: Along the upper-left interior floor and back wall of the trench, a smooth, light-toned hard subsurface layer (water ice) is exposed where the robotic arm scoop scraped away the overlying soil. Longitudinal scrape marks and grooves from the scoop blade run parallel to the trench walls across the floor.
- **Tailings & Spoil Piles**:
  - At the far (upper-right) end of the trench, excavated soil forms a rounded, conical mound piled above the vertical headwall.
  - At the near (bottom-center) end of the trench, a cluster of angular, crumbly soil clods and small pebbles is spilled across the rim and lower slope.
- **Surrounding Terrain**: The undisturbed surface around the trench has a pebbled, granular texture with scattered small rocks, including a prominent light-toned angular rock near the middle-left edge and a flat, polygonal slab at the lower-left corner. A faint outline of the shadow boundary remains visible around the trench perimeter where the shadow brightness was digitally matched to the sunlit terrain.
