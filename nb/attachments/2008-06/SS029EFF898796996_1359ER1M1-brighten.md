# Side-by-Side Comparison of Brightened Phoenix Scoop Over TEGA (`SS029EFF898796996_1359ER1M1-brighten.jpg`)

## Overview
This side-by-side grayscale comparison image shows a Sol 29 photograph from NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander before (left) and after (right) applying a shadow/highlight adjustment in Adobe Photoshop CS3 (`Image -> Adjustments -> Shadow/Highlight` with 50% Shadows and 0% Highlights), as described in the June 24, 2008 blog post (*"Brightening another Phx Image"*). Both frames depict the Robotic Arm's scoop poised directly above the Thermal and Evolved-Gas Analyzer (TEGA) instrument on the lander deck.

## Scene & Hardware Details
- **Robotic Arm Scoop (Upper Left of Each Frame)**:
  - In the **left (original)** frame, the underside and interior cavity of the suspended Robotic Arm scoop are in deep shadow, making the contents nearly impossible to discern.
  - In the **right (brightened)** frame, the shadow adjustment reveals a mound of fine-grained, clumpy Martian soil resting inside the scoop's front trough, along with the mechanical joints, bolts, and metallic housing at the top of the scoop assembly.
- **Lander Deck & Calibration Targets (Center & Lower Half)**:
  - Below the scoop sits the flat metallic deck of the Phoenix lander, with the Thermal and Evolved-Gas Analyzer (TEGA) unit occupying the lower-left foreground (showing its row of slotted oven doors and wiring harnesses, with loose Martian dirt spilled onto the leftmost edge).
  - Mounted on the deck near the center of each frame is the circular **Phoenix DVD** (carrying the Planetary Society's * Visions of Mars* collection and mini-brick calibration target) next to a small rectangular **U.S. flag plaque** and circular cable/fastener ports.
  - In the lower-right foreground is an angled structural bracket and housing on the lander deck.
- **Background Martian Surface**: Beyond the deck edge, the rocky, polygonal Arctic Martian plain stretches across the upper-right background, with a geometric corner of one of Phoenix's decagonal solar panels visible at the top-left behind the scoop.
