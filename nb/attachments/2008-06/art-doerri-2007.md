# Art Trembanis Working on the DOERRI AUV (`art-doerri-2007.png`)

## Overview
A vertical color photograph showing marine scientist Art Trembanis of the University of Delaware crouching inside a workshop or shipboard laboratory to inspect the internal electronics and battery compartment of the DOERRI (Delaware Oceanographic and Environmental Research and Reconnaissance Instrument) autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV). The photo accompanied an August 2007 *Shipwreck Central* article about joining Robert Ballard's expedition in the Black Sea to survey ancient shipwrecks and seafloor dynamics.

## Scene Description
- **Subject**: Art Trembanis is crouched behind the forward section of the AUV, looking down intently at the open electronics bay. He wears rimless rectangular glasses, a blue-and-white checkered button-down shirt, khaki trousers, a dark navy fleece vest with a white inner collar and a small chest logo, and a carved bone fishhook pendant necklace.
- **AUV Nose Cone & Hull**: In the lower foreground, the rounded, torpedo-shaped nose cone of the AUV angles toward the bottom-left corner. It is painted bright yellow over the top/front with a white side panel.
  - On the yellow upper surface, the **University of Delaware** logo is printed in dark serif lettering ("UNIVERSITY OF DELAWARE" with an interlocking "UD" monogram).
  - On the white side panel to the right, the manufacturer logo reads **"sias patterson incorporated"** in black and grey sans-serif text with an orange dot above the "i" in "sias" (representing a Fetch-class AUV built by Sias Patterson).
- **Internal Components**: Just behind the nose cone, an open compartment reveals blue rectangular battery packs or electronics modules wired with a tangle of bright red cables and a yellow connector plug, bordered by a grey and black faceted metal cowling.
- **Background**: An industrial workshop or shipboard mechanical room with grey paneled doors on the right, a large metal compressor or pump assembly with gauges and piping on the left, yellow overhead pipes, and cardboard boxes on a shelf.
