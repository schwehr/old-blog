# UA Phoenix Mars Mission on iTunes U (`phx-itunes.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of the Apple iTunes application on Mac OS X displaying the **UA Phoenix Mars Mission** channel on **iTunes U** (hosted by the University of Arizona's Department of Planetary Sciences and Lunar and Planetary Laboratory), showing 14 free downloadable mission videos, animations, interviews, and press briefings.

## iTunes Window & Sidebar
- **Top Control Bar**: Standard brushed-metal Mac OS X iTunes title bar (`iTunes`) with playback controls (rewind, play, fast-forward), volume slider, central LCD status display showing the Apple logo, three view-mode toggle buttons (`View`), and a search bar (`Search iTunes Store`).
- **Left Sidebar**:
  - `LIBRARY`: `Music`, `Movies`, `TV Shows`, `Podcasts` (badge `21`), `Radio`
  - `STORE`: `iTunes Store` (highlighted in blue)
  - `PLAYLISTS`
  - Bottom-left preview box labeled `Now Playing` displaying `Nothing Playing`.
- **Breadcrumb Bar**: Above the main store pane, navigation arrows and a home icon lead to the path:
  `iTunes U > The University of Arizona > Sciences > UA Phoenix Mars Mission - Landing`, with a `Sign In` button on the far right.

## Channel Header & Description
- **Logo Artwork**: On the left is the circular Phoenix Mars Mission emblem (a stylized blue-and-orange phoenix rising over Mars) above a red banner reading **`A THE UNIVERSITY OF ARIZONA.`** with a reflective mirror effect below it.
- **Title & Metadata**:
  - Heading: **`UA Phoenix Mars Mission`**
  - Subheading: **`Department of Planetary Sciences, Lunar and Planetary Laboratory`**
  - `Last Modified: Thu Jun 5, 2008`
  - `Tracks in Landing: 14`
  - Two rounded gray buttons: **`GET TRACKS`** and **`SUBSCRIBE`**.
- **Description**:
  - *"The Phoenix mission is led by the University of Arizona on behalf of NASA. Project management of the mission is by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Spacecraft development is by Lockheed Martin Space Systems."*
  - *"The spacecraft launched from Cape Canaveral Air Force Station on Aug. 4, 2007, and landed successfully on May 25, 2008. On Mars' northern plains, Phoenix will examine the water-ice known to exist under the top soil surface and look for clues that Mars has been or could be a habitable environment."*
- **Right Sidebar Boxes**:
  - **`TELL A FRIEND`** (with a red right-arrow icon)
  - **`LINKS`**: `The University of Arizona`, `Department of Planetary Sciences, LPL`, `Phoenix Mars Mission`.
