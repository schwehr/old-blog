# Apple Remote Desktop Controlling a Six-Display Mac Setup (`remote-desktop-6heads.png`)

## Overview
A wide macOS screenshot of an Apple Remote Desktop (ARD) window monitoring and controlling a "6-headed" (six-display) Mac workstation used to drive a 60-foot, 5-projector panorama display wall (at 1280×1024 per projector, plus one prep screen) during the Phoenix Mars Lander mission operations.

## Interface & Controls
- **Window Title Bar & Toolbar**:
  - At the top left are the standard Mac OS X red, yellow, and green window buttons. The center title text is blanked out with a white-to-gray rectangle for privacy.
  - Below the title bar, the Apple Remote Desktop toolbar features six mode/control buttons on the left (Observe/Control toggle, curtain/lock screen, scaling, full screen, camera screenshot, copy/paste clipboard buttons), followed by a display selector dropdown menu reading `All Displays: 8320 x 1200`.
  - On the far right of the toolbar is a quality slider and color depth indicator icon.

## Six-Display Desktop Layout
- **Display 1 (Far Left — Prep Screen)**:
  - Shows a taller vertical resolution (`1200` pixels high) compared to the projector screens, with the default Mac OS X green anemone / clownfish desktop wallpaper and the macOS Dock along the bottom edge.
  - Several open windows are visible on the desktop, including `System Preferences` in the foreground, a Finder/terminal window behind it on the left, and desktop drive/folder icons along the right edge of that screen.
- **Displays 2–6 (Right — Five Projector Screens)**:
  - Spanning horizontally across the remaining five monitors is a wide grayscale Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) panorama of the Martian arctic plain taken from the deck of the Phoenix Mars Lander.
  - In the foreground of the Mars panorama are the circular decagonal solar panel (bottom center-left) and lander deck instrumentation including the meteorology mast and robotic arm components (center-right).
  - A small yellow sticky note or text box floats near the upper center of the panorama.
  - Below the bottom edge of the Mars panorama window, strips of each monitor's desktop wallpaper are visible across the 5 projector displays (from left to right: black space with a blue Earth globe arc, green sea anemone, orange-brown striped rock/wood texture, gray smooth stones, and blue water ripples).
