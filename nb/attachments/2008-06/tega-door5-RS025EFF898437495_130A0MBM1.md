# Phoenix Robotic Arm Camera View of TEGA Door 5 and Soil Pile (`tega-door5-RS025EFF898437495_130A0MBM1.jpg`)

## Overview
This grayscale close-up photograph taken by the Robotic Arm Camera (RAC) on NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander on Sol 25 (June 19–20, 2008) shows the top of the Thermal and Evolved-Gas Analyzer (TEGA) instrument after an attempt to open the doors of Oven 5, adjacent to the soil-covered Oven 4. Featured in the June 20, 2008 blog post (*"more on phx ice"*), the image illustrates how clumpy Martian soil covered Cell 4 while the adjacent doors on Cell 5 only partially opened due to a mechanical interference issue.

## Scene & Hardware Details
- **TEGA Slotted Oven Doors & Mesh Screen (Lower Left to Center)**:
  - A row of metallic oven cover doors angles across the lower-left quadrant of the frame. The leftmost visible doors are closed, while the doors of Oven 5 (just left of the soil pile) are partially ajar at an upward angle rather than fully retracted.
  - Directly to the right of the partially open doors, a large mound of dark, clumpy Martian soil sits piled atop Oven 4 from previous sample delivery and vibration attempts. At the lower end of Oven 4, the perforated metallic mesh screen (designed to sieve fine particles into the narrow oven port) is partially visible beneath the dirt, framed by raised metal side baffles.
- **Lander Deck & Background Components**:
  - A shiny, cylindrical metal tube runs diagonally behind the top edge of the TEGA housing, catching bright specular sunlight on the left and wrapped in a dark cable sleeve toward the upper right.
  - Scattered dark soil grains and clumps are dusted across the surrounding metallic deck surface to the right and upper left of the TEGA chute.
  - In the upper-center background, a circular flanged opening and a rectangular bracket with two small fasteners are visible in the shadowed recess of the lander deck.
