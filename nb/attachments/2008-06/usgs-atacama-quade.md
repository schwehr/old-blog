# Researcher Jay Quade Sampling a Test Pit in the Atacama Desert (`usgs-atacama-quade.jpg`)

## Overview
This color field photograph by Julio L. Betancourt (U.S. Geological Survey) shows researcher Jay Quade kneeling inside a shallow hand-dug test pit in the hyper-arid Atacama Desert of Chile. Featured in the June 23, 2008 blog post (*"Microbes and Mars by AP wire"*) via a 2004 National Science Foundation press release, the image illustrates Earth's closest terrestrial analogue to the dry, rocky Martian landscape where microbial life was discovered 8 to 12 inches below the surface.

## Scene Description
- **Researcher & Test Pit (Center Foreground)**:
  - Researcher Jay Quade, wearing a light blue/gray long-sleeved shirt and a dark wide-brimmed sun hat, sits/kneels waist-deep inside an excavated pit in the desert floor, facing right as he examines or samples the pit wall for soil carbonates.
  - Directly behind him to the right rises a rounded mound of excavated tan/brown soil.
  - Around the rim of the pit sit field equipment and supplies: a dark blue/black backpack and a light-colored field notebook or sample tray immediately in front of him, a short-handled metal shovel resting on the ground to his left, and a black backpack, yellow field book, rock hammer/pick, and clear plastic sample bag in the lower-left foreground.
- **Mars-Like Desert Terrain & Background**:
  - The surrounding desert plain is a vast, barren expanse of brownish-gray dirt and gravel strewn with hundreds of dark, angular, wind-weathered rocks and boulders of varying sizes, closely resembling surface panoramas from Mars landers.
  - In the background, a dark, treeless mountain ridge angles down from the upper-left toward the middle-right beneath a cloudless, vivid blue sky.
