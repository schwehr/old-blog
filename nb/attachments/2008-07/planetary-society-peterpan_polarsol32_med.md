# Partial Polar Projection Panorama of the Phoenix Mars Lander Site ("Peter Pan") (`planetary-society-peterpan_polarsol32_med.jpg`)

## Overview
A circular polar-projection color mosaic (nicknamed the "Peter Pan" panorama, assembled by James Canvin and shared via The Planetary Society) showing the 360-degree view around the NASA Phoenix Mars Lander up to Sol 32. Because not all lander deck images had been downlinked yet, the center of the circle contains black polygonal gaps.

## Mosaic Details
- **Projection & Framing**:
  - The image is mapped in a "little planet" stereographic polar projection, forming a rust-orange sphere of Martian terrain set against a smooth tan-orange gradient background.
- **Lander Deck & Solar Arrays (Center)**:
  - At the center of the globe, two circular decagonal fan-folded solar panels extend to the left (around the 8 o'clock position) and upper right (around the 2 o'clock position).
  - Large irregular black polygons occupy the center where unacquired or un-downlinked frames of the lander deck would be located.
  - Metallic components of the lander deck glint along the edges of the black cutout regions.
- **Martian Terrain & Workspace**:
  - Surrounding the lander is the polygonal patterned ground of Vastitas Borealis, covered in reddish-brown soil, small pebbles, and shallow troughs.
  - At the 1 o'clock position near the horizon, the robotic arm scoop/workspace or distant hardware appears as a dark elongated feature extending toward the rim.
  - At the 3 o'clock position on the right side, dark trenched soil ("Wonderland" / "Snow White" trenching area) is visible.
  - Below the center (around the 6 o'clock position), a dark curved arc marks the edge of a stitched image boundary across the smooth soil.
