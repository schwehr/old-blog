# University of Arizona Phoenix Mars Mission Flickr Gallery (`ua-mars-flickr.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of a Flickr photo set gallery page hosted by the University of Arizona (`Mars`), showcasing 26 photographs from the NASA Phoenix Mars Lander mission taken between May 5, 2008, and June 26, 2008. The screenshot was featured in a July 2, 2008 blog post titled *"UofA Mars Website and Flickr"*, highlighting how the University of Arizona Mars website integrated a Flickr photo stream to display mission imagery.

## Interface & Layout
- **Top Header Bar:**
  - On the far left is the block **"A"** logo of the University of Arizona (red inner block "A" outlined in white and dark navy blue).
  - Next to the logo is the bold black title **"Mars"**, with three horizontal navigation links directly below it: **"Thumbnails"** (selected, in bold black text), **"Detail"** (blue link), and **"Comments"** (blue link), separated by vertical gray pipe dividers.
  - Toward the center-left is the classic **"flickr"** logo ("flick" in blue, "r" in pink) with the small light-gray superscript tagline *"LOVES YOU™"* above the "kr".
- **Left Column (Featured Set Cover & Metadata):**
  - A light gray rectangular frame holds a landscape-oriented cover photograph from the High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment (HiRISE) showing layered, terraced, light-toned geological strata and fracturing on the Martian surface.
  - Below the cover image is the set title: **"Phoenix Mars Mission"**.
  - Beneath the title, gray metadata text reads: **"26 photos | 30 views | [Add a comment](blue link)?"**
  - A second line of gray metadata text states: **"items are from between 05 May 2008 & 26 Jun 2008."**

## Thumbnail Grid Details
On the right side of the page is a 6-column by 5-row grid containing 26 square image thumbnails (4 full rows of 6 thumbnails, plus 2 thumbnails on the bottom row):
- **Row 1 (Orbital & Landing Views):**
  1. Grayscale HiRISE view of finely layered Martian terrain (matching the set cover image).
  2. Color-enhanced orbital view of dark brown and bright white frost/ice-covered polygonal terrain.
  3. Textured pinkish-tan Martian surface covered in small polygonal cracks.
  4. False-color blue and tan orbital view of a steep scarp or crater rim shadow.
  5. High-contrast black-and-white view from the lander looking across its circular decagonal solar array panels toward the Martian horizon.
  6. Black-and-white view looking down at one of the Phoenix lander's circular footpads resting on dark soil and a bright patch of exposed subsurface material.
- **Row 2 (Surface Close-ups & Under-Lander Views):**

  2. Nearly solid black frame with two tiny bright white specks near the center (astronomical or atmospheric observation).
  3. Black-and-white view showing alander leg strut and footpad on the ground.
  4. Black-and-white view from underneath the lander deck showing mechanical struts and a brightly lit patch of smooth ground (the "Holy Cow" exposed ice feature beneath the thrusters).
  5. Grayscale view of a robotic arm or instrument component beside flat soil.
  6. Grayscale close-up of a smooth, wind-sculpted or sublimating rock/ice feature beneath the lander.
- **Row 3 (Robotic Arm, Trenches & Microscopy):**
  1. Overhead color view of reddish-orange polygonal ground near the lander.
  2. Color view of tan soil with small rocks and the dark shadow of the robotic arm scoop.
  3. Black-and-white oblique view of an open instrument enclosure on the lander deck.
  4. Color view of smooth tan soil and rocks with a yellow-and-gray structural component in the lower left corner.
  5. Grayscale close-up of a vertical soil trench wall excavated by the robotic arm.
  6. Split-view circular microscopy image showing fine soil particles on the left and a rainbow-colored optical/interferometric fringe ring on the right.
