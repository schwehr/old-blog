# A List Apart "Take Control of Your Maps" Paint-by-Numbers Illustration (`alistapart-openlayers-spash-map.jpg`)

## Overview
An editorial pen-and-ink and watercolor wash illustration of a paint-by-numbers world map alongside a small paint pot strip and paintbrush, created as the splash artwork for the *A List Apart* article *"Take Control of Your Maps"* (an introduction to OpenLayers). Kurt Schwehr included the illustration in his August 29, 2008 blog post *"GeoDjango on This Week in Django podcast"*.

## Illustration Details
- **Paint-by-Numbers World Map:**
  - A rectangular, double-bordered world map is drawn in loose, hand-sketched black ink lines on a white background, showing the outlines of North and South America, Greenland, Europe, Africa, Asia, and parts of Indonesia/Australia.
  - Inside the continents and ocean basins are hand-lettered black digits indicating a "paint-by-numbers" key:
    - The number **`1`** appears four times in the oceans: in the eastern Pacific Ocean off South America, in the North Atlantic Ocean, in the South Atlantic Ocean, in the Indian Ocean, and in the western Pacific Ocean on the far right (five `1`s total in ocean areas).
    - The number **`2`** appears four times on the continents: in central North America, central South America, central Africa, and across Eurasia.
  - **Shaded / Painted Regions:** South America is almost completely filled in with a medium-gray watercolor wash, while southwestern North America (Mexico and the U.S. Southwest) and western Africa have partial, lighter gray watercolor washes applied.
- **Paint Pots & Brush (Lower Left):**
  - Overlapping the lower-left corner of the map frame is a connected plastic strip of four small circular paint pots:
    - The left pot lid is closed and marked **`1`**.
    - The upper-left pot lid is closed and faintly marked **`2`**.
    - The center pot is **open**, showing dark gray/black paint inside.
    - The right pot lid is closed and marked **`3`**.
  - Resting diagonally across the bottom-left border of the map is a slender artist's paintbrush with a dark gray handle and a black-tipped bristles pointing toward South America.
