# Stone Daybeacon in Boston Harbor (`boston-day-marker.jpg`)

## Overview

A close-up telephoto photograph of a historic masonry daybeacon (daymarker) sitting atop a granite block crib on a rocky shoal in Boston Harbor, taken on August 20, 2008 during a photographic survey of maritime navigation aids by Kurt Schwehr and Matthew Plumlee (posted August 21, 2008 in "Boston photo survey").

## Scene Description

- **Conical Daybeacon (Center)**:
  - Rising from the center of the stone platform is a tall, multi-sided (octagonal) pyramidal or conical stone/brick tower.
  - The tower is painted in three horizontal bands: a black base section, a wide white middle band, and a black upper cone.
  - A single seagull perches at the very top apex of the cone, facing right.
- **Granite Block Base & Riprap (Lower Half)**:
  - The conical marker rests on a rectangular retaining wall constructed of roughly dressed, dry-laid gray granite blocks stacked about six to seven courses high.
  - Low green coastal brush and weeds grow along the flat top of the stone crib around the base of the cone, with a taller tuft of grass on the right.
  - Below the cut-granite wall, dark brown and gray boulders, cobbles, and seaweed-covered riprap slope down toward the water's edge at low tide.
- **Background**:
  - Narrow strips of blue harbor water and distant tree-lined mainland shore are visible to the left and right of the stone structure beneath a clear, cloudless light blue sky.
