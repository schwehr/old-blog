# Global Map of Commercial Shipping Traffic – Small (`GlobalShippingImpactsHalpernetal-sml.jpg`)

## Overview
A smaller, web-sized version of the global Mollweide-projection map from Halpern et al. (2008) illustrating the spatial distribution and relative intensity of global commercial shipping traffic across the world's oceans.

## Map & Data Details
- **Continents**: World landmasses are filled in solid dark charcoal gray against a white exterior background.
- **Color Scale & Shipping Lanes**: Vessel traffic density is depicted using a heatmap color gradient from dark blue (lowest) through cyan, yellow, and orange to bright red (highest):
  - **Major Shipping Arteries (Red/Orange)**: Prominent red corridors trace the North Atlantic and North Pacific trade routes, the U.S. East, Gulf, and West Coasts, the Panama Canal approaches, the English Channel, the Mediterranean-Suez-Red Sea route, the northern Indian Ocean route around India and Sri Lanka through the Strait of Malacca, and the East Asian coastal corridors up to Japan, as well as coastal routes along South America and Africa.
  - **Secondary Routes & Background Traffic (Yellow/Cyan/Blue)**: Yellow and cyan shading marks moderate traffic across the northern oceans and along long-distance trans-oceanic tracks connecting South America, southern Africa, Australia, and Hawaii, while deep blue lines and white gaps appear in the remote South Pacific, Southern Ocean, and polar regions.
