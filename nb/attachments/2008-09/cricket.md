# Close-Up of a Field Cricket in the Garden (`cricket.jpg`)

## Overview

This macro photograph shows a dark field cricket resting on a textured stone or concrete garden block. It was posted on September 14, 2008 in the blog entry *"Mid-September Garden"*, where the author noted a late-summer population explosion of crickets in the lawn.

## Scene Description

- **Subject (Cricket)**:
  - A glossy black and dark brown field cricket (*Gryllus* sp.) is positioned near the center of the frame, facing toward the lower-left foreground.
  - Its rounded, shiny black head and pronotum reflect natural daylight, with its slender antennae extending forward to the left and right.
  - Both large, muscular hind jumping legs (femora and spiny tibiae) are raised symmetrically on either side of its abdomen, while its front and middle walking legs grip the rough stone surface.
  - At the rear of the abdomen (pointing toward the upper right), two long cerci and a central ovipositor extend outward.
- **Background & Surroundings**:
  - The cricket sits on the flat, weathered, yellowish-tan surface of a stone or concrete block with a slight greenish moss or lichen tint.
  - Along the upper-right edge of the stone, a dried, papery brown plant leaf or husk lies diagonally across the corner.
  - On the left side of the frame, a bright green vertical grass blade or plant stem rises sharply in the foreground against an out-of-focus background of light gray gravel pebbles and brown soil.
