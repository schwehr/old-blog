# Fall Webworm Silk Nest Enclosing Tree Leaves (`fall-webworms-1.jpg`)

## Overview

This close-up outdoor photograph shows a large silken web nest spun by fall webworms (*Hyphantria cunea*) enveloping skeletonized brown leaves on a deciduous tree branch. It was published on September 14, 2008 in the blog post *"Fall webworms"*, taken on the south shore of Lake Winnipesaukee at the end of July.

## Scene Description

- **Webworm Nest & Skeletonized Leaves**:
  - Occupying the center and left foreground is a woody twig bearing several leaves that have been completely stripped of their green photosynthetic tissue, leaving translucent, papery tan-brown leaf skeletons with dark brown veins and scattered black specks of caterpillar frass (droppings).
  - Fine, translucent white silk webbing stretches tautly between the branches and curled dead leaves, forming a protective tent-like enclosure across the center of the frame.
  - Several small, dark,fuzzy caterpillar silhouettes and dark pupal/molted remnants are visible inside the webbing near the center and upper right.
- **Surrounding Foliage**:
  - Healthy, serrated bright green birch/alder-like leaves frame the upper-left and upper-right corners of the image, while a dense backdrop of out-of-focus dark green bushes fills the right background.
