# Close-Up of Fall Webworm Caterpillars Inside Their Silk Web (`fall-webworms-2.jpg`)

## Overview

This macro photograph provides a detailed close-up view of numerous fall webworm caterpillars (*Hyphantria cunea*) crawling and feeding on skeletonized leaves inside a dense silken web. It is the second photograph in the September 14, 2008 blog post *"Fall webworms"*, captured near the south shore of Lake Winnipesaukee.

## Scene Description

- **Caterpillars**:
  - More than a dozen small, hairy yellowish-tan and brown caterpillars with dark dorsal stripes and tufts of bristles are scattered throughout the frame.
  - In the upper-left corner, two distinct caterpillars crawl upward in profile against the bright white sky/webbing, clearly showing their segmented bodies, dark heads, and fine lateral hairs.
  - Additional caterpillars are clustered in the central crevices between the curled brown leaves and along the lower-left and lower-center leaf veins.
- **Damaged Leaves & Silk Webbing**:
  - The left half of the image features a large, curled, dried brown leaf with a prominent dark venation network.
  - To the right of center, another leaf is partially skeletonized, showing patches of remaining olive-green tissue alongside lacy, chewed-out holes where only the fine vein mesh remains.
  - Thick sheets of translucent white silk webbing span the right and upper portions of the frame, speckled with numerous tiny black pellets of caterpillar frass.
