# Radar and Flight Track Visualization of FedEx Planes Diverting Around a Storm (`fedex-air-traffic.png`)

## Overview

This still frame from a YouTube animation (*"Highly Skilled Air Traffic Controller"*) shows an air traffic and weather radar visualization of FedEx cargo aircraft diverting around a severe thunderstorm band as they approach the FedEx superhub in Memphis, Tennessee. It was featured in a September 23, 2008 blog post titled *"FedEx flight control"*.

## Map & Weather Radar Details

- **Base Map**:
  - A light gray map shows state boundaries in thin gray lines (including Tennessee, Arkansas, Mississippi, Missouri, and Kentucky) and major rivers (the Mississippi, Ohio, and Tennessee Rivers) in light blue lines.
  - Memphis (`MEM`) is labeled in small blue text just south of the main storm cell in southwestern Tennessee.
- **Weather Radar Overlay**:
  - A strong west-to-east line of thunderstorms stretches across the right-center and right side of the frame, just north of the Memphis airport hub.
  - The storm intensity is color-coded with a large green precipitation shield on the east, bordered by yellow moderate-intensity bands and several intense red/dark-orange convective cores running horizontally just north of Memphis.
- **Aircraft Symbols**:
  - Dozens of small black aircraft icons (representing inbound FedEx flights) form single-file streams curving around the western edge of the red storm cells from the north and west, funneling through the gap between the storm cores to land at Memphis from the south and west.
