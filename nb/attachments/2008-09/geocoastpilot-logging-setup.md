# GeoCoastPilot Shipboard AIS and GPS Logging Setup (`geocoastpilot-logging-setup.jpg`)

## Overview
An interior photograph of a temporary data-logging workstation set up on a counter inside a research boat cabin during a GeoCoastPilot survey on August 20, 2008. The setup includes an Apple MacBook Pro laptop running iPhoto and an ACR Nauticast SR162G AIS receiver with external GPS and VHF antennas mounted on the cabin window.

## Workstation & Equipment
- **Laptop (Left)**: A silver 15-inch Apple MacBook Pro resting on the wooden cabin counter.
  - **Screen**: Displays Apple iPhoto with a photograph of a green steel truss bridge spanning across water (the Tobin Bridge in Boston) centered in the main viewer pane, and the Mac OS X Dock along the bottom of the screen.
  - **Tape**: Strips of black electrical/gaffer tape are stuck to the counter in front of the laptop to help secure cables or gear against boat motion.
- **AIS Receiver & Cables (Right)**:
  - A blue metallic box sitting on the counter in the foreground, labeled `SR162G` and `AIS Receiver`, connected via a gray serial/USB adapter cable, power lead, and antenna coax cables.
  - A tangle of black and gray USB, serial, and power cables coiled behind the receiver.
  - CompactFlash/SD memory card readers and spare cards resting on the desk between the laptop and the AIS box.
  - A white power strip on the far right with a glowing red power switch, plugged into a dual wall outlet mounted in a wooden-framed electrical box on the cabin bulkhead.

## Window & Antennas
- **Cabin Window**: A wide, rounded-corner aluminum-framed marine cabin window occupies the upper half of the image, looking out over bright sunlit water with a distant tree-lined shore at the very top edge.
- **GPS Antenna**: A small square black magnetic/suction-mount GPS puck antenna is adhered directly to the center-right of the window glass, with a thin black cable trailing down to the receiver.
- **VHF/AIS Antenna**: A vertical black whip antenna is mounted via a bracket/suction cup to the right side of the window glass, connected by a thicker black coaxial cable leading down to the SR162G receiver.
