# USCGC Healy Aloftcon View Following CCGS Louis S. St-Laurent in Arctic Ice (`healy-aloftcon-20080919-0101.jpg`)

## Overview
A wide-angle photograph captured from the Aloftcon (aloft conning station) camera aboard the U.S. Coast Guard Cutter *Healy* (WAGB-20) on September 19, 2008 (`20080919-0101.jpeg`), during joint US-Canadian Arctic seafloor mapping and seismic operations. The view looks forward over *Healy*'s bow as it follows a open-water channel broken through the pack ice by the Canadian Coast Guard icebreaker *CCGS Louis S. St-Laurent* in the distance.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground (USCGC Healy Bow)**:
  - At the very bottom is the white metal safety railing of the aloft conning station or forward mast platform.
  - Below lies the curved foredeck and bow bulwark of USCGC *Healy*, illuminated on its port (left) inner wall by low-angle golden polar sunlight.
  - A central jackstaff/mast rises from the bow with a small red flag flying near the top. Orange life rings are mounted on the inside of the port and starboard bow bulwarks.
- **Ice Channel (Middle Ground)**:
  - A dark, slush- and brash-ice-filled open water lead cuts straight away from *Healy*'s bow toward the horizon, flanked on both sides by expansive white and pale blue-gray snow-covered Arctic sea ice floes.
  - Low sun casts long shadows and warm golden highlights across the hummocky ice ridges on the left and right.
- **Distant Vessel & Horizon (Background)**:
  - At the far end of the broken channel near the horizon (slightly left of center) is the red hull and white superstructure of the Canadian icebreaker *CCGS Louis S. St-Laurent*, breaking ice ahead of *Healy*.
  - Above the flat ice horizon is a pale yellow-gold and gray-blue polar sky with low stratus cloud bands.
