# Phoenix Mars Lander Upward-Looking Lidar Visualization (`mars-lidar.png`)

## Overview
A composite graphic from NASA's Phoenix Mars Lander mission (released September 29, 2008) illustrating how the lander's upward-looking green laser (lidar) beam operates on the Martian arctic plains and how it appears when captured by the Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) camera.

## Composition & Visual Elements
The image is set against a black background and consists of three overlapping visual components:
1. **Artist's Rendering of the Phoenix Lander (Bottom Right)**:
   - A 3D rendering of the Phoenix Mars Lander resting on the polygonal-patterned brown soil of the Martian northern plains, with its circular decagonal solar panels deployed to the left and right, its robotic arm extending down into a dug trench in the foreground, and its meteorological mast rising on the right.
   - A bright vertical green laser beam shoots straight upward from the Canadian-built lidar instrument on the lander's deck into the tan Martian sky.
2. **Green Projection Fan (Center)**:
   - A translucent green trapezoidal wedge extends leftward from the vertical lidar beam on the lander to a tall vertical strip image on the far left, illustrating the field of view captured by the camera looking up along the laser beam.
   - The left half of the lander rendering and surrounding Martian ground/sky is tinted bright green where the projection wedge overlaps it.
3. **SSI Lidar Image Strip (Far Left)**:
   - A tall, narrow vertical photographic strip with a brownish-gray noisy background (an Experimental Data Record from the Surface Stereo Imager, colorized from greyscale frames).
   - Running nearly vertically down the left-center of the strip is a thin, glowing pale-green line representing the actual laser beam scattering off dust and ice crystals in the Martian atmosphere.
