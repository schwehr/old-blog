# SS Manhattan Navigating the Northwest Passage in 1969 (`northwest-passage-manhattan-1969.jpg`)

## Overview
A historic black-and-white photograph taken in 1969 showing the specially reinforced U.S. supertanker **SS *Manhattan*** pushing through thick Arctic sea ice during its landmark voyage as the first commercial vessel to transit the Northwest Passage, escorted by a smaller icebreaker astern.

## Scene & Vessel Details
- **SS *Manhattan* (Foreground Left/Center):**
  - Viewed nearly head-on from a low angle on the ice ahead of the ship's massive, wide-flared icebreaking bow.
  - The dark hull is heavily scuffed with ice and snow along the bow plates, with the ship's name **`MANHATTAN`** painted in white capital letters on the starboard bow.
  - Above the wide weather deck rises a broad, multi-tiered white superstructure and bridge wings, topped by a central radar/communications mast and a tall forward mast on the forecastle. Billowing gray exhaust smoke drifts from the aft funnel toward the upper left.
- **Escort Icebreaker (Background Right):**
  - Positioned astern and to the port side (viewer's right) of the *Manhattan* is a smaller, narrower icebreaker (such as the CCGS *John A. Macdonald* or USCGC *Northwind*), viewed bow-on with a dark hull, white multi-deck forward superstructure, tall thick funnel, and mast with a crow's nest aloft, trailing dark smoke to the left.
- **Sea Ice Environment:**
  - The foreground and surrounding expanse are covered in fractured, snow-covered pack ice with pressure ridges, cleaved blocks, and a churned ice track between the two vessels. A faint vertical crease/scan line runs down the center of the archival print.
