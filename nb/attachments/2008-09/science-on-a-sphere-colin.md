# Colin Ware's Ocean Flow Visualization on Science on a Sphere (`science-on-a-sphere-colin.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken inside a darkened exhibition gallery at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History (Sant Ocean Hall) in Washington, D.C., showcasing Colin Ware's animated ocean current flow visualization projected onto NOAA's suspended *Science on a Sphere* globe display in September 2008.

## Scene Description
- **Science on a Sphere Globe**: Filling the right two-thirds of the frame is a large, glowing spherical projection screen suspended in mid-air.
- **Ocean Current Flow Visualization**: The ocean basins on the globe are rendered in deep navy blue and black, overlaid with thousands of vibrant, flowing streamlines and streaklets in neon green, yellow, white, and orange-red that trace major ocean circulation patterns (such as boundary currents, gyres, and eddies).
- **Continents & Polar Regions**: The continental landmass of North America appears in the upper-right portion of the sphere in pale, cloud-topped gray-green tones bordered by bright royal-blue coastal shelves, while a bright white polar cap glows at the bottom edge of the globe.
- **Foreground & Exhibition Hall**: In the lower-left foreground, the dark silhouette of a museum visitor's head and shoulders is visible looking up toward the sphere, with a dimly lit wall mural of yellow-and-blue clouds or waves behind them and dark ceiling rigging above.
