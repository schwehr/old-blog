# TeraVision Tiled Display Wall Demonstration at EVL (`teravision-evl.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph from the Electronic Visualization Laboratory (EVL) at the University of Illinois at Chicago showing a researcher interacting with a large tiled LCD display wall streaming a 3D global geophysical visualization from a laptop via the TeraVision system. Featured in the September 24, 2008 blog post *"TeraVision"*.

## Scene Description
- **Tiled Display Wall**: A large 5-column by 3-row array of 15 flat-panel LCD monitors mounted in a dark metal frame with narrow black bezels separating the tiles.
- **Visualization Content**: Across the 15 screens is a 3D perspective rendering of a global relief map of Earth against a black background. Oceans are shaded in deep blue, while continents (North and South America on the left, Africa and Europe in the center, and Asia on the right) are rendered in green, yellow, and brown elevation colors.
  - Several translucent white spheres with yellow centers float above seismic station locations around the globe, accompanied by small green and red 3-letter station labels (such as `BNN`, `BFO`, `BNI`).
  - A bright pink/white ray line extends diagonally from the upper right down through Europe and northern Africa toward the South Atlantic.
  - At the top center of the display (spanning the second and third columns of the top row) is white timestamp text reading `TURKEY-1999/08/17-00:01:39.1`.
  - Along the bottom edge of the globe visualization, large white serif text reads `WiggleView/world.iv`.
- **Foreground & Presenter**: In the right foreground, a woman with blonde hair tied in a low ponytail and wearing a red turtleneck sweater sits at a black-draped table in front of the display wall. She is extending her left arm and pointing with her index finger at a seismic region near Turkey/Europe on the center tile while resting her right hand near a dark gray Dell laptop. The laptop screen mirrors the same 3D globe visualization shown on the tiled wall.
