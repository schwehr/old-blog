# Terrestrial Lidar Point Cloud of Trees from UNM (`unm-lidar-trees.png`)

## Overview
A high-contrast black-and-white rendering of a 3D terrestrial laser scanning (lidar) point cloud depicting a grove of mature cottonwood/deciduous trees along a riverbank or levee path. The image originates from Jed Frechette's terrestrial lidar gallery at the University of New Mexico (UNM) and was featured in the September 21, 2008 blog post *"matplotlib and lidar"* discussing the `TLSpy` open-source Python lidar software.

## Scene Description
- **Point Cloud Rendering**: Rendered in grayscale (bright white points against a pure black background/sky), the image captures millions of laser return points illuminating the three-dimensional structure of the landscape.
- **Tree Canopy & Trunks**: Occupying the left and center-left two-thirds of the frame is a dense stand of tall, branching deciduous trees. The thick trunks and primary limbs appear bright white due to high laser reflectance/point density from the scanner's vantage point, while the fine twigs and leaves form a lacy, cloud-like canopy above. Additional trees are visible on the far right edge.
- **Ground & Embankment Features**:
  - Across the bottom foreground runs a bright white, straight raised path or concrete/gravel embankment wall bordering a dark void (water channel or shadow) at the very bottom left.
  - At the bottom right along the embankment is a small rectangular fenced platform or dock structure with horizontal railings and a vertical lamppost or sensor pole nearby.
  - Between the embankment and the tree trunks, the ground surface is delineated by parallel horizontal scan-line striations and long dark lidar shadow zones cast behind the tree trunks and fence posts away from the scanner location.
  - To the right of the main grove, a sloped path with stepped scan-line contours leads up toward an open grassy or brushy bank in the right midground.
