# Tomato Hornworm Larva on a Metal Ruler (`wikipedia-tomato-hornworm-larva.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up overhead photograph of a bright green tomato hornworm caterpillar (*Manduca quinquemaculata* larva) curled in a C-shape on a brushed steel carpenter's square or ruler to show its scale. Sourced from Wikipedia (`Image:Tomato_Hornworm_Larva_-_Relic38_-_Ontario_Canada.JPG`) and featured in the September 24, 2008 blog post *"Jeff Riffle's new website"* discussing hawk moth neuroecology research at the University of Arizona.

## Scene Description
- **Caterpillar Appearance**:
  - The plump, cylindrical larva is bright leaf-green with a smooth, segmented body covered in fine white pinhead dots (papillae) arranged in transverse rows across its dorsal segments.
  - Along its sides are faint whitish-yellow diagonal/V-shaped markings characteristic of *Manduca* hornworms, accompanied by small dark oval spiracles (breathing pores) ringed in pale rims along the lower lateral folds.
  - At the posterior end (lower left) projects a prominent, sharp,dark-brown/black caudal "horn" or spine with a pebbled texture.
  - The anterior head and thoracic segments (curled inward at the lower center/right) are tucked into a tight curve.
- **Ruler & Background**:
  - The caterpillar rests directly on the corner of a flat, brushed-metal L-square ruler placed over a rough gray concrete or stone surface (visible in the upper-left and upper-right corners).
  - Engraved inch and fractional-inch graduation marks run along both edges of the metal square, with the large stamped numeral `2` visible on both the left scale (near the posterior horn) and the right scale (beside the curved mid-body), indicating the curled caterpillar spans roughly 1.5 to 2 inches across.
