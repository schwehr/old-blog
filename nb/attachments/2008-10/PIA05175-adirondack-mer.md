# Mars Exploration Rover Spirit Front Hazcam View of "Adirondack" Rock (`PIA05175-adirondack-mer.png`)

## Overview
A wide-angle grayscale photograph (NASA/JPL Photojournal image `PIA05175`) captured by the front Hazard Avoidance Camera (Hazcam) on NASA's Mars Exploration Rover *Spirit*. The image shows the pyramidal volcanic rock named "Adirondack" on the plains of Gusev Crater, Mars, positioned directly in front of the rover between its two front wheels prior to Microscopic Imager (MI) and Rock Abrasion Tool (RAT) operations in early 2004.

## Scene Description
- **Central Feature ("Adirondack" Rock)**: Located near the center of the frame, "Adirondack" is a light-toned, roughly pyramidal or triangular-faced rock protruding from the Martian regolith. Its smooth, angled upper surfaces are illuminated from the upper right, while its lower-left base sits slightly recessed in a shallow wind-scoured depression or moat in the fine-grained soil.
- **Martian Terrain & Horizon**: Surrounding the central rock is an expanse of fine-grained, pebbled Martian soil scattered with numerous smaller angular rocks and cobbles extending toward a curved horizon at the top of the frame (curved due to the wide-angle fish-eye lens of the Hazcam). Faint rover wheel tracks or disturbed soil patches appear to the left of the rock.
- **Rover Hardware & Shadows**:
  - **Left Foreground**: The rover's left front cleated aluminum wheel and steering/suspension strut assembly are visible in the lower-left corner, angled slightly inward toward the rock.
  - **Right Foreground**: The rover's right front wheel and strut assembly occupy the lower-right corner, casting a dark shadow onto the soil to its left.
  - **Rover Deck Shadow**: Across the bottom center of the frame, the dark, angular silhouette of the rover's forward chassis and instrument deployment arm (IDD) turret casts a prominent shadow over the pebbled ground just below "Adirondack", with an elongated vertical rock fragment standing near the right edge of the shadow.
