# Opportunity Rover Context View of "Robert E" Outcrop with Microscopic Imager Inset (`PIA05235_MI_Context-B16R1-crop.jpg`)

## Overview
A cropped composite context image (derived from NASA/JPL Photojournal release `PIA05235`) showing the layered bedrock outcrop "Robert E" at Eagle Crater, Meridiani Planum, Mars, as imaged by NASA's Mars Exploration Rover *Opportunity*. The image blends a warm, false-color Panoramic Camera (Pancam) view of the rock outcrop and surrounding soil with a superimposed grayscale Microscopic Imager (MI) close-up patch indicating the exact location of a micro-topographic digital elevation model (DEM) study.

## Scene Description
- **Rock Outcrop ("Robert E")**: A fractured, flat-topped, tan-orange layered sedimentary rock slab dominates the center and right portions of the frame, separated by a deep, narrow diagonal crevice into a larger central block and a smaller right-hand block. To the upper left, smaller, finely laminated rock fins protrude from the dark soil at an angle.
- **Martian Spherules ("Blueberries")**: Small, dark-gray spherical hematitic concretions ("blueberries") are both embedded within the eroded laminae of the outcrop and scattered densely across the dark brown, fine-grained basaltic sand in the upper-right and upper-left background.
- **Superimposed Microscopic Imager (MI) Inset**: On the lower-left face of the central rock block, a small square grayscale photograph (corresponding to `PIA05237`) is registered and overlaid directly onto the color outcrop image. The grayscale patch highlights deeply weathered, diagonal rock laminae and a prominent spherical concretion protruding from the outcrop edge, providing spatial context for the high-resolution focal-stack DEM generated by Mark Lemmon and Kurt Schwehr.
