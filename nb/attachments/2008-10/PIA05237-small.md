# Opportunity Microscopic Imager Close-Up of Spherule in "Robert E" Outcrop (`PIA05237-small.png`)

## Overview
A high-resolution grayscale close-up photograph (NASA/JPL Photojournal image `PIA05237`) taken by the Microscopic Imager (MI) on NASA's Mars Exploration Rover *Opportunity* at the "Robert E" rock outcrop in Eagle Crater, Meridiani Planum, Mars. This image is one of a series of focal-stack exposures taken at varying stand-off distances by Mark Lemmon and used with Kurt Schwehr to construct a millimeter-scale 3D digital elevation model (DEM) in Fledermaus.

## Geological & Visual Details
- **Embedded Spherule ("Blueberry")**: In the lower-right quadrant, a well-rounded, spherical mineral concretion (approximately 4–5 mm in diameter) protrudes from the weathered rock matrix. Its surface displays a finely pitted, granular texture, and a narrow ridge of host rock remains attached to its left and right flanks, demonstrating how the harder concretion resists erosion relative to the softer surrounding rock.
- **Laminated Sedimentary Matrix**: Prominent, craggy rock layers (laminae) run diagonally from the upper left toward the lower right. The ridges are separated by deep, shadowed grooves, overhangs, and small cavernous voids formed by differential wind erosion (eolian abrasion) or chemical dissolution.
- **Fine Sand Grains**: Dark, fine sand grains and silt-sized particles are collected within the recessed pockets, crevices, and shallow depressions across the lighter-toned rock surface, particularly along the middle-left and lower-center troughs.
- **Depth of Field & Lighting**: Sunlight illuminates the scene from the upper right, casting sharp, dark shadows beneath the overhanging rock ledges and below the spherical concretion. Because of the Microscopic Imager's shallow depth of field (~3 mm), the central rock surfaces and spherule are in crisp focus while the highest ridges at the top left and deepest recesses at the bottom edge appear slightly soft.
