# Before-and-After Photoshop Comparison of Phoenix Mars Lander TEGA Soil Image (`RS138EFF908470172_1F8B0MDM1-photoshop.png`)

## Overview
A vertical two-panel comparison image created by Kurt Schwehr on October 17, 2008, illustrating a Photoshop shadow/highlight enhancement applied to a *Phoenix* Mars Lander Robotic Arm Camera (RAC) photograph (`RS138EFF908470172_1F8B0MDM1`, Sol 138) for a NASA/JPL press release (`PIA11236`: *"Soil Fills Phoenix Laboratory Cell"*).

## Panel Comparison
- **Top Panel (`Before`)**:
  - Labeled with orange sans-serif text reading **`Before`** on the middle-left side.
  - Displays the raw Experimental Data Record (EDR) grayscale image of Martian soil piled across cells 7, 6, 5, and 4 of the Thermal and Evolved-Gas Analyzer (TEGA).
  - The left side and upper-left background of the spacecraft deck are in deep, near-black shadow, making the cabling, fittings, and circular deck structures behind the TEGA cylindrical housing difficult to discern.
- **Bottom Panel (`After`)**:
  - Separated from the top panel by a thin white horizontal dividing line and labeled with orange sans-serif text reading **`After`** on the middle-left side.
  - Shows the global shadow-brightened version of the image, which lifts the dark tones across the entire frame.
  - **Revealed Shadow Details**: In the lower-left and upper-left shadowed regions, previously hidden hardware details become clearly visible, including a curved hose/cable with a ribbed connector at the lower left, a small rectangular tray containing soil in the upper-left corner, and a circular recessed bay with a curved rim and rectangular housing in the top-center background behind the horizontal TEGA cylinder.
  - **Soil & Door Contrast**: The dark clumpy soil over the central TEGA doors and perforated mesh screens (cells 7 and 4) appears lighter gray, with slightly reduced local contrast between the soil grains and the slanted metal doors compared to the top panel.
