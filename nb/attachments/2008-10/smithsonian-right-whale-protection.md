# Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History Webpage on Ocean Eavesdropping and Right Whale Protection (`smithsonian-right-whale-protection.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of a webpage from the **Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History** (`http://ocean.si.edu/ocean_hall/ocean_listening.html`) under **The Sant Ocean Hall** exhibition section, titled **"Ocean Eavesdropping: Scientists use their Ears to Protect Marine Mammals,"** which highlights WHOI D-Tags, Cornell University "pop-up" acoustic buoys, and the real-time North Atlantic right whale acoustic detection network in Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary off Boston.

## Main Article Content & Figures
- **Breadcrumbs**: `NMNH Home > Exhibitions > The Sant Ocean Hall >`
- **Headline**: `Ocean Eavesdropping: Scientists use their Ears to Protect Marine Mammals`
- **Body Text**: Explains how marine mammals rely on sound in deep, dark waters and how bioacoustics researchers use acoustic tags and bottom-mounted hydrophones to study marine mammals and protect endangered North Atlantic right whales (*Eubalaena glacialis*) from ship strikes around Boston and Cape Cod.
- **Figure 1 (Top Right)**:
  - **Image**: Close-up photograph of a white hydrodynamic **D-Tag** resting on four translucent suction cups, decorated with green flame decals and a small cartoon whale illustration on its casing, with a thin rear antenna.
  - **Caption**: *"Scientists use "D-tags" to record sounds marine mammals make and hear underwater and to gather data about how they travel. Image courtesy: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution"*
- **Figure 2 (Middle Left)**:
  - **Image**: High-angle indoor photograph of an engineering bay at WHOI where a technician works among several yellow-and-blue spherical/cylindrical right whale acoustic detection buoys and mooring frames.
  - **Caption**: *"An engineering assistant at WHOI's Coastal Research Laboratory puts finishing touches on a buoy for the right whale detection system. Image courtesy: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution"*
- **Figure 3 (Bottom Right)**:
  - **Image**: A map graphic titled **`368 Right Whale Calls Detected Within 24 Hours.`** showing Massachusetts Bay (`Boston`, `Cape Ann`, `Gloucester`, `Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary`, `Provincetown`, `Cape Cod Bay`), a `20 Nautical Miles` scale bar, a `Map Legends` box (`Shipping Lane`), and the shipping lane lined with 10 circular acoustic buoy zones—some green and several highlighted with orange/red right whale icons indicating active whale detections.
