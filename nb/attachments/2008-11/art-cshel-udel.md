# University of Delaware CSHEL Lab Webpage Screenshot (`art-cshel-udel.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot from the website of the Coastal Sediments, Hydrodynamics, and Engineering Lab (CSHEL) at the University of Delaware (`geology.udel.edu/cshel/`), led by Art Trembanis. Featured in a blog post on November 7, 2008, following Kurt Schwehr's visit and seminar at the University of Delaware Department of Geological Sciences.

## Layout & Visual Elements
The image has a dark charcoal-gray background divided into a 2x2 photo grid on the left and a global field site map on the right:

### Left Side: 2x2 Photo Grid
- **Top Left (`Hydrodynamics/ Sediment Transport`):** An oblique photograph of ocean waves breaking in parallel lines across shallow nearshore waters.
- **Top Right (`Autonomous Underwater Vehicles`):** An underwater photograph of a yellow torpedo-shaped Autonomous Underwater Vehicle (AUV) cruising just beneath the water surface, with two scuba divers visible in the blue water in the background.
- **Bottom Left (`Coastal Morphodynamics`):** An aerial photograph taken from a small airplane (with the wing strut/underside visible at top left) looking along a white sandy barrier beach and coastal lagoon/inlet.
- **Bottom Right (`A field day at the beach!`):** A group photograph of approximately twenty students and researchers standing and kneeling together on a sandy beach under an overcast sky.

### Right Side: Field Site Locations Map
- **Header Banner:** A dark glossy pill-shaped button at the top reads `Field Site Locations` in light gray text.
- **Legend:** Below the header, three 5-pointed star icons indicate project status:
  - Green star with red outline: `new`
  - Yellow star with red outline: `ongoing`
  - Solid red star: `previous`
- **World Map:** A color-coded global relief and bathymetry map (with blue oceans and green/yellow/brown/red continents) displaying star markers at field sites around the world:
  - **US East Coast & Gulf of Mexico:** A cluster of green (`new`), yellow (`ongoing`), and red (`previous`) stars along the Mid-Atlantic/Delaware coast, New England, the Southeast US, and the Gulf coast.
  - **Caribbean / Central America:** Red and green stars near Panama/Colombia.
  - **Europe / Black Sea:** A yellow star near the English Channel/UK and a green star in the Black Sea/Turkey region.
  - **Australia & New Zealand:** A yellow star and red star on the east coast of Australia, and a red star on the North Island of New Zealand.
