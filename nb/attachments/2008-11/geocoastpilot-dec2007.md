# Early Version of GeoCoastPilot Showing Portsmouth Harbor in 3D (`geocoastpilot-dec2007.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot from a December 2007 video demonstration of an early version of **GeoCoastPilot** (formerly known as the Digital Coast Pilot), developed at the UNH Center for Coastal & Ocean Mapping / Joint Hydrographic Center (CCOM/JHC) by Matthew Plumlee, Kurt Schwehr, Briana Sullivan, and Colin Ware. Featured in a blog post on November 28, 2008, titled "Chart of the Future Videos on YouTube."

## 3D Chart & Imagery View (Left)
- **Perspective View:** A 3D perspective rendering looking northward into the entrance of the Piscataqua River and Portsmouth Harbor (between New Castle, NH, on the left and Kittery/Gerrish Island, ME, on the right).
- **Bathymetry & Nautical Chart:** High-resolution shaded-relief multibeam bathymetry (in grayscale and blue depths) is seamlessly blended with a NOAA raster nautical chart over the surrounding land (yellow-green) and offshore approaches.
- **3D Geo-referenced Photographs:** Five framed coastal photographs stand vertically as 3D billboards above their corresponding geographic locations, connected to landmarks by white leader lines:
  - Upper left: The historic Wentworth by the Sea hotel on New Castle Island.
  - Upper center-right: Red range markers nestled in green trees on the shoreline.
  - Upper middle: Fort Constitution / Portsmouth Harbor Light area.
  - Center: A wide panoramic view of the Fort Stark / New Castle shoreline and lighthouse.
  - Lower center-right: Whaleback Light, a stone lighthouse tower at the harbor entrance.
- **Logo:** The circular **CCOM / JHC** (Center for Coastal & Ocean Mapping / Joint Hydrographic Center, University of New Hampshire) emblem appears in the bottom-left corner.

## Interactive Coast Pilot Text Pane (Right)
A scrollable white text panel on the right displays hyperlinked paragraphs from the NOAA *United States Coast Pilot* alongside clickable chart numbers (`Chart 13283`, `Chart 13274`, `Chart 13285`):
- **Paragraph (174):** Mentions `Cape Neddick`, `Portsmouth Harbor`, `York Harbor`, `Godfreys Cove`, and `Brave Boat Harbor`.
- **Paragraph (175):** `Brave Boat Harbor (43°06.0'N., 70°39.6'W.), 2 miles southwestward of York Harbor...` mentioning `Raynes Neck`.
- **Paragraph (176):** `Cutts Island, on the south side of the entrance, is connected with Gerrish Island to the south of it by a natural seawall of stones and rock thrown up by winter gales...`
- **Paragraph (177):** `Moores Rock, covered 5 feet and unmarked, is about 0.5 mile eastward of the entrance to Brave Boat Harbor...`
- **Paragraph (178):** `Two dangerous ledges are 2.5 miles offshore. York Ledge...` and `Murray Rock, 1.5 miles south-southwestward of York Ledge...`
