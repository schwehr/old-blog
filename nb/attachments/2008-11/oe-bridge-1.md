# NOAA Ship Okeanos Explorer Bridge Console (`oe-bridge-1.jpg`)

## Overview
An interior photograph of the bridge aboard the NOAA ship *Okeanos Explorer* (docked at Pier 66 in Seattle, Washington, during the eNavigation 2008 conference in November 2008), showing its teal-green helm, thruster, and electronic navigation consoles.

## Scene Description
- **Main Bridge Console**: A large wrap-around metal console painted teal/seafoam green, arranged facing a row of bright forward-looking bridge windows.
- **Foreground Control Panel**:
  - A black control panel labeled `THRUSTMASTER` on the lower-left edge, fitted with a black ball-top joystick, a horizontal T-bar lever, an analog dial gauge, digital readouts, and rows of green, red, and orange illuminated pushbuttons.
  - To the right of the Thrustmaster panel is a gray single-lever propulsion/thruster throttle quadrant with a tall black handle, accompanied by a small square illuminated indicator and silver toggle/push switches.
- **Center Monitors & Trackball**:
  - Two large recessed CRT/LCD display monitors in dark gray bezels sit side-by-side in the sloped center console (used for radar/Transas electronic charting).
  - Below the left monitor is a recessed black panel with trackball/rotary controls and a small joystick.
- **Right Annunciator Panel**: A sloped panel to the right of the dual monitors features a grid of rectangular indicator lights (many glowing orange/red) and three round red emergency/control knobs.
- **Left Wing Console**:
  - Two gray twin-lever engine/propulsion telegraph controls with black handles sit on the far left edge.
  - Behind them is an olive-green sloped console housing analog gauges and a helm wheel/control unit on top.
- **Windows & Overhead Equipment**:
  - Five forward and side bridge windows let in bright daylight; two of the forward windows have circular clear-view spinning window screens mounted in the glass.
  - Above the left windows are a small flat-screen CCTV monitor showing a deck/engine room view, two round wall clocks, and an overhead cylindrical sensor/camera unit hanging from the ceiling.
  - An overhead Pelorus/gyro repeater and gauges hang above the right windows.
