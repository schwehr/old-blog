# NOAA Ship Okeanos Explorer Telepresence Control Room (`oe-teleconsole.jpg`)

## Overview

A photograph of the telepresence and high-definition video control room aboard the NOAA ship *Okeanos Explorer*, used to control and broadcast HD video feeds over Internet2 (I2) during ocean exploration missions.

## Scene & Equipment Description

The dimly lit control room features a multi-tiered array of monitors, video switching consoles, and rack-mounted broadcast equipment arranged along dark walls and desks:

- **Foreground Workstation Row:**
  - Four flat-panel LCD monitors are lined up along the front desk facing the operators:
    - **Left Monitor:** Displays a turquoise computer desktop with desktop icons along the left edge and an open white application or browser window in the center.
    - **Center-Left Monitor:** Displays a presentation slide or document with a blue header banner titled `OKEANOS EXPLORER Shakedown` above bullet points and a map diagram.
    - **Center-Right Monitor:** Shows a document or slide featuring a false-color multibeam bathymetry plot (green, yellow, and red depth shading) alongside smaller inset photographs on the left.
    - **Right Monitors:** Two dark monitors angled at the far right end of the console.
  - Below the center-right monitor is an angled hardware control surface with two rows of illuminated yellow/amber push buttons and a dark status display panel.
- **Middle Desk Row:**
  - Several dark flat-panel monitors and laptops sit above specialized video production switchers, illuminated button matrices, joysticks, computer keyboards with blue wrist rests, mice, and a numeric keypad.
- **Upper Monitor Wall:**
  - **Top Row:** Seven small square/4:3 LCD preview monitors mounted in a horizontal row near the ceiling (powered off).
  - **Main Wall Displays:** Two large widescreen flat-panel displays mounted side by side below the top row, flanked on the right by a 2×2 grid of four smaller LCD monitors.
  - **Audio & Rack Gear (Far Right):** A studio monitor speaker with a yellow woofer cone is mounted high on the wall, and tall equipment racks in the background house illuminated red, amber, and green LED level meters and broadcast electronics.
