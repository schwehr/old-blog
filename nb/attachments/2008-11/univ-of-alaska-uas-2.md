# University of Alaska Autonomous Aircraft SkyHook Recovery on NOAA Ship Oscar Dyson (`univ-of-alaska-uas-2.jpg`)

## Overview

A three-panel horizontal composite photograph illustrating the shipboard recovery of a University of Alaska autonomous flying-wing aircraft aboard the NOAA ship *Oscar Dyson* in October 2008, using a vertical capture line suspended from a shipboard crane boom.

## Panel-by-Panel Description

- **Left Panel (Initial Capture on Recovery Line):**
  - The swept-wing autonomous aircraft—featuring a bright orange cylindrical fuselage/nose pod and natural wood-colored swept wings—hangs vertically by its wingtip hook after snagging the vertical recovery cable suspended over the water off the port side of the *Oscar Dyson*.
  - Five crew members on the working deck in the foreground wear orange life vests/float coats and hard hats (yellow, white, and red/orange) as they tend the recovery lines.
- **Center Panel (Close-Up on Capture Line):**
  - A closer view of the orange-and-wood flying wing suspended mid-air against the gray water and distant dark shoreline, showing the vertical recovery rope secured to the upper wingtip and a lower stabilizing line extending down toward the ship's rail.
- **Right Panel (Deck Crew Securing the Aircraft):**
  - The aircraft has been swung inboard to the ship's railing, where three crew members in hard hats (yellow, white, and black) and orange personal flotation devices reach out to grasp the bright orange fuselage pod and guide the wooden wing safely onto the deck.
