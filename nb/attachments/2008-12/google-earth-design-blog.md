# Google Earth Design Blog Review of Google's Geothermal Resource Project (`google-earth-design-blog.png`)

## Overview

A screenshot of a blog post from Rich Treves's *Google Earth Design* blog (`googleearthdesign.blogspot.com`), captured on December 20, 2008. The post reviews a 3D KML visualization of US geothermal energy resources created by Google.org.

## Visible Text & Visual Elements

- **Post Title**: At the top, rendered in orange-brown Georgia serif font:
  `Review: Google's Geothermal Resource Project`
- **Embedded Google Earth Screenshot**: Framed by a thin black border, an oblique 3D view of the contiguous United States on the Google Earth globe is shown against the black backdrop of space and a blue atmospheric limb:
  - **Extruded State Polygons**: Western states (such as Nevada, Oregon, Idaho, Utah, and California) are extruded upward as tall, dark-gray 3D prisms, while central and eastern states have lower, semi-transparent yellow-green and orange-tinted polygons with white borders.
  - **Floating Pie Charts**: Circular yellow, orange, and red 2D pie chart icons float above individual states across the country, representing geothermal resource data.
- **Image Caption**: Centered below the image in italics with blue hyperlinks:
  *`Screen shot from the `*`Geothermal Resource.kmz`*` by `*`Google.org`*
- **Body Paragraph**: Below the caption, black serif text reads:
  > As you would expect from Google this project is very well designed overall. As a result I've abandoned my usual template for reviewing projects and concentrated on noting good design points and things I think a] should be improved and b] could be improved. The [a] and [b] distinction is important, there are always things that could be improved with a project.
