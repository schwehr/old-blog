# Sunlight Through Ice-Covered Trees After New Hampshire Ice Storm (`post-storm-ice.jpg`)

## Overview
A color outdoor photograph taken outside the Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping (CCOM) at the University of New Hampshire in December 2008, capturing the aftermath of a major New England freezing rain and ice storm. Bright morning sunlight bursts directly through the branches of ice-coated trees above a shaded grassy slope.

## Scene Description
- **Sky & Sunburst**: In the upper left, a patch of vivid blue sky is framed by white clouds. Just left of center, the brilliant sun shines directly through the upper trunk and branches of a tall conifer/pine tree, creating a warm golden-white glare.
- **Ice-Encrusted Trees**: Across the middle and upper right, a dense stand of deciduous and evergreen trees is heavily coated in clear glaze ice. Where the sunlight hits the twigs and branches—particularly on the left and center—the ice sparkles in bright white and golden highlights. Taller dark green pine branches rise on the right side of the frame, weighed down by ice.
- **Foreground Slope**: A gentle hillside slopes down from right to left across the lower third of the image. Most of the grassy bank on the right lies in deep shadow, while the lower-left edge of the slope catches the bright sunlight, illuminating bent, ice-covered brush and fallen or drooping branches resting on the ground.
