# AGU 2008 Poster: Visualizing the Operations of the Phoenix Mars Lander (`schwehr-agu-2008.png`)

## Overview
A vertical scientific conference poster image presented by Kurt Schwehr and co-authors at the American Geophysical Union (AGU) Fall Meeting in December 2008 (Session IN41A-1137: Virtual Globes). The poster showcases 2D, 3D, web-based, and Google Earth visualizations developed by the JPL Solar System Visualization team for the NASA Phoenix Mars Lander mission.

## Header & Panorama Banner
- **Title Box**: Outlined in black at the top:
  - Title: `Visualizing the Operations of the Phoenix Mars Lander`
  - Authors: `Kurt Schwehr, P. Andres, J. Craig, R. Deen, E. De Jong, N. Fortino, Z. Gorjian, K. Kuramura, M. Lemmon, S. Levoe, C. Leung, N. Lutz, R. Ollerenshaw, P. Smith, M. Stetson, S. Suzuki, and the Phoenix Science Team`
- **Full-Width Panorama**: Directly beneath the title is a 360-degree surface panorama taken by the Phoenix lander showing its deck, circular decagonal solar panels, robotic arm, and the reddish-brown polygonal patterned ground of the Martian arctic plains. Inset in the top-left corner of the panorama is a Mars globe showing the northern polar landing site; inset in the top-right corner is an orbital close-up of the lander on the surface.

## Left Column Content
- **ABSTRACT Box**: Four paragraphs of text framed in black describing the JPL Solar System Visualization team's workflow:
  1. Overview of tools ranging from the web-based Image Products Release Website to custom 3D transformation software and studio-quality 2D/3D video production.
  2. Managing the "powers-of-10 problem" from polar dust storms down to the Atomic Force Microscope using Lightwave to blend 2D and 3D visualizations.
  3. The **Zooming and Interactive Mosaics (ZIM)** JavaScript web tool for displaying high-resolution panoramas in a spacecraft-centric view with feature and target identification.
  4. Using **Google Earth** by wrapping Mars textures and mapping Phoenix Mosaics onto the sphere in pseudo-"Street View" and globe modes.
- **Left Column Illustrations** (below the abstract):
  - Two overhead vertical projection views of the Phoenix lander deck and surrounding soil trenches (one rectilinear, one polar/fisheye projection).
  - Two under-deck views (one grayscale photographic view created by the Robotic Arm Camera looking under the lander at a footpad and ground ice, and one 3D computer rendering of the thruster nozzles and landing leg).
  - Bottom left: A realistic 3D rendering of the Phoenix Mars Lander with its deployed octagonal solar arrays and robotic arm set against the Martian terrain.

## Right Column Content & Logos
- **3D CAD & Instrument Visualizations** (top to middle right):
  - Row 1: A grey 3D CAD model of the lander deck and robotic arm alongside a rendered close-up of the deck instruments.
  - Row 2: Three translucent wireframe/X-ray CAD renderings showing internal mechanisms of the TEGA (Thermal and Evolved Gas Analyzer) and microscopy instruments.
  - Row 3: A translucent 3D rendering of the MECA (Microscopy, Electrochemistry, and Conductivity Analyzer) optical/atomic force microscope assembly next to a circular false-color microscopy image of Martian soil particles.
  - Row 4: A grayscale robotic arm scoop image with a yellow callout box zooming in on reddish-orange clods of Martian soil resting on the microscope sample wheel/substrate.
  - Row 5: A grayscale photo of the Surface Stereo Imager (SSI) camera head on its mast, paired with a screenshot of the `Phoenix IPRW: Mission Success Panorama` web interface (`SOL 100`).
- **Logos & Acknowledgements** (bottom right):
  - Three logos side by side: the circular **Phoenix Mars Lander** mission patch, the bold red **JPL** logo, and the circular **UNH Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping / Joint Hydrographic Center (CCOM/JHC)** logo.
  - **Acknowledgements Box**: `We would like to thank the entire Phoenix Team for a fantastically successful mission, NASA for funding the mission and the University of Arizona for hosting the Science Operations Center. For more information see http://schwehr.org/phoenix`
