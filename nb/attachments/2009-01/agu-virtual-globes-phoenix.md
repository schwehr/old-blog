# AGU 2008 Virtual Globes Poster Setup on Phoenix Mars Lander (`agu-virtual-globes-phoenix.png`)

## Overview
A photograph taken by John Bailey at the **2008 AGU Fall Meeting** showing Kurt Schwehr's interactive poster and dual-screen display setup in the **Virtual Globes at AGU** session (`IN41A-1137`), titled *"Visualizing the Operations of the Phoenix Mars Lander."*

## Scene & Display Setup
- **Session Banner (Top Left)**: Mounted on the grey poster board next to the booth number card `1137` is a white banner featuring:
  - A globe logo on the left and the title `Virtual Globes at AGU`.
  - `IN41A-1137` in the upper right above the `Google Earth` and `UAF` (University of Alaska Fairbanks) logos.
  - Paper title: `Visualizing the Operations of the Phoenix Mars Lander`.
  - Author list: `*K Schwehr, P Andres, I Craig, R Deen, E De Jong, N Fortino, Z Gorjian, K Kuramura, M Lemmon, S Levoe, C Leung, N Lutz, R Ollerenshaw, P Smith, M Stetson, S Suzuki, T Phoenix Science Team`.
- **Printed Poster (Right)**: A vertical scientific poster pinned to the right side of the board:
  - Header box: `Visualizing the Operations of the Phoenix Mars Lander` with the same list of co-authors and `and the Phoenix Science Team`.
  - Top panoramic banner: A Martian surface panorama looking across the Phoenix lander deck and solar panels.
  - Left column: An `ABSTRACT` text section above images of the Phoenix lander on the Martian surface, robotic arm trenching views, and the lander footpad/deck.
  - Right column: 3D CAD/visualization renderings of the lander's robotic arm and scoop, soil trenching models, panoramic camera slices, and the `JPL` (Jet Propulsion Laboratory) and NASA/Phoenix mission logos at the bottom.
- **Large LCD Monitor (Bottom Left)**: Sitting on the white table in front of the poster board is a black widescreen monitor displaying:
  - **Google Earth (Mars mode)**: The red globe of Mars centered on screen against black space, with the Google Earth navigation controls on the right and the `Layers` sidebar on the lower left.
  - **QuickTime Movie Window**: Overlaid on the upper-left portion of the monitor is a video player window showing dark imagery of the spacecraft/launch or lander hardware.
- **Apple PowerBook / MacBook Pro Laptop (Bottom Center)**: Placed on the white tablecloth to the right of the large monitor, with its power adapter brick and cables beside it, running companion visualization windows and grayscale imagery on its screen.
