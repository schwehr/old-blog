# Updated Monterey Bay Bathymetry in Google Earth (`ge-santa-cruz-updated.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of Google Earth taken in January 2009 showing newly updated, higher-resolution nearshore bathymetry in Monterey Bay and the Monterey Submarine Canyon off the coast of Santa Cruz and Monterey, California.

## Map & Bathymetric Features
- **Monterey Submarine Canyon**: Dominating the center and left of the view is the deep, winding channel of the Monterey Submarine Canyon cutting across the continental shelf from near Moss Landing (center-right) out toward the deep ocean floor (bottom-left). Smaller tributary canyons (such as Soquel Canyon to the north and Carmel Canyon to the south) branch off the main canyon.
- **Contour Artifacts**: Because the nearshore U.S. Coastal Relief Model was digitized from bathymetric contour lines, prominent concentric "terraced" or stepped shadow rings are visible along the continental slope and canyon walls, particularly on the broad dome-like slope in the central portion of the image.
- **Coastline & Landmass (Right)**:
  - At the top right is the northern shore of Monterey Bay with a red-dot city marker labeled `Santa Cruz` and a yellow highway line running along the coast.
  - Along the eastern curve of Monterey Bay, a narrow dark strip of coastal satellite imagery borders the blue bathymetric shading, with a thin pale line extending westward from the coast toward the canyon head.
  - Further south on the right is the Monterey Peninsula jutting out into the bay, with white circular highway shields for Route `156` and Route `1` along the yellow road network.
- **Navigation Controls**: In the upper-right corner are the Google Earth compass/look ring, pan wheel, and vertical zoom slider.
