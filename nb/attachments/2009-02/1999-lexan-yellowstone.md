# 1999 LExEn Proposal Website: "Robotic Exploration and Biological Sampling of Geothermal Vents" (`1999-lexan-yellowstone.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of a 1999 demonstration webpage illustrated by Peter Coppin for an NSF Life in Extreme Environments (LExEn) proposal by Peter Coppin, Mike Wagner, and Kurt Schwehr at Carnegie Mellon University's Field Robotics Center (FRC). The page describes a tethered underwater robotic vehicle designed to explore and sample geothermal hot springs in Yellowstone National Park.

## Layout & Navigation
- **Top Header Bar**: A salmon-red banner across the top with bold white sans-serif text reading: `Robotic Exploration and Biological Sampling of Geothermal Vents`.
- **Left Sidebar Navigation**: A vertical stack of overlapping circular badge icons against a pale blue-green page background, each with a curved white banner label along the bottom edge:
  1. `WHAT'S A GEYSER?` (photograph of an erupting geyser)
  2. `EXPLORE BEAD` (icy/steaming hot spring pool at Bead Geyser)
  3. `GEOLOGY` (cross-section diagram of rock strata)
  4. `BIOLOGY` (microscopic/filamentous illustration)
  5. `ROBOTICS` (3D rendering of the hot-spring robot)
  6. `3D MODEL` (terrain model with an orange `SITE 6` flag)
  7. `CREDITS` (bottom cap banner)

## Page Content & Illustrations
- **Main Text Block**:
  - **Heading**: `The robot: our eyes in the hot spring.`
  - **Body Paragraph**:
    > The interior of hot springs impose harsh requirements on the design of the underwater robotic system that will transport and deploy the science sensors. The vehicle has a cylindrical frame onto which sensor devices and water collection containers are attached. The vehicle is rigidly attached to a tether which supports the robot and transmits information. Attached to bottom of the robot will be different detachable modules, such as a downward-looking camera, sonar, or a manipulation arm.
- **Right Circular Badge**: A large circular inset on the right labeled `ROBOTICS` showing a top-down 3D illustration of the cylindrical robot descending into a rocky geothermal vent opening.
- **Bottom Center Diagram**: A two-part technical illustration showing a white and blue winch/sheave assembly on the left feeding a yellow tether down into a cracked, tan-brown rocky hot-spring orifice where the robot is being lowered. Overlapping on the right is a blue-bordered circular callout showing a side view of the white cylindrical robot suspended from its yellow tether inside the dark vertical shaft, with its lower robotic manipulator arm extended down and to the right.
