# NOAA Atlantic Hydrographic Branch Vessels including Thomas Jefferson and Bay Hydro II (`noaa-ahb-tj-bayhydro2.jpg`)

## Overview
A daytime photograph of several survey and research vessels moored along the piers at the NOAA Atlantic Hydrographic Branch (AHB) in Norfolk, Virginia, taken on February 25, 2009. The lineup includes the newly built catamaran *R/V Bay Hydro II*, the *Launch Explorer*, and the larger hydrographic survey ship *NOAA Ship Thomas Jefferson*.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Right**: A small blue-hulled survey launch with a white cabin and gray canvas/metal aft enclosure is moored in the immediate right foreground. Sunlight reflects off ripples on the water onto its blue hull. It features a white A-frame or davit on the stern, metal safety railings, a white radome on the cabin roof, and a mast flying both the U.S. flag and the blue state flag of Virginia. Behind it on the right stands the two-story NOAA AHB building with large windows and a light-blue trim roof.
- **Center Left (*Launch Explorer*)**: Just ahead of the blue launch is a white-hulled workboat/survey vessel surrounded by a floating yellow containment boom in the water. It has a yellow A-frame gantry mounted on its stern, and black lettering on its transom partially reads `LA... EXPLO... Norfolk, VA`.
- **Middle Left (*R/V Bay Hydro II* & White Launch)**: Ahead of the *Explorer* (second from the left among the smaller vessels) is the unpainted aluminum catamaran hull and superstructure of the *NOAA Bay Hydro II*, moored stern-to alongside a smaller white motor launch on the far left.
- **Background Center (*NOAA Ship Thomas Jefferson*)**: Rising behind the smaller launches is the large white superstructure, bridge wing, white radome, tall yellow radar mast, and white-and-blue funnel stack of a major NOAA hydrographic ship (*NOAA Ship Thomas Jefferson*, S 222). Cars are parked along the pier between the vessels.
- **Background Left**: Across the harbor basin stands a multi-story waterfront condominium or office building with a distinctive series of peaked roof gables and blue-tinted facade panels under a clear blue sky.
