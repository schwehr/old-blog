# School of Migrating Golden Cownose Rays by Sandra Critelli (`rays-sandra-critelli.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken by amateur photographer Sandra Critelli in the Gulf of Mexico showing a dense, multi-layered school of golden cownose rays (*Rhinoptera bonasus*) swimming near the ocean surface during their seasonal migration.

## Scene Description
- **Marine Life**: Dozens of diamond-shaped cownose rays fill the entire frame from edge to edge. Each ray has broad, pointed pectoral fins ("wings"), a squared-off indented snout with two distinct cephalic lobes, and a long, slender whip-like tail trailing behind.
- **Colors & Depth Layers**: Rays swimming closest to the surface are illuminated in bright golden-yellow, chartreuse, and warm tan hues, while rays deeper in the water column appear in muted olive-green and shadowy teal tones, creating a sense of three-dimensional depth and density.
- **Direction & Movement**: Almost all of the rays are oriented toward the upper-left and top of the frame, gliding in a coordinated formation with their wingtips gently curved upward or downward.
- **Water & Lighting**: The surrounding water is a deep emerald and dark teal blue-green, with soft diagonal ripples and light rays refracting across the surface in the upper left and upper right corners.
