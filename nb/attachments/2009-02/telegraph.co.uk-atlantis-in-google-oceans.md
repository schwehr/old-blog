# Telegraph Article on "Atlantis" Grid Artifact in Google Ocean (`telegraph.co.uk-atlantis-in-google-oceans.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of a February 20, 2009 online news article by Matthew Moore in *The Daily Telegraph* (`telegraph.co.uk`), showing a Google Earth / Google Ocean bathymetric image of a rectangular crisscross grid pattern on the Atlantic seafloor off Northwest Africa that media reports sensationalized as the possible ruins of "Atlantis" (which was actually a sonar ship-track artifact on the Madeira Abyssal Plain).

## Visual Elements & Article Text
- **Article Header & Gallery Controls**:
  - Top-left byline: `By Matthew Moore`
  - Timestamp: `Last Updated: 9:49AM GMT 20 Feb 2009`
  - Gallery navigation bar: `« Previous` | `3 of 5 Images` | `Next »`
- **Google Ocean Image**:
  - Shows a deep-blue shaded-relief bathymetric rendering of the Atlantic Ocean floor.
  - To the left and upper left, lighter blue submarine seamounts and ridges rise from the abyssal plain.
  - Near the center of the image is a distinct, rectilinear grid of crisscrossing vertical and horizontal lines enclosed within a sharp rectangular outline—an artifact created where higher-resolution shipboard sonar survey lines were merged onto a lower-resolution satellite-altimetry bathymetric background.
  - In the top-right corner of the map view are the Google Earth navigation controls (a circular directional look/move joystick and a vertical `+` / `-` zoom slider).
- **Caption & Body Text**:
  - Image caption: `Google Ocean, an extension of Google Earth, allows web users to virtually explore the ocean` followed by credit `Photo: GOOGLE EARTH`.
  - First paragraph: *"The network of criss-cross lines is 620 miles off the coast of north west Africa near the Canary Islands on the floor of the Atlantic Ocean."*
  - Second paragraph: *"The perfect rectangle – which is around the size of Wales – was noticed on the search giant's underwater exploration tool by an aeronautical engineer who claims it looks like an "aerial map" of a city."*
