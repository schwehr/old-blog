# Aerial View of Virginia Coastal Inlet and Jetties (`virginia-coastal-processes-1.jpg`)

## Overview
An oblique aerial photograph taken from a commercial regional aircraft at approximately 14,000 feet during a flight to Norfolk, Virginia on February 25, 2009, showing a coastal inlet, dredged navigation channel, protective jetties, marinas, and shoreline sediment transport features.

## Geographic & Coastal Features
- **Inlet & Jetties**:
  - In the lower-center of the frame, two long, straight, converging rock jetties or breakwaters extend outward from the mouth of a coastal river/estuary into the open bay/ocean water.
  - Between the jetties, a darker green plume marks the deeper dredged navigation channel extending offshore into the open water.
- **Shoreline & Sediment Transport**:
  - Parallel submerged sandbars wait offshore along the left and right beaches, visible as lighter yellowish-green bands beneath the shallow water surface.
  - Small groins/piers project perpendicular to the shoreline along both sides of the inlet.
- **Harbor & Land Development**:
  - Inside the inlet (upper center), the waterway branches into sheltered coves lined with numerous white boat slips and marinas packed with small vessels.
  - Residential neighborhoods, roads, agricultural fields, and patches of bare winter woodland surround the harbor in warm tan and brown winter tones.
  - Slight softness and warm color cast across the frame result from shooting through the aircraft window aft of the turboprop engine exhaust.
