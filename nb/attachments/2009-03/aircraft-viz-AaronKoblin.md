# 24 Hours of U.S. Air Traffic Visualization by Aaron Koblin (`aircraft-viz-AaronKoblin.png`)

## Overview
A data visualization image created by Aaron Koblin (in collaboration with FlightView and *Wired* Magazine) depicting 24 hours of commercial aircraft flight tracks across the continental United States. Without any underlying map boundaries or coastlines drawn, the dense web of flight paths naturally outlines the geography of the United States and its major metropolitan aviation hubs.

## Visual Details
- **Color Palette & Background**: Rendered against a solid black background, individual flight trajectories are drawn as fine, semi-transparent royal blue and indigo lines. Where many flight paths converge or overlap along heavily traveled air corridors and terminal areas, the lines brighten through pale blue to glowing white nodes.
- **Geographic Structure**:
  - **West Coast**: A strong vertical corridor runs along the Pacific coast connecting bright white hub nodes at Seattle/Tacoma in the northwest, the San Francisco Bay Area in northern California, and the Los Angeles/San Diego basin in southern California.
  - **Mountain West & Southwest**: Prominent star-like hub nodes radiate flight tracks in all directions from Phoenix, Las Vegas, Salt Lake City, and Denver (a particularly bright central-western starburst).
  - **Midwest & South**: Intense white focal points mark major hubs at Dallas/Fort Worth, Houston, Minneapolis/St. Paul, Chicago, Detroit, and St. Louis.
  - **East Coast & Southeast**: The eastern third of the country is densely braided with overlapping blue and white corridors linking Atlanta, Charlotte, Washington D.C., Philadelphia, the New York City metropolitan area, and Boston, along with a long, bright peninsula of north-south traffic extending down to Orlando and Miami/South Florida.
  - **International & Transoceanic Tracks**: Faint blue tracks extend northward into Canada, southward toward Mexico, the Gulf of Mexico, and the Caribbean, and eastward over the Atlantic Ocean.
