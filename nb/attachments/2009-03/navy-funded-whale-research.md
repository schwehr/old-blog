# Navy-Funded Whale Research and D-TAG Excerpt (`navy-funded-whale-research.png`)

## Overview
An excerpt from a U.S. Navy *Currents* magazine article (`Win09_Marine_Mammal_Science.pdf`, Winter 2009) illustrating Navy-funded marine mammal acoustic research and showing a close-up photograph of a suction-cup **D-TAG** (digital acoustic recording tag) attached to the back of a dense-beaked whale.

## Scene & Photograph Description
- **Subject**: The lower half of the image shows the mottled dark brown and tan skin of a surfacing male dense-beaked whale (*Mesoplodon densirostris*) at the water surface, scarred with pale linear and oval markings.
- **D-TAG Device**: Mounted near the top of the whale's back via translucent suction cups is a white, hydrodynamically faired plastic housing (D-TAG) with four small black cylindrical sensors/float caps protruding from its top corners, red/blue markings on the front fairing, printed serial/identification lettering on the body, and a thin whip antenna trailing toward the left.
- **Background**: Calm, Rippled blue-gray ocean water fades into white at the top to blend with the article layout.

## Visible Text & Captions
- **Body Text (Top Left & Top Right)**:
  - Left column: *"In 1996, after a stranding in Greece was linked to a sonar exercise, Tyack began on the path toward studying elusive beaked whales. Woods Hole, with the Navy's support, studied deep-diving sperm whales and, later, Cuvier's beaked whales, using a newer generation of the D-TAG. They collected the first known evidence that toothed whales use echolocation while they dive by"*
  - Top right line: *"range stocked with hydrophones—underwater microphones."*
- **Pull Quote (Center Right)**:
  - Styled in green and dark blue display type:
    > **THROUGH NAVY funding,**  
    > **scientists have done groundbreaking work in studying echolocation in whales, the effects of deep diving on some animals and furthered the inventory and accessibility of recorded whale sound libraries.**
- **Photo Caption (Bottom Left)**:
  - `A D-TAG attached to the back of a male dense-beaked whale.`
