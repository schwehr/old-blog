# Carnivorous Sundew Plants at the UNH Greenhouse (`unh-greenhouse-carnivorous.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up macro photograph of carnivorous sundew plants (*Drosera*) growing in pots on a greenhouse bench at the University of New Hampshire (UNH) Macfarlane Research Greenhouses open house, published in a March 29, 2009 blog post titled *"UNH Greenhouse"*.

## Scene Description
- **Plants**:
  - Several rosette clusters of narrow-leaved sundews (*Drosera*, likely *Drosera capensis* / Cape sundew) fill the frame.
  - Long, slender bright green stems radiate from central rosettes, terminating in spatula-shaped leaf blades covered in dense, reddish-pink glandular tentacles (trichomes).
  - Each tiny hair is tipped with a glistening, clear drop of sticky mucilage used to trap and digest insects.
  - Dried, brown older leaves and stems are visible around the bases of the living green rosettes.
- **Greenhouse Details**:
  - The plants sit in terracotta/orange plastic pots on a black open-grid plastic greenhouse bench top (visible at the top left and top right corners).
  - Two white plastic plant identification stakes are partially visible tucked among the foliage—one near the center and another on the right edge bearing handwritten black marker text reading `Drosera...`.
