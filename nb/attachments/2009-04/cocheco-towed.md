# R/V Cocheco Under Tow During Spring Training Drills (`cocheco-towed.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up photograph taken from the stern of the *R/V Coastal Surveyor* showing the UNH research vessel *R/V Cocheco* being towed astern by a taut towline during spring seamanship and towing exercises on the Piscataqua River.

## Scene Description
- **Towed Vessel (*R/V Cocheco*)**:
  - Framed in the center, the blue-hulled, white-cabined *R/V Cocheco* (`R 3401` stenciled in white near the port bow below the black rub rail) is pulled forward toward the viewer and slightly to the left, pushing up a white frothy bow wake.
  - A taut beige/white towline extends from the left edge of the frame to the bow of the *Cocheco*.
  - The vessel features a black metal bow pulpit/railing, an orange life ring resting on the forward cabin trunk below the three-pane windshield, a circular NOAA emblem on the port cabin side, a white liferaft canister and radar dome mounted on the roof rack, two tall white whip antennas, and a black stern frame. A crew member is dimly visible inside the helm cabin.
- **Background Shoreline**:
  - Across the dark blue river water rises a rocky coastal bank topped by evergreens, leafless early-spring trees, a stone fortification wall and white blockhouse structure on a grassy hill on the left (Fort McClary in Kittery Point, Maine), and two light-colored houses with a red channel range marker on the right.
