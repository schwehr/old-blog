# Homemade Foam-Core Magnetic Susceptibility Core Logger (`el-cheapo-core-logger.jpg`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken in February 2002 showing Warren Smith standing in a storage/laboratory room inspecting an improvised sediment core logger built from white foam-core board, a Bartington magnetic susceptibility sensor, a control unit, and a laptop computer on a worktable.

## Scene Description
- **Equipment on the Table (Foreground & Center):**
  - **Foam-Core Sensor Mount (Bottom Right):** An upright rectangular bridge/arch cut from white foam-core board, supported by triangular foam-core gussets and black zip-ties at its base. A white Bartington loop/point sensor is taped to the center of the arch with black electrical tape so sediment cores can pass underneath it.
  - **Measuring Track (Center Right):** A long wooden meter stick / ruler is secured flat along the gray tabletop behind the foam-core arch, alongside a sheet of white paper with two black markers resting on it.
  - **Laptop & Notebook (Center Left):** An open dark gray laptop computer sits on the left side of the table with black cables coiling across the tabletop connecting the sensor to the electronics. In front of the laptop lies a yellow Rite in the Rain / laboratory field notebook with handwritten black text on the cover.
  - **Bartington MS2 Meter (Middle Left):** Behind the laptop, a beige rectangular electronic instrument box (Bartington magnetic susceptibility meter) is propped up on its white folding stand/handle with cables attached to its front panel.
- **Person (Left):**
  - Warren Smith stands at the far end of the table looking down at the laptop and core logger setup with his hands in his pockets. He wears glasses, a mustache, a light blue patterned cap, a rust-red long-sleeve button-up shirt over a black undershirt, gray pants, and gray athletic shoes.
- **Background (Storage Room):**
  - Behind Warren is a wooden door and a tall wooden stepladder leaning against the wall.
  - Along the right wall, metal industrial shelving units are packed with brown cardboard core boxes and red-and-white `"XEROX"` paper boxes (`"Xerox 4200DP Paper"`), some with handwritten inventory notes.
  - A green metal office chair and orange extension cords are visible on the floor at the far left.
