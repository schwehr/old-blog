# CSUMB Combined Shipboard LIDAR and Multibeam Bathymetry of the Golden Gate Bridge (`csumb-lidar-and-bathy.jpg`)

## Overview
A 3D perspective visualization produced by the Seafloor Mapping Lab at California State University, Monterey Bay (CSUMB), combining ship-based terrestrial laser scanning (LIDAR) of the Golden Gate Bridge and coastal headlands with high-resolution multibeam sonar bathymetry of the seafloor beneath the Golden Gate strait.

## Visualization Details
- **Seafloor Bathymetry (Foreground & Center)**:
  - The underwater terrain is rendered as a dense, shaded 3D surface and point cloud colored by depth against a black background.
  - **Color Ramp**: Shallow nearshore areas along the edges are colored red, orange, and bright yellow; intermediate depths transition through vivid green and cyan; and the deepest scoured channel passing toward the bridge in the center is colored deep blue.
  - **Seafloor Features**: Prominent fields of large sand waves and bedforms with rippled crests extend across the brown/tan/olive-green seabed in the lower left and center foreground, bordering the deeper blue channel trough on the right.
- **Terrestrial LIDAR Point Cloud (Background & Left)**:
  - Rendered in grayscale (white and light gray points against the black sky).
  - Spanning the strait in the upper left-center is the unmistakable silhouette of the **Golden Gate Bridge**, showing its two main towers, suspension cables, vertical suspender ropes, and roadway deck.
  - Steep rocky coastal cliffs and headlands (the Marin Headlands on the left and the San Francisco shoreline on the right, plus a rocky outcrop in the lower-left foreground) are depicted in shaded gray relief along the water's edge.
