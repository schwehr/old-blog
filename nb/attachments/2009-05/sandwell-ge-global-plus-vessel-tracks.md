# Google Earth Global Bathymetry and Ship Sounding Tracks off the US East Coast (`sandwell-ge-global-plus-vessel-tracks.png`)

## Overview
A Google Earth visualization created from David Sandwell's global 1-minute topography dataset (`global_topo_1min_V12.1.kmz`), showing shaded-relief bathymetry and overlaid grey ship sounding tracks along the U.S. East Coast continental margin and western North Atlantic basin.

## Map & Bathymetric Details
- **Geography & Depth Coloring**:
  - **Left / Northwest**: The Mid-Atlantic U.S. coastline (including Chesapeake Bay and Delaware Bay) and the broad, shallow continental shelf rendered in pinkish-tan tones, densely covered by tightly spaced nearshore hydrographic survey lines.
  - **Center Diagonal**: The steep continental slope drops off sharply along a dark reddish-brown band marked by submarine canyons and a black contour line, transitioning into the continental rise in shaded yellow-green and pale green hues (representing comprehensive multibeam coverage such as the UNH Law of the Sea cruises).
  - **Right / Southeast**: The deeper abyssal plain of the western North Atlantic is colored in light cyan and pale blue, with several prominent seamounts (part of the New England Seamount chain) rising in green, yellow, and brown relief in the upper right.
- **Vessel Sounding Tracks**:
  - Across the deeper cyan/blue basin where full-coverage swath mapping is absent, hundreds of thin grey dotted linescrisscross the ocean floor in straight and intersecting transit paths, indicating individual research vessel depth-sounding tracks used to constrain the satellite-altimetry gravity bathymetry model.
