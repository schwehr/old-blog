# Surveying NOS Benchmark on Appledore Island, Isles of Shoals (`appledore-island-reference-point.jpg`)

## Overview

A field photograph by Andy McLeod showing researcher Christina tending a high-precision geodetic GPS reference station set up over a 1976 National Ocean Service (NOS) benchmark (`97514 - 1976`) on the rocky shoreline of **Appledore Island** (Isles of Shoals, Maine/New Hampshire) during the UNH Summer Hydrographic Field Course in June 2009.

## Scene Description

- **Survey Tripod & GPS Antenna (Center)**:
  - Positioned on bare, weathered light-gray granitic bedrock ledges is a tall aluminum survey tripod supporting a vertical red-and-white striped range pole centered directly over the embedded brass survey benchmark disk.
  - Mounted at the top of the pole is a white saucer-shaped geodetic GPS antenna.
- **Power & Data Logging Equipment (Bottom Center & Left)**:
  - A black coaxial antenna cable runs down from the GPS antenna, coils across the rock surface, and connects to a gray rectangular GPS receiver/logger unit resting upright on the rock at bottom center.
  - To the left of the receiver is a white canvas tote bag and a compact red **Honda EU2000i** portable inverter generator (`HONDA EU2000i`).
  - To the right of the receiver stands an upright black ruggedized equipment case/battery pack.
- **Person (Bottom Right)**:
  - Christina sits on a red ground cloth on the rocky ledge at the lower right, wearing dark sunglasses, a light beige jacket, and blue jeans while eating a wrapped sandwich or snack and monitoring the station.
- **Background**:
  - Calm blue-gray ocean water separates Appledore Island from **Star Island** across the channel in the upper right background, where the stone tower of the **Star Island / Gosport Chapel** and the large **Oceanic Hotel** building are clearly visible along the low island skyline under an overcast sky.
