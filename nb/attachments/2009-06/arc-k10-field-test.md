# NASA Lunar Science Institute "Robotic Recon" Webpage (`arc-k10-field-test.png`)

## Overview

A screenshot of the **NASA Lunar Science Institute** **"Robotic Recon"** project website (`http://lunarscience.nasa.gov/roboticrecon/`) announcing the June 14–27, 2009 field test of the **K10** planetary rover at Black Point Lava Flow in northern Arizona.

## Banner & Graphics (Top)

- A wide horizontal composite banner transitions from left to right across three visual elements:
  1. **Left**: A blue-toned photograph of the four-wheeled **NASA K10** rover standing on rocky desert terrain with its tall instrument mast (carrying cameras and a 3D lidar scanner) and a second rover faintly visible in the background.
  2. **Center**: A geological/traverse map overlay drawn in black linework showing a scarp line (`LINCOLN SCARP`), numbered Lunar Roving Vehicle waypoints (`LRV 1`, `LRV 2`, `LRV 3`, `LRV 5`, `LRV 6`, `LRV 7`, `LRV 8`), and named lunar/analog craters and features (`TORTILLA FLAT`, `HORATIO`, `CAMELOT`, `EVA 2`, `POWELL`, `STENO`, `SHERLOCK`, `VAN SERG`, `SHAKESPEARE`, `COCHISE`).
  3. **Right**: Against a dark starry space background with a bright four-pointed star flare in the upper-right corner, large glowing white futuristic sans-serif capital letters read:
     ```text
     ROBOTIC
     RECON
     ```

- **Main Content Area (`Overview`)**:
  - **Heading**: Bold white text `Overview` in a dark gray header bar.
  - **Thumbnail Image**: A small bordered color photograph on the left showing an astronaut in a white pressurized spacesuit standing next to NASA's **Lunar Electric Rover (LER)** in a desert field setting.
  - **Body Text** (white sans-serif text on a dark gray background):
    > From June 14-27, 2009, NASA will use a robot to scout portions of the Black Point Lava Flow in northern Arizona. The K10 robot will be remotely operated from the NASA Lunar Science Institute located at the NASA Ames Research Center (Moffett Field, California). The data from "robotic recon" will be used to plan field work, which will be subsequently carried out by astronauts driving in the "Lunar Electric Rover."
