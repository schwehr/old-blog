# First Two 900 kHz Sidescan Sonar Survey Lines in Mendum's Pond (`mendums-mst-sidescan-900kHz-lines.png`)

## Overview
A Google Earth screenshot displaying the first two georeferenced 900 kHz sidescan sonar survey lines (approximately 60-meter swath width) collected by the Gavia AUV using a Marine Sonic Technology (MST) 900/1800 kHz sonar in Mendum's Pond, New Hampshire.

## Sonar & Map Details
- **Survey Track Configuration**: A continuous golden-brown and amber sidescan sonar mosaic overlays the dark water of Mendum's Pond, showing two nearly parallel east–west survey lines connected by a tight 180-degree turn at the left (western) end:
  - **Upper (Northern) Line**: Extends from the upper-right nearshore area toward the left, where the vehicle executes a smooth southward turn (producing a fan-shaped distortion and a dark band near the end of the turn). Bright yellow high-backscatter patches—representing rocky shoals, boulders, and shoreline slope returns with dark acoustic shadows—stand out along the northern edge of the upper swath.
  - **Lower (Southern) Line**: Runs from the turn at the lower left back toward the eastern shoreline at the lower right. Clusters of bright yellow boulder targets and dark shadows are visible near the lower-left and lower-right edges, and a vertical seam/linear feature crosses the swath near its right end where the vehicle approaches the bank.
- **Shoreline & Context**: On the right (eastern) side of the image, aerial photography shows green lakeside forest and a white L-shaped pier/dock jutting into the water between the two sonar lines, all outlined by a thin white digitized shoreline polygon.
