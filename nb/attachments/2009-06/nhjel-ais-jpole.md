# Outdoor AIS J-Pole Antenna at Jackson Estuarine Laboratory (`nhjel-ais-jpole.jpg`)

## Overview
An elevated outdoor photograph taken by Andy M. in June 2009 showing the newly relocated copper J-Pole AIS VHF receiving antenna mounted on the roof of the UNH Jackson Estuarine Laboratory (`nhjel`), overlooking Great Bay / Little Bay in New Hampshire.

## Antenna & Foreground Details
- **AIS J-Pole Antenna (Right Center):** Mounted atop a vertical light-blue/teal metal mast pipe rising along the edge of the gravel/shingle laboratory roof is a vertical copper-pipe J-Pole antenna:
  - The longer vertical radiating element extends high into the upper frame against the overcast sky, while the shorter parallel quarter-wave matching stub sits immediately to its right, joined at the bottom by a U-shaped copper elbow.
  - A black coaxial feed cable attaches near the bottom of the J-stub and drapes down alongside the blue mounting pole toward the roof.

## Background & Facility View
- **Laboratory Grounds & Boat Ramp (Lower Left):** Looking down from the roof toward the waterfront:
  - Several rectangular wooden outdoor experimental seawater tanks/mesocosms and white storage chests sit immediately below the building.
  - A paved driveway/ramp leads down to a concrete pier equipped with a gray davit crane, white shrink-wrapped equipment/material bundles, stacked yellow lobster traps/crates, and a parked dark blue pickup truck with a utility rack.
- **Estuary & Far Shore:** Calm gray-blue estuarine water stretches across the middle of the frame, with a rectangular floating aquaculture/research raft moored just offshore and a couple of small boats moored further out. A continuous green tree-lined shore spans the horizon beneath a gray, cloud-covered sky.
