# UNH VisLab 2006 High-Resolution Stereoscope Setup (`old-stereoscope.jpg`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken in 2006 showing the original high-resolution dual-monitor Wheatstone-style stereoscope system in the Visualization Lab (VisLab) at the University of New Hampshire's Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping (CCOM). Featured in a June 23, 2009 blog post (*"The old VisLab stereoscope"*), the system uses two ultra-high-resolution monitors mounted facing each other at angles, viewed through central angled mirrors, alongside a cluster of four rack-mounted Linux computers.

## Scene & Hardware Description
- **Stereoscope Display Rig**:
  - **Left and Right High-Resolution Monitors**: Positioned on the far left and far right ends of a horizontal extruded aluminum T-slot framing rail (80/20 style) are two large black flat-panel LCD monitors—identified in the blog post as ViewSonic VP2290b (IBM T221) displays capable of 3840×2400 resolution (204 dpi at 41 Hz). They are mounted on vertical aluminum posts and tilted slightly downward toward the center of the rig.
  - **Central Viewing Mirrors**: Mounted on a vertical metal post near the center of the aluminum rail is a black fixture holding two small rectangular front-surface mirrors angled outward in a V-shape (45 degrees) toward the left and right ViewSonic monitors, allowing a user to view left-eye and right-eye images simultaneously for stereoscopic 3D visualization.
- **Control & Cluster Hardware**:
  - **CRT Monitors & Speakers**: Sitting side-by-side on the left half of the aluminum beam between the left LCD panel and the central mirror post are two dark gray ViewSonic CRT monitors. The left CRT shows a dark screen with a white login/dialog box in the center and a light bar at the bottom; the right CRT displays a bright white desktop background featuring a large cyan-blue 3D gear logo (KDE desktop). A small silver-and-black cube speaker sits on top of each CRT monitor.
  - **Four-Node Linux Cluster**: To the right of the central mirror post, stacked two-by-two, are four black rack-mount server/workstation chassis ("gang of 4 linux boxes") with slotted front ventilation grilles, status LEDs, and silver key locks. Four computer mice rest on top of the upper server cases.
  - **Peripherals & Furniture**: In the lower-left foreground, a black keyboard and a black mouse with a red optical glow sit on an adjustable tray extending below the aluminum rail. The rounded gray backrest of an office chair is visible in the lower-right foreground.
- **Lighting & Background**:
  - The room is illuminated by warm, yellowish-orange indoor lighting against a plain light-colored wall with a square recessed window or panel directly behind the central stereoscope mirrors.
