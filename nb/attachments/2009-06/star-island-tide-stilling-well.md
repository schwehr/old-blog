# Star Island Tide Station Stilling Well and Tide Staff (`star-island-tide-stilling-well.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up outdoor photograph by Andy Armstrong ("Andy M.") showing the lower section of the tide gauge installation mounted against the vertical granite stone seawall/pier on Star Island (Isles of Shoals, New Hampshire). Featured in the June 16, 2009 blog post (*"tide station at Star Island"*), the image captures the graduated tide staff (ruler) and adjacent white PVC pipe tubes emerging from the water.

## Scene Description
- **Tide Staff & Stilling Well Assembly (Center)**:
  - **Wooden Support Beam & Graduated Tide Staff**: A vertical dark-brown pressure-treated wooden post is mounted flush against the rough-cut granite blocks of the pier wall. Attached to the front face of the post is a white fiberglass/metal surveyor-style graduated staff marked in meters and decimeters/centimeters. Large black stencil numbers `2` (just above the water surface) and `3` (near the top edge of the frame) mark whole-meter elevations, with smaller two-digit decimeter markings and black-and-white centimeter graduations in between.
  - **PVC Pipes & Cabling**: Fastened to the sides of the wooden post with silver metal strapping/clamps are two vertical white PVC pipes of different heights: a shorter tube on the left that extends down into the water next to the `2` meter mark (with black sensor cables looping into its open top) and a taller tube on the right starting above the `2.6` meter level. Black sensor cables loop between the pipes and the granite wall.
  - **Water Line**: The calm, dark gray-blue harbor water with gentle surface ripples meets the tide staff at approximately the `1.82` mark, casting a dark vertical reflection of the post and white staff on the water's surface below.
- **Granite Pier Wall (Right)**:
  - The right half of the frame is filled with large, weathered, rough-hewn gray granite blocks forming the Star Island pier, with barnacles and marine growth visible on the lower stones near the waterline.
