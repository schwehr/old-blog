# Towing GPS-Acoustic Buoys Like Ducklings on Mendum's Pond (`weber-gps-acoustic-buoy-ducklings.jpg`)

## Overview
An outdoor photograph taken on June 2, 2009, at Mendum's Pond in New Hampshire, showing two upright GPS-acoustic spar buoys being towed in single file behind a small motorized skiff—humorously captioned "Tom's ducklings" in the blog post.

## Scene & Equipment Description
- **Towed GPS-Acoustic Buoys (Left & Center-Left)**:
  - Two identical yellow-and-silver spar buoys float vertically in the dark blue pond water, connected in series by a white tow line extending from the stern of the skiff.
  - Each buoy has a bright yellow cylindrical foam float at the waterline, a short silver aluminum upper housing with cabling, a tall white vertical mast spirally wrapped with black cable and topped with a GPS antenna, and a shorter vertical white whip antenna on the right side of the frame.
  - A small white oblong float trails in the water just ahead of the middle buoy along the tow line.
- **Skiff & Operator (Center-Right)**:
  - John sits near the stern of a small, unpainted aluminum utility boat (jon boat/skiff), wearing an orange life vest over a dark long-sleeved shirt, steering an electric trolling motor or small outboard mounted on the transom. An extra orange life vest rests inside the middle of the boat.
- **Background**: Across the rippled blue water, a dense wall of sunlit green pine and deciduous trees lines the shore under a clear blue sky.
