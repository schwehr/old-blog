# National Geographic Whale Tagging at Sea (`national-geographic-whale-tagging-1.png`)

## Overview
A video still frame from a June 18, 2009 National Geographic News segment titled *"Whales Tagged to Prevent Collisions"*, highlighting CCOM/UNH and NOAA researchers hand-tagging humpback whales off the coast of Massachusetts to study their behavior and prevent ship strikes.

## Scene Description
- **Researcher & Tagging Pole**: In the right foreground, a researcher seen from behind kneels or leans forward at the bow of a small rigid-hulled inflatable boat (RIB). The researcher wears a white t-shirt, dark shorts, a khaki baseball cap, and a dark harness/life-vest strap across the back, holding a long carbon-fiber or fiberglass pole with an orange band near the base that extends diagonally up and to the left over the water. At the tip of the pole is a suction-cup acoustic/movement tag (DTag) assembly.
- **Humpback Whale**: In the left-center midground, the dark black/charcoal back and dorsal fin of a humpback whale break the surface of the ocean, surrounded by white foam and wake as the animal surfaces just ahead of the boat.
- **Ocean & Sky**: Calm, slightly rippled gray-blue sea stretches to a flat, hazy horizon under a bright, overcast white sky.
