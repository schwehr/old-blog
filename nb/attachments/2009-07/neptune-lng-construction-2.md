# Close-Up of Subsea Turret Loading Buoy on Normand Clipper (`neptune-lng-construction-2.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up photograph (credited to the U.S. Coast Guard) of a massive yellow Subsea Turret Loading (STL) buoy mounted on the deck of the offshore construction vessel *Normand Clipper* during the Neptune LNG Deepwater Port installation in July 2009, with a crew member standing on top for scale.

## Scene & Equipment Details
- **Subsea Turret Loading (STL) Buoy**:
  - A tall, yellow-painted steel structure with a cylindrical lower section and a tapered conical upper section lined with vertical black protective fender strips and a metal access ladder running up the left side.
  - **Markings & Stencils**: The lower cylindrical body is stenciled in black with `APL` and a black triangle/arrow logo, above `STL TECHNOLOGY / NORTH / 109 - FS - 100`. Smaller working load limit stencils read `WLL 200 T` and `WLL 75 T` near the top rim, and `WLL 85 T` and `WLL 12 T` around the lower padeyes.
  - **Base & Mounting Frame**: The buoy rests on heavy yellow-and-dark-brown steel sea-fastening beams and hydraulic/mechanical latching mechanisms above the ship's red-orange gunwale.
- **Worker for Scale**: A crew member wearing bright orange coveralls, a safety harness, and a white hard hat stands at the very top of the buoy cone, holding onto the top lifting padeyes, emphasizing the multi-story height of the submersible buoy.
- **Surrounding Ship Structures**:
  - **Left**: The massive cylindrical yellow pedestal of the ship's main offshore crane with circular walkways, railings, and the white crane operator's cab visible behind it.
  - **Center Background**: Large blue cable/mooring reels on the working deck.
  - **Right**: Part of the aft yellow gantry/crane structure.
