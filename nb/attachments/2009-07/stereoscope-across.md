# Looking Across the Dual-Monitor Mirror Stereoscope (`stereoscope-across.jpg`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken in the visualization lab at CCOM/JHC (University of New Hampshire), showing the side/oblique view of a custom Wheatstone-style dual-monitor mirror stereoscope displaying ship-based lidar point cloud data of Norfolk, Virginia in GeoZui.

## Scene & Equipment Details
- **Stereoscope Assembly (Foreground)**:
  - A black-bezel **ViewSonic** flat-panel LCD monitor is mounted at a tilted angle on an extruded aluminum T-slot framing post near the center of the image.
  - The monitor screen displays a grayscale/white-on-black 3D perspective rendering of waterfront buildings, piers, and vessels from a Norfolk, VA ship-based lidar dataset.
  - To the left of the monitor, two angled front-surface mirrors are mounted in a V-arrangement on a black bracket, positioned so a viewer looking into the mirrors sees the left and right monitors fused into a single stereoscopic 3D image.
- **Lab Background**:
  - Mounted high on the wall behind the stereoscope is a large rear-projection or flat-panel display showing 3D bathymetric/hydrographic visualizations in blue and red tones.
  - Further down the room under ceiling track lights, computer workstations, a dark CRT/hooded display enclosure on an aluminum frame, and office chairs are visible.
