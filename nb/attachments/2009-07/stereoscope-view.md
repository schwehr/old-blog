# Split-Mirror View Through the CCOM Stereoscope (`stereoscope-view.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up photograph looking directly into the two angled front-surface mirrors of the CCOM Visualization Lab's Wheatstone stereoscope, showing the left-eye and right-eye reflected views of a ship-based lidar point cloud of Norfolk, Virginia in GeoZui.

## Visual & Stereoscopic Details
- **Left and Right Mirror Reflections**:
  - The two rectangular mirrors sit side-by-side in the foreground (slightly out of focus along their edges and central mounting bracket), each reflecting one of the lateral LCD monitors.
  - Both mirrors show crisp white-on-black 3D point cloud renderings of waterfront buildings with pitched roofs, multi-story background buildings, vertical pilings/piers along the water's edge, and masts/flagpoles.
  - Because the camera lens is wider than a single human interpupillary position or positioned slightly off-center, the left and right mirrors capture slightly different horizontal portions/angles of the waterfront scene, which a human viewer's eyes fuse into a 3D stereoscopic image when looking closely into the mirror apex.
