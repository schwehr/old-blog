# 3D Fledermaus Visualization of Northern California Mid-Water Gas Plume (`GardnerPlume-Fledermaus.png`)

## Overview
A 3D perspective screenshot from **IVS 3D Fledermaus 7** (incorporating mid-water visualization technology originally developed by Roland Arsenault in GeoZui4D at CCOM/JHC), illustrating the 1,400-meter-high northern California gas plume in three dimensions above the multibeam bathymetric surface collected by the **NOAA Ship Okeanos Explorer**.

## 3D Scene & Visualization Details
- **Bathymetric Surface:** The seafloor across the lower half of the frame is rendered as a smooth, shaded 3D surface colored by depth using a rainbow palette: deep purple and dark blue on the far left (deepest), transitioning through cyan and green in the central depression to yellow and orange-yellow on the shallower slope to the right.
- **3D Point-Cloud Gas Plume (Center):**
  - Rising vertically from the cyan/green depression in the middle of the slope is a tall, columnar 3D point cloud of water-column acoustic returns representing the gas plume extending into the black background space above the seafloor.
  - The plume points are colored in shades of blue, cyan, and green. A white mouse cursor arrow points just to the left of the lower section of the plume column.
- **Vertical Water-Column Curtain / Cross-Section Fan (Right of Plume):**
  - Immediately to the right of the 3D point-cloud plume stands a tall, vertically oriented planar slice (a single multibeam ping fan cross-section) outlined in thin red/orange lines.
  - The slice displays the full water-column backscatter field in blue, cyan, and dark purple/indigo, with bright green, yellow, and red backscatter along its bottom edge where the acoustic fan intersects the seafloor.
