# AprizeSat S-AIS Multi-Report Spiderweb Fan-Out in Google Earth (`aprizesat-south-africa-2min-many-ships.png`)

## Overview
A close-up **Google Earth** screenshot over Southern Africa demonstrating the high density of repeated Space-Based AIS (S-AIS) position reports received from a single vessel during a two-minute **SpaceQuest AprizeSat** satellite pass on July 29, 2009. Clicking on a densely stacked vessel cluster causes Google Earth to fan out the overlapping placemarks into a large radial "spiderweb" pattern.

## Visual Details & Placemark Fan-Out
- **Central Cluster & Radial Lines:**
  - Near the center-bottom of the frame (just off the southern coast of South Africa near Cape Agulhas / Cape of Good Hope), a bright white starburst hub marks the true geographic position of a selected vessel.
  - Radiating outward in all directions from this central hub are dozens of white leader lines terminating in expanded teardrop paddle icons arranged in concentric rings across the entire screen—stretching north across South Africa, east toward Madagascar, and south/west over the ocean.
  - Most of the expanded pins are **yellow with a black center dot** (standard AIS Class A position reports), interspersed with **light-green pins** (reports containing SOTDMA hour/minute synchronization data), illustrating how a single vessel's ~22 reports (or multiple co-located packet records in the 2-minute dataset) expand when selected in Google Earth.
- **Background Vessel Pins:**
  - Beneath the bright radial fan-out, hundreds of dimmed/shaded **dark olive-yellow** and **dark green** teardrop pins trace the shipping lanes around the South African coastline, into the South Atlantic (left) and Indian Ocean (right).
  - Several small dark red star icons mark cities or reference points onshore in South Africa.
- **Interface Controls (Top Right):**
  - The Google Earth pan control wheel (hand icon surrounded by four blue directional arrows) and the vertical zoom slider bar (`+` to `-`) are visible along the upper right edge.
