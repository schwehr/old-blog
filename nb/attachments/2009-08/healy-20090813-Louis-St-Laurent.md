# CCGS Louis S. St-Laurent Following USCGC Healy in Arctic Sea Ice (`healy-20090813-Louis-St-Laurent.jpg`)

## Overview
A shipboard photograph taken from the aft deck/flight deck area of the U.S. Coast Guard icebreaker **USCGC *Healy* (WAGB-20)** on August 13, 2009, looking astern across a freshly broken channel of Arctic sea ice toward the Canadian Coast Guard heavy icebreaker **CCGS *Louis S. St-Laurent*** during the joint 2009 U.S.–Canada Extended Continental Shelf Arctic survey (`HEALY-0905`).

## Scene Description
- **Vessel (*CCGS Louis S. St-Laurent*):**
  - Positioned in the upper-left quadrant, seen nearly bow-on as she follows in *Healy*'s wake while towing seismic profiling gear.
  - The vessel displays the distinctive Canadian Coast Guard livery: a bright red hull with a broad vertical white stripe painted on the port forward hull side, a multi-deck white superstructure with bridge windows across the top level, a yellow deck crane on the foredeck, and a tall white mast/funnel structure rising above the bridge.
- **Sea Ice & Broken Channel:**
  - Extending from the foreground back to the bow of the *Louis S. St-Laurent* is a wide swath of slushy, pulverized brash ice and gray open-water wake cleared by the *Healy* so the *Louis S. St-Laurent* can safely tow her seismic streamer array.
  - Flanking the cleared channel on the left, right, and lower-left foreground are flat white and pale blue-gray first-year and multi-year Arctic ice floes.
- **Foreground & Weather Conditions:**
  - In the lower-right foreground is a section of gray metal safety netting/fencing (flight deck edge perimeter net) mounted on tubular steel frames aboard the *Healy*.
  - The sky is a uniform pale, overcast Arctic gray with light fog or haze softening the horizon line behind the Canadian icebreaker.
