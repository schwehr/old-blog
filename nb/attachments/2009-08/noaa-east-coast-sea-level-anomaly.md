# NOAA East Coast Sea Level Anomaly and Florida Current Transport (`noaa-east-coast-sea-level-anomaly.png`)

## Overview
A two-part scientific figure from a NOAA technical report illustrating the inverse seasonal relationship between **Florida Current transport** (in Sverdrups) and coastal **sea level predictions** (in meters) along the U.S. Atlantic Coast, paired with a cross-sectional schematic diagram of sea surface slope across the continental shelf and Florida Current.

## Top Panel: Seasonal Cycle Line Graph
- **Legend (Top Box)**: Lists six data series in two rows:
  - **Florida Current** (solid black line)
  - **Nantucket, MA** (light green line)
  - **Atlantic City, NJ** (orange-yellow line)
  - **Duck, NC** (purple line)
  - **Charleston, SC** (red line)
  - **Trident Pier, FL** (light blue line)
- **Axes**:
  - **Horizontal Axis (Months)**: Labeled **`Jan`**, **`Feb`**, **`Mar`**, **`Apr`**, **`May`**, **`Jun`**, **`Jul`**, **`Aug`**, **`Sep`**, **`Oct`**, **`Nov`**, **`Dec`** with vertical grid lines at each month.
  - **Left Vertical Axis**: Labeled **`Florida Current (Sv)`**, ranging from **`30`** to **`34`** Sv in increments of `1` (with minor grid lines every `0.5` Sv).
  - **Right Vertical Axis**: Labeled **`Predictions (m)`**, ranging from **`-0.2`** to **`0.2`** m in increments of `0.1` m.
- **Trends Shown**:
  - The **Florida Current** (black curve) peaks in **`Jul`** at approximately **`33.4 Sv`** and drops to a minimum in **`Sep`**–**`Oct`** at approximately **`30.2 Sv`**.
  - Conversely, the five coastal tide gauge prediction curves (**Nantucket**, **Atlantic City**, **Duck**, **Charleston**, and **Trident Pier**) reach their seasonal peaks in **`Sep`**–**`Oct`** (up to **`+0.16 m`** at Trident Pier) when the Florida Current transport is weakest.

## Bottom Panel: Cross-Sectional Schematic Diagram
- **Bathymetric Profile**: A tan-shaded profile steps down from left to right through three labeled zones: **`Shore`** (left), **`Shelf`** (middle flat terrace), and **`Slope`** (steeply descending right slope), with **`Ocean`** labeled near the surface on the far right.
- **Florida Current Core**: A large blue circle over the continental slope contains white centered text:
  ```
  FLORIDA
     X
  CURRENT
  ```
  (where `X` indicates flow into the page / northward).
- **Sea Surface Profiles & Legend**: Three black/blue lines connect the offshore `Ocean` surface height across the `FLORIDA CURRENT` to the coastal `Shore`/`Shelf`, showing how geostrophic tilt changes coastal sea level:
  - **Low Transport** (light blue dotted line): Flatter slope across the current, resulting in **higher** sea level at the coast/shelf.
  - **Average Transport** (solid black line): Intermediate slope and intermediate coastal sea level.
  - **High Transport** (dark blue dash-dotted line): Steeper downward slope toward the coast across the current, resulting in **lower** sea level at the coast/shelf.
