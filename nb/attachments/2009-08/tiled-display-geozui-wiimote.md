# Controlling GeoZui on the CCOM Tiled Display Wall with a Nintendo Wii Remote (`tiled-display-geozui-wiimote.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken at a University of New Hampshire (UNH) GIS Day event showing software engineer **Roland Arsenault** using a **Nintendo Wii Remote (Wiimote)** to interactively navigate a 3D nautical chart and bathymetric visualization of Portsmouth, New Hampshire in **GeoZui** on the **CCOM/JHC** 4x3 multi-monitor tiled display wall.

## Scene Description
- **People (Left)**:
  - **Center-Left (Operator)**: Roland Arsenault stands facing the display wall, seen from the back/right profile, wearing a dark blue UNH CCOM/JHC T-shirt (with a white diamond logo on the back) and dark pants. He holds a white **Nintendo Wii Remote** raised in his right hand with its wrist strap and Nunchuk cord hanging down, pointing it toward the tiled display.
  - **Far Left (Observer)**: An older bearded man wearing a black knit beanie, a bright orange-and-blue jacket with reflective silver cuff stripes, and khaki pants stands beside an indoor potted plant, watching the tiled display.
- **CCOM/JHC Exhibit Booth (Center Background)**:
  - Behind the operator stands a curved black fabric trade-show display booth topped with a wide banner reading:
    - **`Center for Coastal & Ocean Mapping`**
    - **`Joint Hydrographic Center`**
    - *`A center for expertise in Ocean Mapping and Hydrographic Sciences`*
    - Accompanied by the circular NOAA/UNH CCOM emblem on the left and a strip of ocean mapping photographs below.
  - Three vertical scientific posters are mounted on the booth wall behind a folding table holding the back of a flat-panel LCD monitor, keyboard, and a black desktop PC tower on the carpeted floor beneath the table.
- **4x3 Tiled LCD Display Wall (Right)**:
  - A freestanding aluminum rolling rack holds **12 flat-panel LCD monitors arranged in a 4-column by 3-row grid**.
  - Across all 12 screens is a continuous, oblique 3D **GeoZui** visualization of the **Portsmouth, New Hampshire / Piscataqua River** area, combining a yellow-tan NOAA raster nautical chart (showing soundings like `15`, `17`, `6`, channel lines, and shoreline features) with bright blue high-resolution multibeam bathymetry in the river channel.
