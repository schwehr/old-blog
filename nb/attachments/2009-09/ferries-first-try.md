# Initial AIS Ferry Tracks Around Long Island and Southern New England in Google Earth (`ferries-first-try.png`)

## Overview

A Google Earth screenshot from a September 1, 2009 blog post titled *"Ferries"*, showing an initial test visualization of one day's worth of Automatic Identification System (AIS) ferry tracks (filtered for speeds greater than 1 knot) across the waters surrounding Long Island, New York, and southern New England.

## Map & Track Details

- **Geographic Region**: A top-down satellite view centered on **Long Island, New York**, **Long Island Sound**, and the coastlines of **New York**, **Connecticut**, and **Rhode Island** (with Block Island visible near the upper right), delineated by yellow shoreline and state boundary vectors.
- **Ferry Tracks (White Lines)**:
  - **New York Harbor (Bottom Left)**: Dense white track lines running between Manhattan, Staten Island, Brooklyn, and Sandy Hook / New Jersey (including the Staten Island Ferry and local commuter ferries).
  - **Long Island Sound (Center-Left)**: A thick bundle of white back-and-forth tracks crossing Long Island Sound between **Bridgeport, Connecticut**, and **Port Jefferson, New York**.
  - **Block Island Sound (Upper Right)**: White ferry tracks connecting **Point Judith, Rhode Island**, and **New London / Montauk** corridors to **Block Island**.

## Interface & Controls

- **Time Slider (Top Left)**: A translucent Google Earth timeline bar displays the timestamp `Jul 11, 2009 12:43 am`.
- **Navigation Controls (Right Edge)**: Partially visible along the right border are the Google Earth look/compass ring, pan control wheel, and vertical zoom slider (`+` / `-`).
