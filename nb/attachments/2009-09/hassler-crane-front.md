# Bow View of NOAA Ship Ferdinand R. Hassler Suspended from a Crane (`hassler-crane-front.png`)

## Overview

A video still frame from a September 29, 2009 blog post titled *"Hassler on a crane"*, showing the newly constructed coastal mapping vessel **NOAA Ship *Ferdinand R. Hassler* (S 250)**—a Small Waterplane Area Twin Hull (SWATH) ship—being lifted out of the water by a heavy shipyard crane prior to her delivery to New Hampshire.

## Scene Description

- **Vessel (*Ferdinand R. Hassler*)**: Viewed nearly head-on from the bow, suspended in mid-air by heavy steel cables attached to the upper deck structure.
  - **SWATH Twin Hulls**: The distinctive dark gray/black submerged twin cylindrical pontoons (lower hulls) and narrow vertical struts are completely exposed below the white upper hull box structure, with inward-facing stabilizing fins protruding from the inner sides of both pontoons.
  - **Bow & Superstructure**: The broad, flat white bow features two circular hawsepipes near the center. Above the main deck, the bridge superstructure is painted primer red/brown with a row of nine rectangular pilothouse windows framed in white, topped by a railed flying bridge.
- **Crane & Shipyard Background**:
  - A massive dark green lattice crane boom rises diagonally behind the vessel's starboard (left in frame) side.
  - A blue-and-white tugboat or workboat is partially visible at the bottom left edge, with shipyard buildings, light poles, and overcast sky in the background.
