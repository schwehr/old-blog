# Aerial View of Structural Geology East of Yellowstone (`plane-structural-geo.jpg`)

## Overview
An oblique aerial photograph taken from a commercial airplane window looking down at folded and eroded sedimentary rock formations just east of Yellowstone (in the Bighorn Basin / Wyoming region) during late afternoon sunlight (September 2009).

## Scene Description
- **Aircraft Wing (Top Left):**
  - The trailing edge and flap fairing canoe of the aircraft's wing extend across the top-left corner of the frame.
- **Geological Features:**
  - Displays a classic structural geology landscape of semi-arid basins, hogbacks, cuesta ridges, and plunging anticlines/synclines etched into tan, reddish-brown, and gray sedimentary strata.
  - In the lower-left quadrant, a sharp, sunlit hogback ridge forms a distinct linear and curving strike ridge bordering a flat, greenish-tan agricultural valley floor.
  - Across the center and right half, deeply incised dendritic drainage networks (badlands-style gullies and canyons) cut across tilted rock layers, catching warm, low-angle orange sunlight on their western slopes and casting long blue-gray shadows into the valleys.
  - Greenish vegetated floodplains follow a winding river channel cutting horizontally across the middle distance.
