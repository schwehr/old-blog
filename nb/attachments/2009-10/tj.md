# Google Earth View of NOAA Ship Thomas Jefferson Survey Tracklines (`tj.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot from Google Earth displaying the white AIS ship track of the NOAA hydrographic survey vessel *Thomas Jefferson* (`tj.kmz`) off the coast of New Hampshire and southern Maine (Kittery, Maine / Portsmouth, New Hampshire) at the beginning of October 2009.

## Map & Track Details
- **Coastline & Basemap**:
  - The left side shows high-resolution satellite imagery of the Piscataqua River mouth, New Castle/Portsmouth, New Hampshire, and Kittery/Gerrish Island, Maine.
  - A purple teardrop map pin labeled **Rcvr** with a white **`R`** inside marks the AIS receiver station near the entrance of Portsmouth Harbor / Fort Point.
  - Offshore to the east and south, the blue shaded bathymetry of the Gulf of Maine is visible, along with small rocky islands of the Isles of Shoals at the bottom center and Boon Island near the upper right.
- **Vessel Trackline**:
  - A thin white line traces the path of the *Thomas Jefferson* entering from the bottom right (southeast), heading northwest, and executing a dense, tightly spaced east-west "lawn-mowing" multibeam hydrographic survey grid off the coast of Kittery, Maine, in the upper-center portion of the image, with teardrop turn loops at either end of the parallel survey lines.
  - Another branch of the white track extends southwestward across the lower half of the image toward the harbor approaches off Odiorne Point / Rye, where the ship maneuvered at anchor before heading toward Portsmouth.
