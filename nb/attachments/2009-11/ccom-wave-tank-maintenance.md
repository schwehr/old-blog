# Drained Wave Tank Maintenance at CCOM (`ccom-wave-tank-maintenance.jpg`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken inside the high-bay engineering facility at the Center for Coastal and Ocean Mapping (CCOM) at the University of New Hampshire, looking lengthwise down the long, narrow wave/tow tank after it was drained of water for periodic maintenance.

## Scene Description
- **Drained Wave Tank (Center)**:
  - The long, rectangular concrete channel extends from the foreground into the background, terminating at a vertical bulkhead wall where a short ladder leans against the right corner.
  - The high vertical sidewalls are painted off-white/cream and show brownish-orange water-line staining and mineral deposits along their lower halves.
  - The flat floor of the tank is mostly empty except for a thin puddle/film of residual greenish-brown water reflecting the overhead lights.
- **Instrument Bridge & Rails (Top & Sides)**:
  - Metal guide rails run along the top edges of both the left and right tank walls.
  - Spanning across the width of the tank in the mid-ground is a horizontal metal carriage/bridge beam mounted on the rails.
- **Right Ledge & Foreground**:
  - Along the wide wooden-topped right wall of the tank, a green hose and a thinner orange hose or cable drape from the foreground into the tank.
  - Resting on the right ledge in the mid-ground is a white and blue cardboard box printed with the word `WADERS` (and `Bootfoot...` on the end flap), with a beige piece of equipment on top of it.
- **Person & Background**:
  - Standing at the far right edge behind the tank wall is an older man with white hair and glasses, wearing a blue collared work shirt, smiling slightly toward the left.
  - Behind the tank in the high-bay lab are industrial shelving, yellow ladders/frames, piping, and overhead lighting.
