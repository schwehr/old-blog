# USCGC Healy Stern and Starboard Propeller Shaft in Dry Dock (`healy-drydock-1-aft.jpg`)

## Overview
An evening/low-light iPhone photograph taken from the upper wall of the dry dock at Todd Pacific Shipyards in Seattle, looking toward the aft/starboard quarter of the U.S. Coast Guard icebreaker *USCGC Healy* (`WAGB-20`) while she rests out of the water on support columns for maintenance and a multibeam sonar upgrade.

## Scene Description
- **Upper Hull & Stern (Top & Center)**:
  - The angled red transom and starboard quarter of the hull dominate the upper half of the frame.
  - On the angled transom plate on the left, the ship's name `HEALY` is painted in white capital letters; on the slanted starboard quarter to the right, the large hull number `20` is painted in white with a small red-and-white emblem to its left.
  - Above the red hull plating on the working aft deck are yellow crane/A-frame structures, grey mooring bollards/chocks, and yellow safety railings.
- **Lower Hull, Support Columns & Starboard Shaft (Bottom)**:
  - Below the red waterline, the curving underbody of the hull is painted black/dark grey, with several white chalk or paint markings along the starboard side.
  - Directly beneath the centerline skeg/stern sits a tall, multi-column white steel support cradle (`A-frame` style dry dock stanchions) bearing the weight of the aft end of the ship.
  - To the lower right, beneath the starboard counter, the massive cylindrical starboard propeller shaft housing/bossing is visible with the propeller and rudder removed.
- **Dry Dock Wall & Lighting (Left)**:
  - The vertical grey wall of the dry dock forms the left background, illuminated by a bright white floodlight that casts a strong flare at the left edge of the hull, alongside vertical depth/draft markings on the far left wall.
