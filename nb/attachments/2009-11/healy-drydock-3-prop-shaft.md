# Port Propeller Shaft Partially Pulled Out on USCGC Healy (`healy-drydock-3-prop-shaft.jpg`)

## Overview
A medium close-up photograph taken from the dry dock floor beneath the stern of the *USCGC Healy*, showing the massive port propeller shaft partially withdrawn (about 10 feet) from its hull strut/bossing above a metal scaffolding platform, with a shipyard worker on the right providing scale.

- **Propeller Shaft & Strut (Upper Center & Left)**:
  - Extending horizontally to the left from the heavy cast-steel shaft strut/bossing (upper right) is the thick cylindrical steel port propeller shaft, terminating in a wide circular flange on the far left.
  - The exposed shaft is supported by heavy rigging suspended from overhead chain hoists (including a yellow hoist block and rusted chains), with a dark grey lifting sling and bright blue protective wrapping/tape around the polished middle section of the shaft.
- **Scaffolding & Rigging (Center & Foreground)**:
  - A two-level tubular steel scaffolding structure occupies the lower half of the frame, with metal plank decking directly beneath the shaft.
  - Coiled tan manila/synthetic rope and a brown lifting strap hang draped over the top horizontal guardrail of the scaffolding on the left.
- **Worker for Scale (Right)**:
  - On the far right, silhouetted against the bright daylight outside the dry dock shadow, a shipyard worker wearing dark coveralls and a white hard hat stands on the angled access stairs leading up to the scaffolding platform, illustrating the immense diameter of the icebreaker's propeller shaft and strut above him.
