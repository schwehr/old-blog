# Measuring a Hull Dent Under USCGC Healy with a 2x4 (`healy-drydock-5-dent.jpg`)

## Overview
A low-light, grainy iPhone photograph taken underneath the forward hull of the icebreaker *USCGC Healy* in dry dock, showing a member of the Oregon State University (OSU) group holding a wooden `2x4` board up against the dark steel hull plating to provide a straightedge scale across a 3-foot-wide, 4-inch-deep ice-impact dent discovered during dry-docking.

## Scene Description
- **Hull Plating & Dent (Upper & Center)**:
  - The curved, dark-painted underside of the ship fills most of the frame, scored with several parallel diagonal reddish-brown scrape marks where sea ice abraded the hull coating.
  - Across the concave depression in the hull plating, a light-colored wooden `2x4` stud is held diagonally, bridging the outer edges of the ~3-foot-wide dent.
- **Person Holding Scale (Bottom Center)**:
  - A man wearing a dark jacket, glasses, and a dark hard hat stands beneath the hull, reaching both hands overhead to press the wooden `2x4` board flat against the hull surface while looking up toward the left end of the board.
- **Background (Bottom Right)**:
  - A vertical grey post stands in the foreground to the right of the person, and bright daylight from the open end of the dry dock illuminates the narrow passageway between the hull and the dry dock wall at the far lower right.
