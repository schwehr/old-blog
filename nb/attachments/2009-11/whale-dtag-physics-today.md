# Physics Today Cover Featuring a DTAG on a Whale (`whale-dtag-physics-today.png`)

## Overview
A composite magazine cover graphic from the **November 2009** issue of *Physics Today* (`www.physicstoday.org`), illustrating Peter Tyack's article *"Human-generated sound and marine mammals"* with a close-up photograph of a non-invasive acoustic and movement recording tag (**DTAG**) attached via suction cups to the back of a whale near its dorsal fin.

## Visual & Text Details
- **Magazine Masthead (Upper Left):** Inside a golden-brown gradient rectangle in the top-left corner is the white sans-serif title **`physics today`**, with the URL `www.physicstoday.org` across the top and the issue date `November 2009` below.
- **Whale & Dorsal Fin:** The dark gray, scarred skin of a whale's back and prominent curved dorsal fin fill the center and right side of the frame, breaking the surface of calm, pale blue-gray ocean water.
- **DTAG Recording Device (Lower Center):**
  - Positioned just below the base of the dorsal fin is a streamlined white plastic housing mounted on four translucent blue/gray rubber suction cups pressed against the whale's skin.
  - The front nose of the white tag body has a bright yellow-orange patch, and a thin, long flexible VHF radio antenna wire trails off to the left from the rear of the tag.
  - Handwritten in black marker on the translucent central electronics housing is the radio frequency identifier **`148.433`**.
  - Four small black cylindrical release/vent caps with red/white wire leads sit atop the suction cup mounts.
