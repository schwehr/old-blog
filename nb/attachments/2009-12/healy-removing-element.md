# Removing a Multibeam Transmit Element Beneath USCGC Healy in Dry Dock (`healy-removing-element.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken by Dale Chayes beneath the hull of the U.S. Coast Guard icebreaker *USCGC Healy* (WAGB-20) in dry dock during the 2009–2010 multibeam echosounder replacement project, showing two technicians lowering a sonar transmit transducer element assembly onto a wooden cribbing block.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Action & Personnel**:
  - **Center**: A rectangular, metal-cased multibeam sonar transducer module with a yellow-orange polyurethane acoustic face on top and a thick gray power/signal cable protruding upward from one end rests atop a vertical, rough-sawn wooden timber post.
  - **Left Worker**: A technician wearing a yellow hard hat with an illuminated LED headlamp, clear safety glasses, brown work gloves, and a dusty brown long-sleeved shirt steadies the left side of the transducer module with his gloved hand.
  - **Right Worker**: A second technician in profile on the right, wearing a yellow hard hat with a metal brim bracket, tinted safety glasses, a plaid flannel shirt over a gray undershirt, and work gloves, reaches up with his right hand toward the edge of the wooden support post while looking up at the hull recess.
- **Ship Hull & Overhead Structure**:
  - Directly overhead is the flat steel bottom of the *Healy*'s hull, flanked by two heavy longitudinal steel box keelsons/skegs showing patches of orange surface rust.
  - Between the steel runners is the recessed multibeam sonar array bay painted light blue-gray, divided by transverse steel ribs/frames into individual rectangular mounting slots where transducer modules are bolted and wired.
- **Dry Dock Environment (Background)**:
  - The cramped space beneath the flat hull is supported by rows of heavy timber and concrete keel blocks receding into the dark background on the left.
  - On the right, a bright circular work light illuminates two other crew members in orange and dark work gear further back under the hull.
