# Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary Traffic Separation Scheme Shift (`sbnms-lane-shift-marine-spatial-planning.png`)

## Overview
A figure and explanatory caption from the White House Council on Environmental Quality's *Interim Framework for Effective Coastal and Marine Spatial Planning* (December 2009), illustrating the reconfiguration of the Boston Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) through the Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary (SBNMS) to reduce ship strikes on baleen and endangered North Atlantic right whales.

## Map & Symbology Details
- **Geographic Coverage**: Massachusetts Bay and Cape Cod Bay along the eastern coast of Massachusetts (`42°0'0"N` to `42°50'0"N` latitude, `71°0'0"W` to `69°50'0"W` longitude), showing Boston Harbor on the mid-left, Cape Ann to the north, and the hooked tip of Cape Cod (Provincetown) at the bottom-center/right.
- **Map Overlays & Legend (Lower Left)**:
  - **Baleen Whale Density**: Continuous heatmap color bar from `Low` (blue/cyan) through green and yellow to `High` (orange/red), concentrated along the north-south spine and southern lobe of Stellwagen Bank inside the sanctuary boundary.
  - **Right Whale Sightings**: Hundreds of small white circles with black outlines (`o`) densely clustered across Cape Cod Bay in the south and extending northward along Stellwagen Bank.
  - **Deepwater LNG Port**: Four larger bright green circles with black central dots located just west of the sanctuary boundary near the Boston Harbor approach.
  - **Traffic Separation Scheme (Solid Black Line)**: The reconfigured shipping lanes extending eastward from the circular precautionary area outside Boston Harbor, angled slightly northward to pass through a narrower, lower-density neck of whale habitat before turning southeast offshore.
  - **Old Traffic Separation Scheme (Dashed Black Line)**: The previous shipping lanes angled slightly further south across the high-density baleen and right whale aggregation area on southern Stellwagen Bank.
  - **Stellwagen Bank NMS (Purple Outline)**: A polygonal boundary enclosing Stellwagen Bank, labeled vertically along its eastern edge: `Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary`.
- **Map Elements**: Compass rose (`N`, `S`, `E`, `W`) in the upper left on land, and a scale bar at the bottom left from `0` to `10 Nautical Miles` (`0`, `1`, `2`, `4`, `6`, `8`, `10`).

## Visible Text & Caption
- **Title (Top)**: *Example of the Potential Benefits of CMSP: Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary*
- **Caption Paragraph (Bottom)**:
  > Comprehensive planning enabled the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), United States Coast Guard, and several other government agencies and stakeholders to examine shipping needs, proposed deepwater liquefied natural gas port locations, and endangered whale distribution in a successful effort to reconfigure the Boston Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) to reduce the risk of whale mortality due to collisions with ships in the Stellwagen Bank National Marine Sanctuary. The reconfigured TSS reduced risk of collision by an estimated 81% for all baleen whales and 58% for endangered right whales. Industry TSS transit times increased by only 9 – 22 minutes (depending on speed) and conflict with deepwater ports was eliminated. In addition, the new route decreased the overlap between ships using the TSS, commercial fishing vessels, and whale watch vessels, thereby increasing maritime safety. CMSP has the significant potential of applying this integrated, multi-objective, multi-sector approach on a broader, sustained scale.
