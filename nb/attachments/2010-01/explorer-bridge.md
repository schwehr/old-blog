# Interior View of the Bridge on the LNG Carrier *Explorer* (`explorer-bridge.jpg`)

## Overview
A wide-angle color photograph showing the clean, modern interior of the navigation bridge aboard the liquefied natural gas (LNG) tanker *Explorer* while moored at the Northeast Gateway deepwater port in Massachusetts Bay on January 21, 2010.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground & Seating**: In the lower left foreground sits a sturdy wooden captain/observer armchair with armrests and a black cushioned seat, positioned on a dark gray non-slip bridge deck floor.
- **Forward Windows & Counter (Right)**:
  - Large, forward-sloping bridge windows along the right side look out over the bright blue ocean and sky. Window wiper mechanisms, pull-down sunshade cords, and black junction boxes are mounted on the frames, along with two small white directional/info stickers on the glass.
  - Running beneath the windows is a long, teal-blue counter console with a stainless-steel grab rail along its inner edge. A silver Apple MacBook Pro laptop sits open at the far end of this counter.
- **Central Navigation Console**:
  - In the middle distance stands a large, angled light-gray and teal-blue helm and radar/ECDIS instrument island with hinged access cabinet doors along its base, a notebook resting on its counter, and gooseneck microphones extending upward.
  - Beyond the central console toward the port bridge wing, a crew member in a dark shirt stands looking forward through the bridge windows.
- **Aft Instrument Panels & Chart Area (Left)**:
  - Along the left side are light-green and beige electronics cabinets fitted with digital displays, indicator lights, push buttons, and binders/manuals stored in slots below.
  - A dark navy blackout curtain separates the forward bridge walkway from the chart/communications room on the left, where a second man in a dark polo shirt stands near wall-mounted green electrical/alarm panels and white task lights.
- **Ceiling**: Dark teal/slate acoustic ceiling panels with recessed fluorescent light fixtures and overhead status indicators.
