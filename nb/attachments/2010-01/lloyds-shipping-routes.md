# Global Cargo Ship Routes Map (`lloyds-shipping-routes.gif`)

## Overview
A global map visualization from Kaluza et al. (2010), *"The complex network of global cargo ship movements"*, showing the trajectories and traffic density of 16,363 cargo ships during the year 2007 based on Lloyd's Marine Intelligence Unit itinerary data, plotted along great-circle routes between ports.

## Map & Legend Details
- **Base Map**:
  - World continents and major islands are rendered in flat light gray against a medium periwinkle-blue ocean background.
- **Shipping Route Network**:
  - Curved great-circle arcs and coastal shipping corridors criss-cross the globe, connecting ports across the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans, as well as the Mediterranean Sea, Red Sea, Persian Gulf, and East Asian seas.
  - Route density is color-coded from black (lowest frequency) through dark red, bright red, and orange to bright yellow (highest frequency).
  - Bright yellow high-traffic corridors stand out prominently along the US East and West Coasts, through the Panama Canal, across the North Atlantic to Western Europe, through the Strait of Gibraltar, Mediterranean Sea, Suez Canal, Red Sea, around the southern tip of India, through the Strait of Malacca, along the coast of China and Japan, around the Cape of Good Hope in South Africa, and along the east coast of South America and Australia.
- **Color Scale Legend**:
  - Along the bottom against a white background is a horizontal color bar labeled **`journeys`** on the right.
  - Logarithmic tick marks and labels run along the bottom of the color bar from left (black) to right (bright yellow):
    - **`<10`**, **`20`**, **`50`**, **`100`**, **`200`**, **`500`**, **`1000`**, **`2000`**, **`>5000`**.
