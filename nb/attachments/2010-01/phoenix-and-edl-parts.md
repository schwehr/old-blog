# Close-Up Insets of Phoenix Mars Lander and EDL Hardware (`phoenix-and-edl-parts.png`)

## Overview
A three-panel horizontal composite image from NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) HiRISE camera (part of NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona release PIA12490) showing close-up orbital views of the Phoenix Mars Lander and its Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) hardware amid receding seasonal spring carbon-dioxide ice on the northern plains of Mars.

## Panel Details (Left to Right)
- **Background Terrain (All Panels)**:
  - Each square inset is framed by a black border and shows a patterned Martian periglacial surface composed of dark brown/olive-gray polygonal soil mounds outlined by a network of bright bluish-white troughs filled with lingering seasonal frost/ice.
- **Left Panel (Heat Shield / Backshell)**:
  - Near the center-left sits a bright, specular white spot casting a small dark shadow to the lower left, surrounded by a lighter gray frost/dust patch interrupting the dark polygons.
- **Middle Panel (Parachute / Backshell Site)**:
  - Near the center of the polygonal network is a subtle, pale circular/oval ring-like feature and light-toned patch blending into the frost-lined troughs where the parachute and backshell came to rest.
- **Right Panel (Phoenix Lander)**:
  - Near the center sits a dark central spot flanked on both sides by two smaller, slightly brighter lobes (the lander's circular solar-panel wings) within a broader pale-gray frost halo.
