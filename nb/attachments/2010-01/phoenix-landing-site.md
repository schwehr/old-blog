# Phoenix Mars Lander Landing Site Context View (`phoenix-landing-site.png`)

## Overview
A wide-area orbital image captured by the HiRISE camera aboard NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (from release PIA12490) showing the polygonal patterned ground of the Martian northern plains during northern spring as seasonal ice recedes, with three green square bounding boxes marking the locations of the Phoenix Mars Lander and its Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) hardware (shown magnified in `phoenix-and-edl-parts.png`).

## Image Details
- **Surface Morphology**:
  - The entire frame is covered by a dense, honeycomb-like network of periglacial polygonal terrain: dark brownish-gray polygon interiors surrounded by bright bluish-white interconnecting troughs filled with residual carbon-dioxide ice and frost.
  - Several longer, wider, brighter linear fractures/troughs run diagonally from the upper center toward the lower right.
- **Highlighted Hardware Locations (Green Squares)**:
  - **Left Box (Mid-Left)**: Marks a bright white point (the heat shield) surrounded by a pale frost patch.
  - **Bottom Center-Right Box**: Marks the subtle pale feature of the backshell/parachute resting in the polygonal network.
  - **Top Right Box**: Marks the Phoenix Mars Lander itself, visible as a tiny dark dot with flanking solar arrays inside a lighter-toned disturbed frost zone.
