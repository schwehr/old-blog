# SONS 2010 Team Reviewing ERMA Display (`sons2010-erma.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken on March 24, 2010, during the Spill of National Significance (SONS) 2010 oil spill response exercise in Portland, Maine. It shows two exercise participants examining the Environmental Response Management Application (ERMA) web-based GIS interface on a large flat-panel monitor.

## Scene & Participants
- **Left Participant**: A woman seen from behind and the side, with shoulder-length blonde hair, wearing a dark long-sleeved shirt and a bright orange emergency response vest. She is raising her right hand and pointing with her index finger at a specific coastal feature on the map display.
- **Right Participant**: A man seen from behind in a dark blue United States Coast Guard utility uniform jacket with a white lanyard around his neck. The back of his jacket features large white-outlined block letters reading **`USCG`**, and his left sleeve bears a full-color U.S. flag patch. He is looking toward the area on the screen where the woman is pointing.
- **Background**: A large black-bezeled flat-screen monitor mounted on a stand draped in black cloth. To the bottom right, the corner of a laptop screen is visible on a table.

## ERMA Display & Interface Details
- **Browser & OS**: The monitor displays a Mac OS X desktop running the Mozilla Firefox web browser (with the top menu bar showing `Firefox  File  Edit  View  History  Bookmarks  Tools  Window  Help` and `Wed 12:08 PM` in the upper right).
- **Header Banner**: Below the browser address bar is the **`ERMA`** (*Environmental Response Management Application — New England*) banner with a coastal photograph on the right.
- **Map View**: The central pane shows an OpenLayers coastal map of southern Maine and New Hampshire (centered near Casco Bay and the Piscataqua River / Portsmouth area) with state route markers and colored rectangular/polygonal overlays in red, orange, and tan along the coastline representing response zones or shoreline impact segments.
- **Right Sidebar (`Legend` Tab)**: The active `Legend` tab displays symbology for the exercise, including:
  - `SONS M/V Tula`
  - `Command Posts` (with purple, blue, and pink triangle icons for Incident Command Post, Forward Command Post, etc.)
  - `M/V Tula Backlight`
  - `M/V Tula`
  - Several shoreline and boom/response line symbols
  - `ERMA Tools` and `Map Labels` at the bottom, above the Coastal Response Research Center (CRRC) / University of New Hampshire (UNH) attribution logo.
