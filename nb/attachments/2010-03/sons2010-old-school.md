# SONS 2010 Operations Team Examining Paper Map (`sons2010-old-school.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken on March 24, 2010, during Day 1 of the Spill of National Significance (SONS) 2010 oil spill exercise in Portland, Maine. The image illustrates the continued reliance on printed paper maps and manual notes ("old school" tools) alongside laptops inside the command center.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Participants**: Two male responders are seated side-by-side at a conference table, photographed from behind over their shoulders. Both wear bright orange incident command vests.
  - **Left Participant**: Wears a tan collared shirt under his orange vest and glasses, leaning in closely to inspect the paper map.
  - **Right Participant**: Wears a dark maroon striped collared shirt under his orange vest and glasses. A large white rectangular label across the back of his vest reads **`OPS`** in bold black capital letters (indicating the Operations Section).
- **Paper Map**: The responder on the right holds an 8.5×11-inch color printout of a coastal GIS map showing a green landmass (the Maine/New Hampshire coast), light blue water, and colored red/orange/yellow shoreline segments along the coast, accompanied by tabular text blocks printed over the ocean area on the right side of the page. Another person's hand holding a pen is visible just above the top edge of the paper map, pointing toward the title block.
- **Surrounding Workspace**: Between the two responders, the glowing screen and keyboard of a small black laptop are partially visible on the table. Stacks of white paper documents sit on the white-tablecloth-covered tables to the left and right, with additional exercise participants (including one wearing a green vest) visible in the background.
