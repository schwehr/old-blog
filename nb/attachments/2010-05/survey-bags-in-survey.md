# Google Earth View of Individual BAG Files Within a Hydrographic Survey (`survey-bags-in-survey.png`)

## Overview

A screenshot from Google Earth showing a zoomed-in view of the Elizabeth Islands and the western tip of Martha's Vineyard (Aquinnah/Gay Head, Massachusetts), illustrating how KML regions display both overall NOAA hydrographic survey bounding boxes (in orange) and the individual Bathymetric Attributed Grid (BAG) file bounding boxes and icons (in white) inside survey `H11920`.

## Map & Overlay Details

- **Geographic Base Map**: Satellite imagery of southern Massachusetts waters, with cyan place labels for `Elizabeth Islands`, `Naushon Island`, `Pasque Island`, `South Rock`, and `Edys Island`.
- **Zoomed-Out Survey Icons & Orange Bounding Boxes**:
  - Several large orange rectangular outlines mark the overall boundaries of neighboring hydrographic surveys that have not yet switched to their individual BAG views.
  - **`H11076`**: Located near Pasque Island / South Rock, displaying a rainbow-colored gridded square icon with a brown/red circle badge in the lower-right corner showing `13` (indicating 13 BAG files in that survey).
  - **`H11921`**: Located to the west near Cuttyhunk Island, displaying the survey icon with a badge showing `4`.
- **Zoomed-In Individual BAG Files (`H11920`)**:
  - Inside the large orange bounding box on the right (covering Menemsha Bight and the northwestern coast of Martha's Vineyard), the KML region has activated to reveal 18 individual BAG sub-grids, each outlined by a white rectangular bounding box and marked with an inverted rainbow-grid icon (without a number badge):
    - `H11920_2m_3of18` (bottom left of the survey box)
    - `H11920_50cm_15of18` and `H11920_50cm_9of18` (near the southwest corner)
    - `H11920_50cm_5of18`, `H11920_2m_4of18`, `H11920_50cm_7of18`, `H11920_50cm_8of18`, `H11920_50cm_14of18` (clustered through the center and south near Edys Island)
    - `H11920_50cm_11of18`, `H11920_50cm_6of18`, `H11920_2m_2of18`, `H11920_50cm_13of18`, `H11920_2m_1of18`, and `H1192...` (along the northeastern shoreline of the survey area)
