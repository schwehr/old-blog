# Radar Water Level Sensor Mounted Over the Wave Tank (`wave-tank-radar.jpg`)

## Overview

An indoor high-angle photograph of the wave tank facility at the University of New Hampshire (UNH) Chase Ocean Engineering Laboratory, showing a downward-looking radar water level measurement sensor mounted on an overhead frame above the agitated water surface and a floating yellow buoy.

## Scene Description

- **Radar Water Level Sensor (Left Foreground)**:
  - Mounted on a light gray metal support arm extending out over the left wall of the wave tank is a blue-and-white cylindrical radar transmitter housing with a circular display window on its face and a downward-pointing conical horn antenna aimed directly at the water surface.
  - Immediately behind the sensor stand are the dark metal forks and mast of a forklift positioned beside the tank wall.
- **Wave Tank & Buoy (Center)**:
  - The long rectangular concrete and glass-walled wave tank runs diagonally from the lower-left foreground toward the upper-right background, filled with clear turquoise-green water showing active surface waves and ripples.
  - Floating in the middle of the tank is a round, bright yellow instrumented buoy with a dark red/orange base and a top ring mount holding dark cables/sensors.
  - At the far end of the tank (upper right), a metal frame spans the tank above brown/orange wave-absorbing netting/baffles and two small round orange floats.
- **Laboratory Interior (Background)**:
  - Beyond the tank are the high-bay laboratory floor, interior office windows, a workbench, blue trash barrels, double blue doors, a wooden stepladder leaning against the back wall, and a bicycle parked beside the right tank wall in the lower-right corner.
