# Early Public Preview of GeoPlatform.gov ERMA (`geoplatform.gov.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of the initial public preview of **WWW.GEOPLATFORM.GOV** running NOAA's **Environmental Response Management Application (ERMA)** on June 2, 2010, displaying the Deepwater Horizon MC 252 wellhead location and nearshore/offshore oil spill trajectory estimates in the Gulf of Mexico.

## Map View & Overlays (Left Pane)
- **Base Map**: A Google Maps road/terrain map of the Gulf of Mexico and the southern United States (`Texas`, `Louisiana`, `Mississippi`, `Alabama`, `Georgia`, `Florida`), as well as northeastern Mexico (`Heroica Matamoros`, `Tampico`) and western Cuba (`Pinar del Río`) at the bottom.
- **Trajectory & Boundary Polygons**:
  - **Outer Black Outlines**: Two overlapping black-outlined polygons: one enclosing the main northern Gulf slick area off southeastern Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and Pensacola, Florida, and a second elongated polygon extending southeastward into the central/eastern Gulf toward the Florida Straits (following the Loop Current).
  - **Light-Blue & Dark-Blue Slick Contours**: Inside the northern polygon is a large light-blue/cyan region representing the main surface oil slick, containing smaller medium-blue and dark-blue patches near the center (marking heavier oil concentrations) and a bright red shoreline impact segment along the Chandeleur Islands / Breton Sound in Louisiana and a small red mark near Pensacola.
  - **Cross-Hatched Light-Blue Patches**: Several detached light-blue cross-hatched polygons lie inside the southeastern extension polygon, indicating potential offshore trajectory areas in the Loop Current.
