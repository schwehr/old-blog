# ERMA Running in the USCG Mobile Incident Command Post in Mobile, Alabama (`dwh-erma-at-uscg-cmd-AL.png`)

## Overview
A cropped U.S. Coast Guard photograph taken by Petty Officer 3rd Class Walter Shinn on July 2, 2010, inside an Enhanced Mobile Incident Command Post in Mobile, Alabama, during the Deepwater Horizon oil spill response. The image shows two Coast Guard personnel working at laptops while a large wall-mounted flat-screen monitor behind them displays NOAA's Environmental Response Management Application (ERMA).

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Personnel**:
  - Two U.S. Coast Guard service members in dark navy blue operational dress uniforms sit side-by-side at a long command table.
  - The person in the left foreground is a young man with short light-brown hair looking toward a laptop screen to the right.
  - Beside him, a woman with her dark hair tied back in a bun smiles slightly while typing on a laptop keyboard.
  - On the desk in front of them are a handheld VHF radio with a bright green antenna, a plastic water bottle, a black desk lamp, and two multi-line office telephones.
- **Left Wall Monitor (ERMA Display)**:
  - A large black-bezel flat-panel display (with a small laptop resting on its stand below) shows a web browser running **ERMA (Environmental Response Management Application) – Gulf of Mexico**.
  - The map viewport displays satellite imagery of the northern Gulf Coast (Mississippi Sound / Mobile Bay / Chandeleur Islands region) in deep blue water and green coastline, with white text labels scattered across the water and shore.
  - The right-hand sidebar of the ERMA interface is expanded, showing the hierarchical layer tree of available GIS layers.
- **Right Wall Monitor (Television Broadcast)**:
  - A second wall-mounted monitor at the upper right is tuned to an **MSNBC** television broadcast showing dark water footage with a white target circle and a lower-third banner reading **`JUMP CUT SHORT`** above the `msnbc.com | ENTERTAINMENT` ticker.
