# ANSS Earthquake Catalog Visualization in Google Earth: Oblique View of Chile and Argentina (`anss-google-earth-southamerica-2.png`)

## Overview
A close-up 3D oblique perspective screenshot from Google Earth looking north along the **Andes subduction zone** in **Chile and Argentina** (with the white coastline floating at 700 km altitude), clearly illustrating the 3D geometry of the **Wadati-Benioff zone** as the Nazca Plate subducts eastward beneath the South American Plate. Featured in Kurt Schwehr's August 19, 2010 blog post *"ANSS quake catalog in Google Earth"*.

## 3D Subduction Zone Structure
- **Perspective & Orientation:** The camera looks north-northeast along the strike of the Peru-Chile Trench, so west (the Pacific Ocean) is to the left, east (Argentina/Brazil) is to the right, and depth increases downward from the white coastline near the top.
- **Shallow Trench Seismicity (Left / Upper Left):**
  - Dense clouds of overlapping white and pale pink circles—each labeled with its numerical magnitude (such as `7.5`, `7.7`, `6.7`, `6.8`, `6.5`, `6.4`, `6.2`, `6.0`, `5.8`, `5.6`, `5.5`, `5.4`, `5.2`, `5.0`)—cluster near the white coastal outline on the left.
- **Intermediate-Depth Slab (Center):**
  - Dipping downward and to the right (eastward) from the coast is a thick, inclined sheet of pink and light-red earthquake circles (with visible magnitudes including `6.4`, `6.3`, `6.0`, `5.6`, `5.4`, `5.3`, `5.2`, `5.1`, `5.0`), tracing the descending oceanic lithosphere at intermediate depths (roughly 70–200 km).
  - Noticeable variations in slab dip angle (flat-slab vs. steeper-slab segments) can be seen between the foreground and background.
- **Deep-Focus Earthquakes (Lower Right):**
  - On the far right, separated from the intermediate-depth seismicity by an aseismic gap, a near-vertical line of bright red circles (including prominent `6.9` magnitude events and smaller `5.1`–`6.0` events) marks deep-focus earthquakes (>200–600 km depth) beneath Argentina and Bolivia.
