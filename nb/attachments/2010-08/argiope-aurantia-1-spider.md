# Yellow Garden Spider (*Argiope aurantia*) in Shrubbery (`argiope-aurantia-1-spider.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up photograph of a female yellow garden spider (**Argiope aurantia**) perched upside-down among green shrub leaves in Kurt Schwehr's yard. Featured in the August 23, 2010 blog post *"Critters in the yard"*, where Kurt noted that her body was about the size of a quarter.

## Subject & Scene Description
- **Spider Anatomy & Markings:**
  - **Abdomen:** A large, oval abdomen with a striking black central dorsal band (containing two pairs of small yellow spots) flanked on both sides by bright yellow-and-black tiger-like transverse stripes/scallops.
  - **Cephalothorax:** A smaller, silvery-gray pubescent cephalothorax visible at the lower-right end of the abdomen (as the spider is oriented head-downward/rightward).
  - **Legs:** Long, slender dark black legs with reddish-brown proximal segments (femora) extending outward across the surrounding leaves.
- **Foliage Background:** Dense, glossy green oval leaves with finely serrated edges (such as euonymus/wintercreeper), including a few variegated yellow-and-green leaves near the upper left and behind the spider.
