# Earthquakes, Volcanoes, and Plate Margins: Northwestern US to Alaska (`quakes-volcanoes-margins-1.png`)

## Overview
A 3D Google Earth visualization screenshot showing historical earthquakes from the ANSS catalog, Smithsonian volcanoes, and tectonic plate boundaries extending from the northwestern United States and Western Canada up through the Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone, with an open callout bubble over the Yellowstone caldera.

## Map / Visualization Details
- **Background & Reference Frame**: The globe is rendered solid black with thin white coastlines (NOAA NGDC World Coast Line) and colored plate boundary lines suspended at 700 km elevation so that deep earthquakes appear in 3D space beneath the surface boundaries.
- **Tectonic Plate Boundaries**:
  - **Yellow lines** mark convergent/subduction boundaries along the Cascadia subduction zone (off Oregon, Washington, and Vancouver Island) and the Aleutian Trench sweeping across southern Alaska and the Aleutian Islands.
  - **Green lines** trace transform faults, including the Queen Charlotte Fault along the coast of British Columbia/Southeast Alaska, segments of the San Andreas / Mendocino system, and fracture zones offsetting the Juan de Fuca / Explorer / Gorda ridges.
  - **Orange lines** mark divergent spreading ridge segments along the Juan de Fuca, Gorda, and Explorer ridges offshore of the Pacific Northwest.
- **Earthquakes**: Plotted as circular icons containing their numeric magnitude (e.g., `7.8` in Alaska, `7.2`, `7.5`, `6.7` near the Mendocino triple junction) and shaded from white (shallow) to blue (deep). Dense dipping clouds of blue and white seismicity trace the subducting Pacific plate beneath the Aleutian arc, while shallow white earthquakes cluster tightly along the Juan de Fuca fracture zones and Queen Charlotte transform fault.
- **Volcanoes & Pop-up Balloon**: Red-and-orange volcano icons trace the Cascade Volcanic Arc (from Northern California through Oregon, Washington, and British Columbia), the Aleutian Volcanic Arc, and interior volcanic fields across the western US. A white Google Earth info balloon points to the **Yellowstone** volcano icon in Wyoming and displays:
