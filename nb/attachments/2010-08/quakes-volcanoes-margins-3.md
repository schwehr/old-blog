# Oblique 3D View of Subducting Slab, Earthquakes, and Volcanoes in Chile (`quakes-volcanoes-margins-3.png`)

## Overview
An oblique, northward-looking 3D Google Earth view of the Nazca–South America subduction zone along Chile and Peru, illustrating the Wadati–Benioff zone of earthquakes dipping eastward beneath the Andean volcanic arc.

## Map / Visualization Details
- **3D Perspective & Elevation Offset**: Coastlines (white lines) and plate boundaries (yellow, orange, and green lines) are suspended at an altitude of 700 km above the black globe surface, with earthquake hypocenters plotted relative to that 700 km datum so that the 3D geometry of the subducting slab is visible below the surface.
- **Earthquake Depth & Magnitude Symbology**:
  - Earthquakes are rendered as circular icons labeled with their magnitude (visible values include `6.0`, `5.0`, `5.2`, `6.4`, `6.7`, `5.9`, `5.7`, `6.5`, `6.3`, `5.3`, `5.6`, `6.2`).
  - **Shallow earthquakes** near the Peru–Chile Trench (yellow line on the left) are colored white and pale lavender-blue.
  - **Intermediate-depth earthquakes** shift to medium blue as they dip eastward beneath the volcanic arc.
  - **Deep-focus earthquakes** (down to 500–650 km depth in the back right and far right) appear in saturated royal blue, forming a distinct steep lineament of deep seismicity far inland beneath South America.
- **Volcanoes & Plate Boundaries**:
  - Prominent red-and-yellow erupting volcano icons form a dense linear chain along the crest of the Andes directly above the intermediate-depth slab, with a few isolated oceanic volcanoes (such as Easter Island / Salas y Gómez and Galápagos) visible along the orange divergent ridges to the west (left).
