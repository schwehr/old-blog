# L3 AIS AtoN Multi-Pin Serial Connector and Penny Comparison (`l3-ais-aton-expensive-connector.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up macro photograph showing the proprietary circular multi-pin `RS-232 I/O` connector on the front panel of the yellow **L3 Desktop AtoN** unit alongside its unplugged mating cable connector and a United States one-cent coin (penny) for scale.

## Scene & Hardware Details
- **L3 Front Panel & Receptacle (Upper Right)**:
  - The brushed aluminum front faceplate is mounted to the bright yellow chassis and secured by two Phillips-head machine screws positioned vertically to the left of the port.
  - The chassis-mount circular receptacle has a hexagonal outer mounting nut, a black cylindrical shell, and a red/orange insulator insert populated with 19 gold-plated male pins arranged in a dense circular grid.
- **Mating Cable Plug (Center Left)**:
  - Resting unplugged on the gray desk surface to the left of the enclosure is the black circular female plug, showing matching gold-plated socket contacts recessed inside a black insulator and a bayonet/locking outer ring.
  - A small white label fragment with partial black letters (`CHIN...`) is visible inside the left rim of the connector shell.
  - Extending backward from the plug is a thick bundle of cables encased in translucent spiral wrap with a red tracer stripe, installed to strain-relieve and protect the fragile wiring.
- **Scale Reference (Bottom Foreground)**:
  - A copper U.S. Lincoln penny (`1984` date visible to the right of Lincoln's profile, `LIBERTY` on the left, `IN GOD WE TRUST` along the top rim) lies flat on the gray desk surface in the foreground, demonstrating that the circular 19-pin connector has roughly the same diameter as a penny (~19 mm).
