# Close-Up of Mylonitic Rock with Porphyroclasts (`bc-field-trip-10.jpg`)

## Overview
A macro geological photograph showing a sheared, foliated fault-related rock surface (mylonite or gneiss) with prominent mineral porphyroclasts, taken during the Boston College 2010 ES-SSA field trip in eastern Massachusetts. A human finger enters from the lower left for scale.

## Geological Details & Composition
- **Scale Reference**: In the lower-left corner, an index finger points diagonally upward toward the right, resting just to the left of the largest mineral clast.
- **Rock Matrix & Foliation**: The rock consists of a dark greenish-gray to charcoal-black fine-grained matrix with strong, steeply dipping planar/wavy foliation running nearly vertically (slightly tilted from top-right to bottom-left).
- **Feldspar Porphyroclasts**:
  - Directly to the right of the fingertip is a large, pinkish-white to cream-colored feldspar porphyroclast (approximately 2–3 cm across) with asymmetric sheared tails extending upward and downward along the foliation plane (forming a sigma/delta clast geometry indicative of shear sense).
  - Smaller rounded and elongated white/pinkish clasts and thin stringers are distributed throughout the left and right sides of the matrix.
- **Dark Mafic Band**: Just to the right of the central feldspar porphyroclast is a distinct, slightly recessed vertical band of very dark black mineral grains (likely hornblende or biotite-rich layer) cut by fine horizontal fractures.
- **Right Side**: On the middle right, a rounded, pale gray-violet mineral grain (likely quartz or feldspar) sits embedded within the mottled gray-and-black gneissic matrix.
