# Close-Up of Pseudotachylite Vein in Faulted Rock (`bc-field-trip-3-pseudotachylite.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up geological field photograph showing a dark pseudotachylite fault vein cutting through banded, reddish-and-gray rock, taken during the Boston College 2010 ES-SSA field trip in eastern Massachusetts. A hand holds a white ballpoint pen against the outcrop to point out the feature and provide scale.

## Geological Details & Composition
- **Scale & Pointer**: In the lower-left quadrant, a person's hand holds a white ballpoint pen angled diagonally upward to the right. The brass tip of the pen points directly to the dark, glassy-looking pseudotachylite vein near the center of the frame.
- **Host Rock**: The surrounding rock is a finely laminated or foliated dark greenish-gray rock interlayered with prominent horizontal to gently dipping bands of brick-red, salmon-pink, and pale gray/white material.
- **Pseudotachylite & Fault Offset**:
  - Running diagonally from the upper-left/center toward the lower-right is a dark charcoal-black, aphanitic vein of pseudotachylite (frictional melt generated during seismic slip).
  - The reddish-orange horizontal bands are visibly offset and dragged along the diagonal shear plane where the pen tip rests.
- **Surface Features**: Fine, steep fractures cross the rock face from top to bottom. Near the lower-right corner, small patches of pale greenish-gray foliose lichen and whitish crusty lichen adhere to the dark rock surface.
