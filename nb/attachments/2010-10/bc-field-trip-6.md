# Contact Between Dark Mafic and Light Felsic Rock Units (`bc-field-trip-6.jpg`)

## Overview
A geological field photograph from the Boston College 2010 ES-SSA field trip in eastern Massachusetts, showing an exposed bedrock outcrop with a sharp contact between dark gray/black rock in the foreground and lighter pinkish-tan rock above, topped by autumn forest litter and trees against a clear blue sky.

## Geological & Scene Details
- **Foreground Unit (Lower Half)**: Angular, blocky outcrops of dark charcoal-gray to greenish-black rock (such as mafic volcanic, amphibolite, or gabbro/diorite) occupy the bottom half of the image, casting sharp shadows in the bright sunlight. At the bottom-right corner, a flat fracture plane exhibits rusty yellow-brown weathering.
- **Upper Unit & Contact Zone (Middle Band)**: Directly behind and above the dark rock is a contrasting band of light pinkish-white to buff-tan rock (felsic granite, pegmatite, or felsite) cut by close-spaced fractures. Small patches of green moss grow along the contact hollow on the left.
- **Background & Vegetation (Upper Third)**:
  - A carpet of dry brown pine needles and fallen autumn leaves covers the slope above the light-colored rock ledge.
  - To the upper right rises a steep, shadowed rock face mottled with orange-brown weathering.
  - Bare and partially leafed tree trunks and dried golden-brown leaves on the left frame a deep, vivid blue autumn sky.
