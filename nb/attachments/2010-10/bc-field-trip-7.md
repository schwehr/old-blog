# Fractured and Jointed Roadcut Outcrop (`bc-field-trip-7.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph of a steep, blocky, fractured rock wall taken during the Boston College 2010 ES-SSA field trip examining fault-related rocks in eastern Massachusetts.

## Geological & Scene Details
- **Outcrop Structure**: A nearly vertical rock cut spanning the full width of the frame, rising from a narrow strip of green grass and brown pine-needle litter at the base to a wooded slope at the top.
- **Jointing & Fractures**:
  - The rock face is broken into rectangular and tabular blocks by prominent sets of sub-horizontal and steeply dipping sub-vertical joints.
  - On the right side, a steep, planar fault or joint surface dips steeply to the left, casting a dark diagonal shadow line from top to bottom.
  - Left of center, a vertical fracture zone shows white mineral veining or light-colored mineralization streaking down the rock face.
- **Color & Weathering**: Fresh or less-weathered surfaces appear medium to dark bluish-gray, while joint planes across the central section are coated with tan, buff, and rusty orange-brown mineralization and weathering rinds.
- **Upper Slope**: Above the top edge of the cliff on the upper left are green pine boughs, a smooth gray tree trunk, and low bushes with reddish-orange autumn leaves.
