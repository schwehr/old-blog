# Margaret Boettcher Pointing to Folded Felsic Veins in Gneiss (`bc-field-trip-8.jpg`)

## Overview
A medium close-up photograph from the Boston College 2010 ES-SSA geology field trip showing geophysicist Margaret Boettcher smiling and pointing at deformed, folded pinkish-white felsic bands and veins within a dark gray gneissic rock face.

## Scene & Geological Details
- **Subject**: In the lower-right quadrant, **Margaret Boettcher** stands in profile facing left, smiling warmly as she looks at the outcrop. She wears a black long-sleeved top, a red conference lanyard around her neck, silver hoop/drop earrings, and sunglasses pushed up on top of her wavy brown hair. Her right arm extends diagonally upward to the left, with her index finger pointing directly at a tight fold hinge in the rock.
- **Rock Outcrop**:
  - The background is filled by a vertical face of dark gray metamorphic/fault rock (gneiss or mylonite) heavily intruded and banded with light pinkish-orange and white granitic/pegmatitic veins.
  - **Folded Feature**: Right at the tip of her index finger, a thin white-and-pinkish vein forms a distinct, tight asymmetric fold (hook-shaped fold nose) within the darker gray foliated matrix.
  - **Surrounding Bands**: To the left of her finger is a thicker, steeply dipping lens of coarse pinkish-tan feldspar and quartz; to the right (above her arm) is a wide vertical band of white-and-gray streaked granitic material.
