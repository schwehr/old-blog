# NOAA David Skaggs Research Center in Boulder, Colorado (`boulder-noaa.jpg`)

## Overview
An exterior photograph of the entrance to the NOAA David Skaggs Research Center (home to the National Geophysical Data Center, Space Weather Prediction Center, and Earth System Research Laboratory) in Boulder, Colorado, built with local red-pink sandstone quarried from the nearby Flatirons.

## Architectural & Scene Details
- **Building Facade**:
  - A large, modern multi-story government research building clad in horizontal courses of warm reddish-pink and salmon-toned sandstone with rows of square recessed windows across four visible levels.
  - **Entrance Atrium (Left)**: Rising on the left side of the main wing is a tall, four-story charcoal-gray steel-and-glass curtain-wall atrium entrance. At ground level, a wide brick-paved walkway with low stone walls and metal handrails leads to glass double doors topped by a white canopy bearing two circular government agency seals.
- **Flags & Signage (Center-Left)**:
  - Two tall silver flagpoles stand in front of the building just to the right of the entrance walkway:
    - The left flagpole flies the **United States flag**.
    - The right flagpole flies the **Colorado state flag** (blue-white-blue horizontal stripes with a red `C` enclosing a golden disk).
  - At the base of the flagpoles, in front of dark green bushes, is a low red-sandstone monument sign featuring the circular blue-and-white **NOAA** emblem followed by large silver capital letters reading `NOAA`.
- **Foreground & Sky**: An asphalt driveway and parking area run across the bottom right, while a deep blue sky with faintwispy clouds spans the top of the frame.
