# Red Bug Nymph on a Sidewalk in Dover, NH (`bug-in-dover.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up macro photograph of a small red insect nymph crawling across a textured concrete or asphalt sidewalk in Dover, New Hampshire, taken on September 11, 2010, and posted in an October 9, 2010 blog entry comparing red insects seen in the Seacoast area.

## Scene Description
- **Subject (Insect)**: Positioned near the center of the frame and facing toward the upper right is a wingless (nymph stage) true bug (order Hemiptera, likely a red-shouldered bug or boxelder bug nymph). It has a plump, oval, bright crimson-red abdomen with a small paler orange-red spot near the center-rear. Its thorax and head are a darker brownish-red with dark wing pads developing along the sides of the thorax. Six dark brown/black jointed legs splay outward across the stone grains, and two long, segmented dark antennae extend forward from its head toward the upper right and right.
- **Background Surface**: The sidewalk surface consists of coarse, weathered aggregate packed with small, sub-angular mineral grains in shades of grey, translucent white quartz, tan, and dark charcoal.
