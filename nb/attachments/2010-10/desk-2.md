# School Desk Concept Design: Top-Down Annotated View (`desk-2.jpg`)

## Overview
An iPhone close-up photograph of a hand-drawn top-down (plan) diagram from Kurt Schwehr's 1996 Mechanical Engineering 101 final project, illustrating how multiple trapezoidal wraparound school desks fit together into a collaborative semi-circle.

## Diagram Details & Visible Annotations
Drawn in dark ink on white sketchbook paper, the diagram shows one complete central desk flanked on the left and right by the outlines of two adjacent desks angled to form a curved arc:
- **Central Desk**:
  - **Front/Outer Edge**: A double-lined raised lip runs along the outer three sides of the desk, labeled at the top left with an arrow reading `RAISED`.
  - **Center Work Surface**: A square recessed panel in the middle of the desk is drawn with four corner screw/fastener dots and diagonal shading lines indicating glazing, with a smaller rectangular sub-panel (`CALC` / display) inside. An arrow at the top points to this area with the label `TRANSPARENT COVER`.
  - **Left Wing Compartment**: The angled left arm of the desk is labeled at the bottom left with an arrow reading `BACKPACK`. Where the left wing meets the adjacent desk on the left, two arrows point to the shared boundary with the note `EASY TO COLABORATE HERE`.
  - **Right Wing Compartment**: The angled right arm of the desk is labeled at the bottom right with an arrow reading `BOOKS ETC.`.
  - **Seating**: Centered between the two side wings is a square cushion with diagonal creases meeting at a central button, labeled at the bottom with an arrow reading `PILLOW`.
- **Semi-Circle Geometry Note**: On the right side, an arrow points to the angled wedge gap/joint between the central desk and the neighboring desk on the right, accompanied by the handwritten explanation:
  `ANGLE DETURMINES # OF DESKS CAN HAVE IN A SEMI-CIRCLE`
