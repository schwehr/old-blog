# Kurt Schwehr and Val Schmidt Explaining a Global Marine Gravity Map (`know-the-coast-kurt-val.png`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph taken during the "Know the Coast" open house at UNH's Chase Ocean Engineering Laboratory, showing Val Schmidt and Kurt Schwehr explaining a large wall-mounted global marine gravity and predicted bathymetry map to a visitor.

## Scene Description
- **People**:
  - On the left, an older woman with short wavy grey-blonde hair and glasses stands in profile facing right toward the map. She wears a white t-shirt, blue jeans, and holds a light-blue denim jacket over her left arm.
  - Behind her in the open doorway stands Kurt Schwehr, wearing glasses, a dark grey cap, a blue CCOM polo shirt, and a blue name badge on a lanyard, gesturing with his left hand as he looks toward the map.
  - On the right, Val Schmidt stands with his back to the camera, pointing at the right side of the wall map. He wears a grey baseball cap and a navy-blue long-sleeve shirt featuring a large circular white and blue logo on the back.
- **Logo & Visible Text**:
  - The circular logo on the back of Val's shirt reads **`CCOM JHC`** in bold white letters over a blue graphic, bordered by curved text reading **`Center for Coastal & Ocean Mapping • Joint Hydrographic Center • University of New Hampshire`**.
- **Wall Map**:
  - Mounted on the dark blue wall to the right is a large-format color poster showing global satellite-derived marine gravity anomalies / predicted seafloor topography.
  - Landmasses (including Western Europe, Africa, and South America/North America margins) are rendered in neutral grey, while the Atlantic and Pacific oceans are vividly colored in a rainbow false-color palette (deep blues for abyssal plains/trenches, greens and yellows for mid-ocean slopes, and bright oranges, reds, and magentas along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, fracture zones, and continental shelves).
