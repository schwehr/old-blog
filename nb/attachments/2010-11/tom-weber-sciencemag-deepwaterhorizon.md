# 3D Fledermaus MidWater Acoustic Visualization of the Macondo Wellhead (`tom-weber-sciencemag-deepwaterhorizon.jpg`)

## Overview
A 3D Fledermaus visualization featured in *Science* (*ScienceNOW*, "Listening for Oil Spills," November 2010) created by acoustician Thomas Weber (UNH CCOM) and NOAA colleagues during the Deepwater Horizon response, combining shaded seafloor bathymetry around the Macondo wellhead with an 18 kHz water-column acoustic backscatter curtain and 3D point clouds of natural gas/oil seeps.

## Visualization Elements & Labels
- **Seafloor Bathymetry**: A gray shaded-relief 3D surface representing the Gulf of Mexico seafloor terrain, exhibiting undulating ridges, basins, and pockmarks against a black background.
- **Macondo Wellhead**: A tall, vertical bright-red cylinder rises straight up from the central seafloor depression, labeled in bold white text at its base: **`Macondo Wellhead`**.
- **18 kHz Acoustic Curtain**:
  - Behind the red cylinder, a vertical ribbon/curtain of water-column sonar data curves across the seafloor along the survey ship's track, bordered at the bottom by a thin red seafloor-detection line.
  - The curtain is colored in shades of light blue and cyan with a prominent horizontal bright-green band near the upper-middle portion.
  - Labeled across the upper center in white text: **`18 kHz Acoustic Data`**.
  - At upper left, bold white text **`Deep Scattering Layer`** with a white arrow points directly to the horizontal green acoustic scattering layer in the upper water column.
  - At upper right, bold white text **`Natural Seeps`** with a long white arrow points down-left to vertical blue/cyan acoustic plumes rising from the seabed inside the right portion of the curtain.
- **3D Point Clouds of Natural Seeps**:
  - On the left and lower-center foreground of the gray bathymetry, four vertical white speckled columns (3D point clouds) rise upward from the seafloor.
  - A two-line caption in bold white text at the bottom center reads: **`White point clouds represent natural seeps detected in the acoustic data (not all are shown).`**
