# Side-by-Side Comparison of JEL Camera Rotation Adjustment (`jel-camera-rotation.jpg`)

## Overview
A side-by-side composite photograph comparing the view before and after rotating the rooftop camera mount by one notch to the left at the University of New Hampshire's **Jackson Estuarine Laboratory (JEL)** to reduce sun glare while keeping the StarDot camera housing and pier in frame. The image illustrates a December 11, 2010 blog post titled *"Stardot Camera at Jackson Lab"*.

## Left vs. Right Comparison
- **Left Panel (Before Rotation)**:
  - Taken from just above and behind the white weatherproof StarDot camera housing (visible in the lower-left foreground pointing toward the water).
  - The JEL concrete pier, with its small davit crane at the end and stacks of square and circular aquaculture tanks at its base, is positioned near the center-right of the frame. The floating dock and boats have been removed for the winter.
  - Across the calm, glassy water of the estuary (with thin patches of early winter ice drifting on the surface), the wooded shoreline of Adams Point / Great Bay reflects darkly in the water beneath an overcast, brightly lit gray sky on the right that was causing glare issues.
- **Right Panel (After One-Notch Left Rotation)**:
  - The camera stand has been rotated one notch counterclockwise (to the left), shifting the field of view slightly northward/leftward.
  - The white top of the StarDot camera enclosure now sits centered in the bottom foreground, and the JEL pier and aquaculture tanks appear closer to the right edge of the frame, reducing exposure to direct southern/western sky glare while preserving a clear view of the pier and estuary.
