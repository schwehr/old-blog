# USCG NAIS Vessel Traffic Coverage Map Around Alaska (`nais-arctic.png`)

## Overview
A web map screenshot illustrating 24 hours of terrestrial Automatic Identification System (AIS) vessel position reports received by the U.S. Coast Guard Nationwide AIS (NAIS) network around Alaska and the Arctic, posted on December 3, 2010.

## Map & Data Details
- **Geographic Extent**: Shows the state of `Alaska`, neighboring `Yukon` (Canada), the eastern tip of Russia across the Bering Strait, the `Chukchi Sea`, the `Bering Sea`, and the `Gulf of Alaska`.
- **Maritime Boundaries**: Outlined along the Alaskan coastline and offshore islands in concentric colored bands: blue (inner coastal waters), green (contiguous/territorial zone), and orange (outer boundary), enclosed within a broad red Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) / maritime boundary line extending through the Bering Sea, Chukchi Sea, and Gulf of Alaska.
- **AIS Vessel Targets**: White directional triangular/arrow icons outlined in black represent individual vessels reported via NAIS over a 24-hour period:
  - **Northern / Arctic Coast**: No vessel icons appear along the North Slope, Chukchi Sea, or Beaufort Sea, highlighting the lack of terrestrial NAIS receiver coverage in the Arctic.
  - **Southcentral Alaska**: Dense clusters of vessel icons appear in Cook Inlet (leading to `Anchorage`), Prince William Sound, Kodiak Island, and Resurrection Bay.
  - **Southeast Alaska**: Heavy concentrations of vessels run through the Inside Passage from Yakutat and Juneau down to Ketchikan.
  - **Aleutian Islands & Bering Sea**: Scattered vessels appear around the Pribilof Islands, Dutch Harbor / Unalaska (dense circular cluster), Akutan, and the western Aleutians.
- **Interface Controls**: A standard pan-and-zoom control bar appears in the upper left corner with directional arrows and a vertical zoom slider.
