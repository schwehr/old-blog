# Hornblower Hybrid Ferry Power Systems Diagram (`hornblower-hybrid-power-systems.png`)

## Overview
An illustrated infographic diagram titled **"How Power Moves Us"** from Hornblower Cruises & Events (`© 2008 Hornblower Cruises & Events`), explaining the multi-source hybrid energy generation, conversion, storage, and propulsion architecture aboard the *Hornblower Hybrid* ferry in San Francisco Bay.

## Title & Explanatory Text Columns
Across the top in large bold dark-blue sans-serif lettering is the title **`How Power Moves Us`**. Beneath the right side of the title are three text columns:

1. **`ALTERNATIVE ENERGY`**
   > "Similar to popular hybrid vehicles, this vessel uses power generated by wind, solar and electricity with some fuel, but far less than other boats. By testing these technologies on this boat, we'll learn how to replicate them on bigger vessels that use large amounts of fuel."
2. **`CONVERTING RESOURCES`**
   > "This vessel's wind turbines and solar panels generate direct current power (DC), most of which is inverted to create alternating current power (AC). Additional AC power is supplied by state-of-the-art diesel generators and clean, local energy from the utility grid."
3. **`USING POWER`**
   > "The direct current (DC) is used to power the navigational equipment and small LED lighting (Light Emitting Diodes), while the alternating current (AC) is used for low power LED lighting, monitors and the electric motors that propel the boat."

## Power Sources & Vessel Cutaway Flowchart
On the left side, four primary energy inputs feed via orange arrows into a green cutaway profile of the vessel sitting in blue water:

### External Energy Inputs (Top Left)
- **`DIESEL`**: Illustrated by a brown oil droplet icon. An orange line leads down to the **`EPA CLEAN GENERATORS`** cylinder in the lower hull.
- **`ELECTRICITY`**: Illustrated by a yellow-orange lightning bolt and a two-prong electrical plug icon. An orange line leads directly to the **`SWITCHBOARD`** inside the hull.
- **`WIND`**: Illustrated by a blue-and-white swirling wind icon above a vertical-axis lattice **`WIND TURBINES`** tower mounted on the top deck, feeding into a **`CONTROLLER`**.
- **`SUN`**: Illustrated by a glowing yellow-orange sun icon above angled blue **`PV SOLAR PANELS`** mounted on the top deck, feeding into a second **`CONTROLLER`**.

### Internal Electrical & Propulsion Components (Inside Green Hull)
- **`CONTROLLER` (x2)**: Two blue rounded rectangular blocks at the top of the hull receiving power from the wind turbines and PV solar panels, directing power downward with orange arrows to the **`DC BATTERY BANK`**.
- **`NAVIGATION`**: An orange rectangular box with three round dial gauges on the left (bow side), powered by an orange arrow from the **`DC BATTERY BANK`**.
- **`DC BATTERY BANK`**: Three blue 3D battery blocks connected to the controllers, navigation instruments, **`INVERTER`**, and **`CHARGERS`**.
- **`INVERTER`**: A blue rounded block at the upper right of the system flow, receiving DC power and outputting to **`LED LIGHTS`** (illustrated by an array of yellow/white dots) and **`MONITORS`** (a dark screen showing the Hornblower Hybrid logo).
- **`CHARGERS`**: A blue rounded block positioned between the **`INVERTER`** and **`SWITCHBOARD`**, exchanging power with both the **`DC BATTERY BANK`** and **`PROPULSION BATTERY BANK`**.
- **`EPA CLEAN GENERATORS`**: A dark blue horizontal cylinder at the bottom left, sending power to the **`SWITCHBOARD`**.
- **`SWITCHBOARD`**: A central blue rounded block routing power between the shore electricity plug, clean generators, chargers, and **`VFDs`**.
- **`PROPULSION BATTERY BANK`**: Three blue 3D battery blocks near the stern (below a small green pennant flag on the stern rail), connected to the **`CHARGERS`** and **`VFDs`**.
- **`VFDs (Variable Frequency Drives)`**: A blue rounded block at the bottom center receiving power from the switchboard and propulsion battery bank, feeding rightward into the **`ELECTRIC MOTORS`**.
- **`ELECTRIC MOTORS`**: An orange-gold horizontal cylinder in the stern driving a shaft connected to a four-bladed bronze propeller extending into the water.
