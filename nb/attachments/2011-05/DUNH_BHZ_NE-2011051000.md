# UNH Seismometer Broadband Helicorder Plot (`DUNH_BHZ_NE-2011051000.png`)

## Overview
A 24-hour seismic helicorder plot displaying broadband vertical-component (`BHZ`) waveform data recorded on May 10, 2011, by the newly installed New England Seismic Network (`NE`) station `DUNH` at the University of New Hampshire in Durham, New Hampshire (located in the basement of James Hall).

## Plot Layout & Axes
- **Header (Top Left)**:
  - `May10,2011`
  - `DUNH BHZ NE --`
  - `(UNH - Durham, NH [Vertical | Broadband])`
- **Vertical Axes (Hours)**:
  - **Left Axis (`EDT`)**: Labels each horizontal trace in Eastern Daylight Time from `20:00` (top trace, unlabeled just below `EDT`), `21:00`, `22:00`, `23:00`, `00:00` through `19:00` at the bottom.
  - **Right Axis (`UTC`)**: Labels corresponding Coordinated Universal Time hours from `01:00` (top trace, below `UTC`), `02:00`, `03:00` through `00:00` at the bottom.
- **Horizontal Axis (`TIME (MINUTES)`)**:
  - Spans `00` to `60` minutes across each 1-hour horizontal trace, with major vertical grid lines every 5 minutes (`00`, `05`, `10`, `15`, `20`, `25`, `30`, `35`, `40`, `45`, `50`, `55`, `60`) and minor tick marks every 1 minute along the bottom border.
- **Footer Scale Notes**:
  - Bottom left: `Each Vertical Division = 50.00 microvolts`
  - Bottom right: `Traces clipped at plus/minus 10 vertical divisions`

## Waveform Details
- The 24 hourly traces cycle through four colors from top to bottom: black, red, blue, and dark green.
- Continuous high-frequency background noise (from building activity and nearby trains) appears as a steady band of oscillations along all 24 traces.
- A prominent teleseismic earthquake event begins around `04:55` EDT (`09:55` UTC) on the red `04:00` EDT (`09:00` UTC) trace, building into large-amplitude surface waves between `06:00` and `06:20` EDT (`11:00`–`11:20` UTC) on the blue `06:00` EDT (`11:00` UTC) trace, where vertical excursions span roughly 15–20 vertical divisions and overlap several adjacent hourly traces above and below.
- A smaller burst of elevated amplitude is also visible on the red `21:00` EDT (`02:00` UTC) trace around the 19–23 minute mark.
