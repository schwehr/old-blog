# UNH Seismometer High-Pass Filtered Helicorder Plot (`DUNH_SHZ_NE-2011051000.png`)

## Overview
A 24-hour seismic helicorder plot showing short-period / high-pass filtered vertical-component (`SHZ`) waveform data from May 10, 2011, at station `DUNH` (`NE` network) in the basement of James Hall at the University of New Hampshire in Durham, New Hampshire. Because of the high-sensitivity scale (`2.50 microvolts` per vertical division) and high-pass filter, the plot is heavily saturated by local cultural noise from the building and passing trains.

## Plot Layout & Axes
- **Header (Top Left)**:
  - `May10,2011`
  - `DUNH SHZ NE --`
  - `(UNH - Durham, NH [Vertical | High-Pass])` (partially obscured by overlapping black and red waveform spikes at the top of the plot).
- **Vertical Axes (Hours)**:
  - **Left Axis (`EDT`)**: Hourly traces labeled from `21:00` down through `00:00` to `19:00`.
  - **Right Axis (`UTC`)**: Corresponding UTC hours labeled from `02:00` down to `00:00`.
- **Horizontal Axis (`TIME (MINUTES)`)**:
  - Marked from `00` to `60` minutes in 5-minute intervals with vertical reference lines and 1-minute bottom ticks.
- **Footer Scale Notes**:
  - Bottom left: `Each Vertical Division = 2.50 microvolts`
  - Bottom right: `Traces clipped at plus/minus 10 vertical divisions`

## Waveform Details
- The four-color hourly traces (black, red, blue, green) overlap densely across the entire 24-hour chart due to high-frequency ambient noise in the building.
- Several intense, solid-red vertical bands of clipped high-frequency energy appear on the `00:00` EDT (`05:00` UTC), `01:00` EDT (`06:00` UTC), and `04:00` EDT (`09:00` UTC) traces—especially around minutes 10–14 and 20–26—extending to the top edge of the image.
- During daytime working hours (`07:00` to `18:00` EDT), continuous overlapping spikes in black, red, blue, and green reflect heightened building activity and local traffic/train vibrations.
