# Palm Pilot Screen Showing Seismometer Jump Test Waveform (`dunh-seismometer-jumptest.jpg`)

## Overview
A close-up photograph of a Palm Pilot PDA in a blue ruggedized case displaying a real-time seismic waveform recorded during a "jump test" in the basement of James Hall at the University of New Hampshire in May 2011, verifying that the newly installed seismometer sensor was responding to ground motion.

## Device & Screen Details
- **Hardware**:
  - A handheld Palm OS device enclosed in a bright blue contoured plastic shell, held in a person's left hand.
  - Below the square monochrome LCD screen are six physical buttons: a power button on the left (`⏻`), a four-way directional pad (up, down, left, right arrows) with a central select button, and a downward-pointing triangle button on the right.
  - Below the main display is the classic Palm Graffiti handwriting input area flanked by four circular silk-screened icons: Home (top left), Menu (bottom left), Applications launcher grid (top right), and Find/magnifying glass (bottom right).
- **Displayed Waveform & Interface Text**:
  - **Top Status Bar**: Displays `Midpt: 18103` on the left and `Range: 2175` on the right.
  - **Center Plot Area**: A horizontal baseline runs across the middle of the screen with a sharp, high-amplitude bipolar spike near the center (representing the impulse from someone jumping on the floor), preceded and followed by smaller oscillations.
  - **Bottom Controls**:
    - Left labels: `Str: 1` stacked above `Ch: 1` (Stream 1, Channel 1).
    - Three rectangular on-screen buttons across the bottom of the plot area: `[Update]`, `[Ch.Sel]`, and `[Done]`.
- **Reflections & Background**:
  - The reflection of the photographer's hands holding a smartphone/camera is faintly visible on the glass LCD screen.
  - In the upper right background, coiled gray cables rest on the wooden shelf of the seismometer cabinet.
