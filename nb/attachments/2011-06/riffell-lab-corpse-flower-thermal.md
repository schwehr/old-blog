# Thermal Infrared Image of a Blooming Corpse Flower (`riffell-lab-corpse-flower-thermal.png`)

## Overview
A close-up false-color thermal infrared image of the blooming corpse flower (*Amorphophallus titanum*) captured by the Riffell Lab at the University of Washington in June 2011, illustrating thermogenesis (heat production) along the rim of the plant's spathe to volatilize and disperse its scent chemicals.

## Thermal Pattern & Colors
- **Hot Spot (Red, Orange, and Yellow)**: A prominent crescent-shaped band of high temperature arches across the center of the frame, colored in bright yellow, orange, and deep red hotspots. The warmest red and orange core is concentrated along the upper-middle and left-curving edge of the ruffled spathe rim.
- **Intermediate Temperatures (Green and Teal)**: Surrounding the bright yellow/orange crescent is a halo of vivid green and blue-green (teal) representing intermediate surface temperatures across the body of the spathe.
- **Cool Background & Ribs (Blue)**: In the upper left and upper center, radially fanning stripes of dark blue and cyan correspond to the cooler pleated ribs of the spathe and the cooler ambient greenhouse background.
