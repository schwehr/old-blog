# Composite Photograph of a Blooming Corpse Flower (*Amorphophallus titanum*) Sampled by the Riffell Lab (`riffell-lab-corpse-flower.jpg`)

## Overview
A three-panel horizontal composite photograph showing a blooming giant "corpse flower" (*Amorphophallus titanum*) in the University of Washington Biology Department Botanical Garden Greenhouse in June 2011, where researchers from Professor Jeff Riffell's lab sampled the plant's pungent scent chemicals.

## Panel Descriptions
- **Left Panel (Researcher Sampling the Bloom)**: Shows a side view of the large corpse flower in the greenhouse during daytime, with glass roof panes and lush green tropical foliage in the background. The plant features a tall, pale yellowish-green central spadix rising out of a ribbed, pale green outer spathe with a ruffled, dark maroon/burgundy inner rim. Standing to the right of the plant in profile is a researcher (Riffell Lab graduate student Kelsey Byers) with brown hair tied back, wearing a dark navy short-sleeved shirt, gray trousers, and a wristwatch on her left wrist, holding a small sampling probe or tube up to the rim of the spathe.
- **Center Panel (Full Bloom at Night)**: Shows the corpse flower against a dark nighttime background inside the greenhouse. The pleated, funnel-shaped spathe is flared open, displaying its vertical pale-green ribs on the exterior, dark maroon frilled margin at the top, and a mottled medium-green base supported by a dark green stalk. Thin light-blue sampling tubes curve down along the right side of the plant.
- **Right Panel (Close-Up of Cutaway Window into the Base)**: A detailed close-up view of a rectangular cutaway window excised in the thick, fleshy green wall of the lower spathe. Visible inside the chamber at the base of the spadix are tightly packed rows of bright reddish-orange female flowers (pistillate zone) below a band of small, bead-like pale yellow/cream male flowers (staminate zone) and the swollen, cream-colored lower rim of the central spadix above.
