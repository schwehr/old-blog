# FEMA National Response Coordination Center (`fema-national-response-coordination-center-50331.jpg`)

## Overview
An indoor photograph of FEMA's National Response Coordination Center (NRCC) during Hurricane Irene operations in August 2011, showing emergency management personnel working at cubicles and monitoring wall-mounted news and status displays.

## Scene Description
- **Workstations & Personnel**:
  - The room is arranged with rows of low-walled gray fabric cubicles with light wood desktops, black flat-screen computer monitors, keyboards, mice, and multi-line desk telephones.
  - Several staff members are seated at their computers or standing in the aisles. Many wear blue vests over their shirts or drape them over the backs of their chairs to identify their operational roles.
  - In the center-left aisle, a woman in dark clothing stands holding documents while speaking toward seated colleagues wearing blue vests. To the right of the aisle, two men in blue vests sit at an elevated desk counter.
- **Signage & Vest Labels**:
  - Chair-back vests in the foreground and middle ground display white title patches, including `"Technical Specialist (TSA)"` on a blue vest at right-center and `"TECHNICAL"` at the bottom right.
  - Cubicle nameplates and section signs are mounted along the partitions, including `"MITIGATION"` and `"Action Planning"` in the left-center foreground.
  - Charts, phone lists, and reference sheets are taped to the interior glass/plastic panels of the foreground cubicles.
- **Wall Displays & Background**:
  - Centered on the far back wall is a large, glowing blue rectangular sign featuring the white circular Department of Homeland Security seal and bold white text reading `"FEMA"`. To the left of the sign is a wall-mounted map next to double wooden doors.
  - On the upper right wall are two large flat-panel television monitors:
    - The left monitor shows a status briefing slide titled `"HURRICANE IRENE"` with a satellite/radar map on the left and red horizontal bullet banners on the right.
    - The right monitor displays a live television broadcast (showing the Washington Monument against a blue sky) with a news ticker at the bottom reading `"EARTHQUAKE CAUSES CRACKS IN MONUMENT / Engineers assessing damage now"`.
