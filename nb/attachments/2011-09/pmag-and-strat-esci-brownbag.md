# Ardath Shale Soft-Sediment Slump Fold (`pmag-and-strat-esci-brownbag.jpg`)

## Overview
A golden-hour coastal geological photograph showing a well-exposed soft-sediment deformation slump fold in the Eocene Ardath Shale along the beach cliffs near Scripps Institution of Oceanography in La Jolla, California, used to illustrate a UNH Earth Science Brown Bag seminar on paleomagnetic and stratigraphic techniques for identifying sediment processes on continental slopes.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground Outcrop & Slump Structure**:
  - Rising directly from the smooth, wet, tan beach sand in the center-left foreground is a prominent rocky outcrop illuminated by warm, low-angle orange sunset light.
  - The lower half of the outcrop displays tightly curved, concentric, U-shaped recumbent fold laminae (a classic soft-sediment slump fold or convolute bedding structure) etched in relief by wave erosion.
  - Capping the folded layer is a more massive, blocky, horizontally jointed sandstone/siltstone ledge with vertical fractures and dark shadows on its right side.
  - In the lower-right corner, a smaller layered rock boulder with greenish algae staining along its base juts into the frame.
- **Background Coastline**:
  - To the left, gentle ocean surf and whitewater wash onto the beach, with a distant headland silhouette visible across the bay on the horizon.
  - To the upper right, tall coastal bluffs extend into the distance, glowing warm orange-pink in the late afternoon sun, with cliffside houses and trees perched along the top edge under a pale, clear sky.
