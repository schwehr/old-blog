# Touring the RMS Titanic Prow in Google Earth (`titanic-tour.png`)

## Overview
A screenshot of the **Google Earth** 3D underwater viewport during the **Titanic Tour**, showing a close-up view of the *RMS Titanic* 3D bow model alongside an open popup balloon titled **Titanic Prow** featuring an iconic National Geographic photograph by Emory Kristof.

## 3D Underwater Scene
- **Titanic Bow 3D Model**:
  - Occupying the left half of the frame, the detailed 3D photogrammetric/textured model of the *Titanic*'s bow section is viewed from off the starboard bow.
  - Visible features include the sharply pointed prow, the white-lined forecastle deck railings, the fallen forward mast sloping diagonally down across the superstructure toward the deck, open bridge/superstructure windows, and dark rusticles cascading down the hull plating.
- **Seafloor & Ocean Surface**:
  - The ship rests in dark blue water with a gently mounded bathymetric ridge visible in the background beneath the darker blue ceiling of the ocean surface.

## Titanic Prow Information Balloon
A white rounded popup card on the right points directly to the tip of the ship's bow:
- **Title**: `Titanic Prow`
- **Subtitle Link**: `National Geographic: Emory Kristof`
- **Photograph**: The famous underwater photograph showing the rusted prow and encrusted port/starboard railings of the *RMS Titanic* emerging from the abyssal darkness, illuminated by submersible lights, with the yellow National Geographic border logo in the lower right corner.
- **Caption Text**:
  > Rusted prow of the RMS *Titanic* ocean liner, sunk in the North Atlantic Ocean, 563 kilometers (350 miles) off Newfoundland after hitting an iceberg on the night of April 14, 1912. The ship was located in 1985 by a joint American-French expedition led by Robert Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel.
