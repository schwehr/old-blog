# First Hazcam Image of Mars from MSL Curiosity Rover (`mars.jpg`)

## Overview
A `256 × 256` grayscale fisheye photograph captured by one of the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) *Curiosity* rover's Hazard Avoidance Cameras (Hazcams) moments after touchdown in Gale Crater on August 5, 2012 PDT (August 6, 2012 UTC), with the clear dust cover still in place.

## Visual Details
- **Circular Fisheye Framing**: The wide-angle Hazcam lens produces a circular field of view surrounded by black vignetting in all four corners, with mechanical fixtures of the rover body protruding into the left, bottom-left, and right edges of the frame.
- **Martian Surface & Horizon**:
  - Across the center of the frame stretches the flat, pebble-strewn floor of Gale Crater, textured with fine dust and small scattered rocks.
  - The gently curving Martian horizon divides the dark gray ground from the bright sky above.
- **Solar Glare & Dust Particles**:
  - The low sun shines brightly at the top center of the frame, creating a strong white glare that illuminates specks of Martian dust clinging to the transparent protective lens cover from the sky-crane landing kick-up.
- **Rover Wheel (Bottom Right)**:
  - In the lower-right quadrant sits one of *Curiosity*'s six aluminum wheels in shadow, with its distinctive bright zigzag grouser/tread pattern clearly visible on top of the wheel.
