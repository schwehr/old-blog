# JPL Space Flight Operations Facility / Mission Control (`msl-aug04-2-mission-control.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph taken inside the Space Flight Operations Facility (Mission Control) at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Pasadena, California, on August 4, 2012—one day before the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) *Curiosity* rover landing. The image shows rows of flight controller consoles, multi-monitor workstations, and a massive overhead display wall tracking Deep Space Network (DSN) antennas and mission status.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground & Consoles**: Several curved rows of mission control consoles run diagonally from the lower left toward the right. In the immediate foreground on the right is the "Center of the Universe" seal/console border illuminated with bright blue accent lighting. Two personnel in dark shirts are seated at workstations in the middle row, facing banks of LCD monitors displaying telemetry, tables, and graphs. A third person stands near the left edge wearing a headset.
- **Background Viewing Gallery & Signage**: Behind the main floor, a glass-partitioned gallery area is visible, featuring a large illuminated NASA meatball logo alongside glowing yellow-orange text reading **"Jet Propulsion Laboratory / California Institute of Technology"**.
- **Clocks & Countdown Displays**: To the right of the glass partition, a red digital LED clock displays `15:35`, while a larger dark digital status board further right lists:
  - `UTC` / `217:15:35:17`
  - `MSL LANDING` / `-001:13:56:40`
  - `8 YEARS OPPORTUNITY` / `+193:10:20:17`

## Overhead Display Wall
Across the top of the room, a continuous horizontal projection wall is divided into multiple status panels (from left to right):
1. **Timeline Bar Chart (Far Left)**: Horizontal color-coded schedule bars in green, purple, and blue against a white and dark grid.
2. **Deep Space Network (DSN) Antenna Status**: A dark blue diagram showing icons of parabolic dish antennas arranged by complex (Goldstone, Canberra, Madrid) with animated signal waves (green and white) indicating active uplinks and downlinks. A smaller monitor mounted on the left console repeats this DSN view.
3. **Live Camera Feed**: A bright, slightly overexposed daytime outdoor feed showing a tall vertical structure or tower against green hills.
4. **Telemetry & Schedule Tables**: Green and cyan tabular text on a dark blue background listing spacecraft IDs (`S/C`), antenna numbers (`ANT`), `START` and `END` times, and tracking pass data for missions including `MSL`, `MER1`, `VGR1`, `VGR2`, `CAS`, `DAWN`, and `GRAIL`.
5. **70-Meter Antenna Graphic (Far Right)**: A photograph of a large DSN parabolic dish illuminated at night, titled **"70 - Meter Antenna"** at the top left, with a yellow inset diagram showing the scale and structure of the dish.
