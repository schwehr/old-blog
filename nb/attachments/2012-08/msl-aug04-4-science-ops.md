# MSL Science Operations / Flight Support Room (`msl-aug04-4-science-ops.jpg`)

## Overview
A photograph of an unoccupied operations and engineering support room at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) on the morning of August 4, 2012, one day prior to the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) *Curiosity* rover landing. The room is filled with rows of cubicles, dual-monitor workstations, voice communication panels, and a wall-mounted countdown display.

## Scene Description
- **Workstations & Seating**: Several parallel rows of light-colored desks separated by low fabric-covered partition walls occupy the room. Maroon/burgundy ergonomic office chairs sit in front of the desks. Each station is equipped with black flat-panel LCD monitors, Cisco IP telephones, multi-channel digital voice intercom units (with glowing white LCD screens), and black mesh pen cups holding yellow pencils. White three-ring binders rest on the desk surfaces.
- **Console Position Signs**: Black rectangular placards are mounted atop the monitors to identify specific engineering and operations roles. Each sign features the NASA meatball logo on the left, white text in the center, and the red JPL logo on the right. Legible signs include:
  - **`Flight Software`** (foreground right)
  - **`Surf Attitude P&P`** (foreground left)
  - **`Payload U/L Coordinator`** (second row left)
  - **`Thermal`** (second row center-left)
  - **`Flight Director`** (back row center)
  - **`ACS`** (Attitude Control System, back row center-right)
  - **`Tactical Uplink Lead`** (back row right)
  - **`Systems`** (middle row far right)
- **Walls, Whiteboards & Clocks**: Large whiteboards line the back and right walls below dark window blinds. On the left pillar is a vertical poster featuring the NASA logo and reading **"Jet Propulsion Laboratory / California Institute of Technology"**. On the back wall to the right, a red digital LED clock displays `217 15:39:14`, mounted above a large flat-screen monitor showing white, yellow, and green countdown text on a black background (`2012-217 15:46:18 UTC`, `2012 08:46:18 PDT`, and `Landing: -001 13:45:32`).
