# Whale Alert System Diagram by Michelle Wray (`michelle_wray_whale_alert.jpg`)

## Overview
A hand-drawn cartoon infographic illustrated by Michelle Wray (`michw.com` / *Deep Sea News*) explaining the 3-step workflow of the **Whale Alert** acoustic detection and ship-notification system used to protect endangered North Atlantic right whales. Featured in the March 3, 2013 blog post *"MichelleW post on Whale Alert"*.

## Diagram Steps & Visual Elements
- **Step 1 (Top-Left, on a turquoise ocean background)**:
  - Marked with an orange circle numbered `1`.
  - A small black whale icon with a dashed swimming trail moves into the top circle of a chain of three overlapping light-blue dashed circles, each representing a `5-mile listening radius`.
  - Curved acoustic sound waves radiate from the whale toward a triggered red center circle (`buoy picks up the call`), while the two adjacent listening zones have green center circles (`More buoys`).
  - Handwritten labels and arrows read: `Right whale swims by`, `buoy picks up the call`, `5-mile listening radius`, and `More buoys`.
- **Transition from Step 1 to Step 2**:
  - A large yellow lightning-bolt arrow points from the ocean buoys into a tan/sand-colored land circle on the upper right, labeled `Signal transmitted to technician`.
- **Step 2 (Top-Right, on a tan background)**:
  - Marked with an orange circle numbered `2`.
  - Shows a technician with brown hair and a purple shirt sitting in a rolling office chair at a gray desk, typing on a keyboard and looking at a desktop computer monitor displaying a blue acoustic waveform/spectrogram.
  - A white speech bubble from the technician reads: `"Yep, that's a right whale"`.
- **Transition from Step 2 to Step 3**:
  - A second large yellow lightning-bolt arrow curves downward from the technician's desk back toward the water at the bottom, labeled `AIS Message sent to whale Alert App`.
- **Step 3 (Bottom-Left, on the turquoise ocean background)**:
  - Marked with an orange circle numbered `3`.
  - A small brown boat with a tan cabin and mast sits on wavy water lines at the tip of the incoming yellow lightning bolt.
  - Handwritten text to the left of the vessel reads: `Mariners can check their ipad or iphone and learn there's a whale in the area!`.
