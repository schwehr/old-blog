# Mapping Steep Cone Hot Spring in Yellowstone (`1995-yellowstone-steep-cone.jpg`)

## Overview
A color field photograph taken in 1995 showing a researcher standing beside Steep Cone, an active geothermal hot spring in Yellowstone National Park, rigged with a grid of string lines across the vent for spatial mapping.

## Scene Description
- **Foreground (Hot Spring & Mapping Grid)**:
  - A circular geothermal pool sits atop a broad, light gray and white sinter mound (travertine/geyserite apron) with patches of dark brown microbial mat in the lower-left corner.
  - The pool's rim is encrusted with a raised, scalloped border of orange-brown sinter deposits.
  - Inside the rim, the deep blue water is actively boiling at the center, sending up a frothy white fountain of water and a rising plume of white steam.
  - A rectangular grid of taut white strings is stretched across the circular pool, anchored beyond the sinter rim, dividing the vent into square reference sectors for mapping.
- **Midground (Researcher & Gear)**:
  - Standing on the far left edge of the sinter mound where it meets the grass is a person wearing a wide-brimmed hat, a light gray long-sleeved shirt or jacket, and dark pants, looking down toward equipment at their feet.
  - Several dark gear bags, cases, and a blue tarp or pack are laid out along the grassy edge behind the sinter rim.
- **Background**:
  - A flat green and tan meadow stretches behind the hot spring (Sentinel Meadows area).
  - Beyond the meadow rises a dark, tree-covered ridge on the left and distant blue-gray hills on the right under an overcast, cloudy sky.
