# TrailBehind Skipper Website on iPad (`trailbehind-skipper-0.png`)

## Overview
An iPad Safari screenshot showing the homepage of TrailBehind's "Skipper" marine navigation and NOAA charting application (`tryskipper.com/`), captured on July 30, 2013.

## Interface & Browser Chrome
At the top of the screen, the black iOS status bar displays `iPad`, the Wi-Fi icon, `3:18 PM`, and `94%` battery. Below it is the standard iOS 6 Safari navigation bar featuring Back, Forward, Bookmarks, iCloud Tabs, and Share buttons, an address bar showing `tryskipper.com/` with a grey `Reader` button and reload icon, and a `Search` input box on the right. A single browser tab is titled `Skipper | NOAA charts for iPhone and iPad`.

## Webpage Layout & Content
- **Header Bar:** Set against a light grey textured background, the left side features the `SKIPPER` logo in tall, distressed teal-blue uppercase lettering next to an anchor entwined with an orange rope. The right side contains navigation links: `App`, `Charts`, `Login`, and `Support`.
- **Main Hero Section:** On a light speckled off-white background, right-aligned promotional text reads:
  - Heading: `NOAA charts on iPad and iPhone, done right.`
  - Body text: `Skipper is a modern chart-plotter. Powerful mapping and weather tools, with data synced across web and mobile.`
  - Call to action: A black badge reading `Available on the App Store` with an iPhone icon.
- **Product Mockups:** On the left side of the hero section are two black iPads displaying the Skipper app:
  1. **Left iPad (Portrait):** Shows a NOAA nautical chart of `Deer Harbor` and `Fawn I` with depth soundings (`52`, `65`, `63`, `73`, `82`, `16`, `14`), a red navigation track line, and a pink `Cable Area`. An open popover menu on the left shows `NOAA Charts` opacity slider, `Base Maps` (`Global Imagery`, `OpenStreetMap`, `USGS Topo`), and `Map Layers` (`Radar`, `My Waypoints`, `Marinas`, `Anchorages`, `Hazards`, `Local Knowledge`, `Grid Overlay`).
  2. **Right iPad (Landscape, angled):** Shows satellite/hybrid imagery of a coastline with a red route line and a `Weather` panel on the left displaying a 5-day forecast with sun/cloud icons, temperatures (`80°F`), and a tide/current sine-wave graph at the bottom.
