# SRT Marine Technology and exactEarth ABSEA Satellite AIS Diagram (`srt-exactearth-absea.png`)

## Overview
An informational diagram and excerpt from an SRT Marine Technology / exactEarth whitepaper explaining "ABSEA" satellite Automatic Identification System (AIS) tracking technology. The image is featured in a May 26, 2014 blog post titled *"Confusion over SRT and ExactEarth's press release"*, where Kurt Schwehr critiques the lack of technical transparency regarding how ABSEA modifies Class B AIS transmissions for satellite reception.

## Diagram Comparison (Center)
The central graphic compares standard satellite AIS reception (left) with ABSEA-enabled satellite AIS reception (right):
- **Left Illustration (Standard AIS)**:
  - **Sea Surface & Coastline**: A perspective grid represents the ocean surface with a gray landmass/coastline along the back edge. Two large commercial vessels (gray cargo/tanker ships, representing Class A AIS transceivers) are positioned on the left and right, while a small sailboat (representing a small vessel with a Class B transceiver) sits in the center.
  - **Radio Signals**: Strong yellow curved wave arcs radiate upward from both large vessels all the way to an orbiting satellite at the top. Faint, thin blue wave arcs rise from the small central sailboat but fade out midway, failing to reach the satellite.
  - **Satellite & Map Display**: An orbiting satellite in the upper left points via two black arrows to a square chart display. The chart shows the gray coastline at the top and two black triangular vessel icons (the two large Class A ships) on the grid, while the small central vessel is missing.
- **Right Illustration (With ABSEA)**:
  - **Sea Surface & Coastline**: The same perspective grid, coastline, two large vessels, and central small vessel. A callout box points to the small central vessel containing a blue globe/wave icon and the word **`ABSEA`** (with `AB` in black bold text and `SEA` in bright blue bold text).
  - **Radio Signals**: Alongside the yellow wave arcs from the two large vessels, bold cyan/blue wave arcs now reach all the way from the small central vessel to the satellite.
  - **Satellite & Map Display**: The satellite now has three arrows pointing to the chart display (two black arrows and one blue arrow). The chart displays both black vessel triangles plus a third blue triangular vessel icon in the center representing the tracked small craft.

## Visible Text & Bullet Points
- **Top Paragraph**:
  > ABSEA is fully compatible with existing AIS infrastructure and enables vessels to be tracked by both coastal receiver networks and by satellite. Fusion of the data from these sources enables a seamless tracking solution with the benefits of detailed tracking close to shore and regular, reliable reporting beyond the horizon.
- **Middle Paragraph**:
  > ABSEA technology is available for activation in all SRT Marine Technology AIS transceivers including the 'Identifier' AIS tracking beacon which is specifically designed for large scale small vessel tracking applications.
- **Lead-in & Bulleted List**:
  > An ABSEA enabled transceiver can provide:
  > - Regular AIS position reporting (typically every 5 minutes for an 'Identifier') when in range of coastal AIS receivers.
  > - Reliable and regular position reports beyond the horizon.
  > - Flexible vessel track history reporting contained within every satellite position report provides even more information about the vessel behaviour in-between each satellite capture.
