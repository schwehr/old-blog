For the paleomagnetic meeting this morning, Julie has picked this reference:
<pre>
Timing of volcanism along the northern East Pacific Rise based on
paleointensity experiments on basaltic glasses.
Calut, Cormier, Kent, Donnelly, and Langmeir
JGR, 109-B04104
doi:10.1029/2003JB002672
</pre>
First off, I have to say that not doing the volume/issue/page number
thing is idiotic.  Thanks AGU.  Grr.  Some notes from the paper:

<ul>
<li>EPR spreading rates are 80-150mm/yr implying dike injected every
7-13 years if they are about 1m thick or it maybe less frequent events
with many dikes</li>
<li>Lava flows may extended several kilometers from the vent.  Wow.
That is incredible that it can go that distance with cold water right
up against it.</li>
<li>Used the <em>Periodic Table X</em> software to lookup what Po is: 84
polonium 210.  Was mentioned in the paper for Po-Pb dating of fresh
lavas.</li>
<li>Using sedimentation over a lava flow is bad in that the
sedimentation rate is dependent on hydrothermal and tectonic activity
- why tectonic activity?</li>
<li>Paleointensity from fresh MORB eruptions give zero age test
determination on the order of 1&micro;T.  That would be very useful if
the results are of this quality.  This is from averaging many samples
from one flow.</li>
<li>Found that the centers of the ridge segments tend to have younger
flows than the ends.</li>
<li>This study does not actually do transects of glass samples.</li>
<li>Study area was chosen due to a big seismic experiment here to map
layer 2A.</li>
<li>Goal to comparare north and south segments.  The northern segment
is an unusual inflated zone with a wide central valley. </li>
</ul>
