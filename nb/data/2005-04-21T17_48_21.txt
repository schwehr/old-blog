Just yesterday, I noticed python's doctest module and realized what a
really excellent idea it is.  It is the perfect compliment to pyunit.
The idea is that you put examples with answers in your module's doc
strings.  doctest then goes in, finds these examples, and verifies
that they work as advertized.  Here is a super small example to get
started with that I call dc1.py:
<pre>
<font color="#444444">#!/usr/bin/env python</font>

<font color="#2040a0">def</font> <font color="#2040a0">square</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="#2040a0">x</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">:</font>
    <font color="#008000">&quot;&quot;</font><font color="#008000">&quot;
    &gt;&gt;&gt; square(2)
    4
    &quot;</font><font color="#008000">&quot;&quot;</font>
    <strong>return</strong> <font color="#2040a0">x</font><font color="4444FF">*</font><font color="#2040a0">x</font>

<font color="#2040a0">def</font> <font color="#2040a0">_test</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">:</font>
    <strong>import</strong> <font color="#2040a0">doctest</font>
    <font color="#2040a0">doctest</font>.<font color="#2040a0">testmod</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="4444FF">)</font>

<strong>if</strong> <font color="#2040a0">__name__</font> <font color="4444FF">=</font><font color="4444FF">=</font> <font color="#008000">&quot;__main__&quot;</font><font color="4444FF">:</font>
    <font color="#2040a0">_test</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="4444FF">)</font>
</pre>
Then run it like this:
<pre>
  chmod +x dc1.py
  ./dc1.py -v
</pre>
The -v is for verbose mode.  Here is what verbose mode should give you
showing that everything is all happy:
<pre>
  Trying:
      square(2)
  Expecting:
      4
  ok
  2 items had no tests:
      __main__
      __main__._test
  1 items passed all tests:
     1 tests in __main__.square
  1 tests in 3 items.
  1 passed and 0 failed.
  Test passed.
</pre>
To learn more <a
href="http://docs.python.org/lib/module-doctest.html">read on
here</a>
