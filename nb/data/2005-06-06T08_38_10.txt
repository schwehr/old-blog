<a

Colorado Mountain Aquifers Throw Water On 'Teflon Basin' Myth</a>:
<pre>
  New research shows that high-altitude aquifers honeycomb parts of
  the Colorado Rockies, trapping snow melt and debunking the myth that
  high mountain valleys act as "Teflon basins" to rush water
  downstream.

  Scientist Mark Williams of the University of Colorado at Boulder
  conducted geochemical studies that show that less than half of the
  annual snow melt in the Green Lakes Valley in the mountains west of
  Boulder arrives at a downstream watershed treatment facility as "new
  water." He found that most of the water sampled from North Boulder
  Creek during runoff months was "old groundwater" that had been
  stored in subterranean mountain catchments.
