<pre> 
  ARCHAEOLOGISTS have solved one of the greatest mysteries of
  Stonehenge - the exact spot from where its huge stones were
  quarried. 

  A team has pinpointed the precise place in Wales from where the
  bluestones were removed in about 2500 BC. 

  It found the small crag-edged enclosure at one of the highest points
  of the 1,008ft high Carn Menyn mountain in Pembrokeshire's Preseli
  Hills. 

  The enclosure is just over one acre in size but, according to team
  leader Professor Tim Darvill, it provides a veritable "Aladdin's
  Cave" of made-to-measure pillars for aspiring circle
  builders. Within and outside the enclosure are numerous prone pillar
  stones with clear signs of working. Some are fairly recent and a
  handful of drill holes attest to the technology used. Other blocks
  may have been wrenched from the ground or the crags in ancient
  times. 

  They were then moved 240 miles to the famous site at Salisbury
  Plain, Wiltshire.
