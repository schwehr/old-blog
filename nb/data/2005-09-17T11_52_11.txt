TITLE: compression bakeoff
AUTHOR: Kurt

DESC: which is best
-----
BODY:
I just did a quick test of compression systems on seimsic data.  I
took two files from the EdgeTech xstar system (2 channels) which are
19MB and 164MB.  My requirements were that the program be in fink.
The results:
<pre>
19705360 EEL5kL05.xstar

18789077 EEL5kL05.xstar-compress.Z
15711363 EEL5kL05.xstar-zip.zip
15704232 EEL5kL05.xstar-gzip.gz
13580310 EEL5kL05.xstar-7z.7z
13549235 EEL5kL05.xstar-rzip.rz
13539313 EEL5kL05.xstar-bzip2.bz2

TESTING: seismic.xstar

163843600 seismic.xstar
161645283 seismic.xstar-compress.Z
134228345 seismic.xstar-zip.zip
134142038 seismic.xstar-gzip.gz
119504153 seismic.xstar-rzip.rz
119409223 seismic.xstar-bzip2.bz2
118475932  seismic.xstar-7z.7z
</pre>
The winners on size were p7za and and bzip2.  The 7z was super slow,
so I will be sticking to bzip2.  I was surprised that rzip did not do
better, but I think that would be the case if there were a lot more
small files that were almost the same.
<pre>
<font color="0000ff"><strong>#!/bin/bash</strong></font>
<font color="#444444"># Testing for compression programs</font>
<font color="#2040a0">infile</font>=test0.txt
<font color="#2040a0">infile</font>=EEL5kL05.xstar
<font color="#2040a0">infile</font>=seismic.xstar
echo TESTING: <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>

rm -f <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-*

for type in bzip2 gzip rzip zip compress; do
    cp <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font> <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-<font color="#2040a0">$type</font>
done

bzip2 -9 <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-bzip2
gzip -9 <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-gzip
rzip -9 <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-rzip
zip <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-zip.zip <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-zip
compress <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-compress
7za a <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-7z.7z -<font color="#2040a0">mx</font>=9 <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-7z 

ls -l <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font> | awk '{print <font color="#2040a0">$5</font>,<font color="#2040a0">$9</font>}'
ls -lS <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-*.* | awk '{print <font color="#2040a0">$5</font>,<font color="#2040a0">$9</font>}'

rm -f <font color="#2040a0">$infile</font>-*
</pre>
