Question: "What do you do for your job?"

Answer: "I shoot laser beams at mud."

Today Jason and I did our first X-ray Diffraction (XRD) measurements
today.  I know this is not a huge deal for all of you who do XRD all
the time, but it is new for us.

SIO's Analytical Facility has a Scintag XDS 2000, which looks like
this:

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-machine.png">

Bruce Deck setup the instrument for us and ran the software.  Here are
the settings that we used on the front.

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-settings.png">

I created the quick look sample by scraping the dried cube with a
razor blade to fill up a 1 cm square patch around the dimple in the
glass slide.  The slides are to small for the sample holder, so it has
to go in diagonally.  

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-sample.png">

Once the system is closed up with the lead glass shield down and the
interlock disabled, it is time to run the sample.  The measurements
are done in degrees which are actually the emitter and receiver moving
up in angle above the sample.  Here is the emitter:

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-xray-emitter.png">

The software to control the system is DMSNT on windows... it
unfortunately requires a floppy disk to copy the data off.  Here is
the software running.

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-software1.png">

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-software2.png">

Once the software is done running the system through your range (for
me 2 to 50&deg;) it can help you try to identify the materials
present.  Here is the original and baseline corrected data  with an
illite standard below.  The middle graph are the peaks that the
machine has found.  The machine currently has a 0.2&deg; offset.

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2005-10/xrd-results.gif">
