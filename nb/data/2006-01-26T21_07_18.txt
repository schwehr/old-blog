I told Becca that I would show her how much easier it was to do a
chirp pulse in python as compared to C/C++, so here it is.  This is
just a schematic, so do <b>not</b> take this as implying that this is
exactly the pulse shape for the XStar.
<pre>
<font color="#444444">#!/usr/bin/env python</font>
<strong>from</strong> <font color="#2040a0">math</font> <strong>import</strong> <font color="4444FF">*</font>
<font color="#2040a0">samples</font><font color="4444FF">=</font><font color="#FF0000">5000</font>
<strong>for</strong> <font color="#2040a0">i</font> <strong>in</strong> <font color="#2040a0">range</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="#2040a0">samples</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">:</font>
    <font color="#2040a0">x</font> <font color="4444FF">=</font> <font color="#2040a0">i</font>/<font color="#FF0000">100</font>.
    <font color="#2040a0">y</font> <font color="4444FF">=</font> <font color="#2040a0">sin</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="#2040a0">x</font><font color="4444FF">*</font> <font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="#FF0000">1</font><font color="4444FF">+</font>.<font color="#FF0000">7</font><font color="4444FF">*</font><font color="#2040a0">x</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">)</font>
    <font color="#FF4400"># cosine taper</font>
    <font color="#2040a0">y</font> <font color="4444FF">=</font> <font color="#2040a0">y</font> <font color="4444FF">*</font> <font color="4444FF">(</font>.<font color="#FF0000">5</font> <font color="4444FF">-</font> .<font color="#FF0000">5</font><font color="4444FF">*</font> <font color="#2040a0">cos</font> <font color="4444FF">(</font> <font color="#2040a0">i</font> <font color="4444FF">*</font> <font color="#FF0000">2</font><font color="4444FF">*</font>  <font color="#2040a0">pi</font> / <font color="#2040a0">float</font><font color="4444FF">(</font><font color="#2040a0">samples</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">)</font><font color="4444FF">)</font>
    <strong>print</strong> <font color="#2040a0">x</font>, <font color="#2040a0">y</font>
</pre>
I then ran the program like this to see what it produces:
<pre>
./taper.py > taper.dat
gnuplot
gnuplot&gt; plot 'taper.dat' with l
</pre>

This produces a tapered pulse that looks like this:
