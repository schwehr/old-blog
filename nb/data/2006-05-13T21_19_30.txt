In my previous post, I wondered how to get the text within a tag that
comes after a child tag.  Looking through the lxml test suite, I found
test_tail in test_elementtree.py that answered my question.

Or here is what I did in ipython:
<pre>
ipython
In [1]: from StringIO import StringIO
In [2]: f = StringIO('&lt;doc&gt;This is &lt;i&gt;mixed&lt;/i&gt; content.&lt;/doc&gt;')
In [3]: from lxml.etree import ElementTree
In [4]: root = ElementTree(file=f).getroot()
In [6]: print root.text
This is 
In [7]: print len(root)      
1
In [8]: print root[0]  
&lt;Element i at 1178378&gt;
In [9]: print root[0].text
mixed
In [10]: print root[0].tail
 content.
</pre>
The key is that the text is the "tail of the child."  Not exactly what
I expected, but it works.
