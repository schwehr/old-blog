I have been skeptical of X3D ever since I first heard about it.  I
felt like VRML was a step backwards from the OpenInventor ASCII file
format.  X3D has long suffered from poor support and from no one else
around me using it.  I think now the tide is changing for me.  Roland
was looking for a new file format for some of his work and he was
feeling some of the same frustrations I was having.  Things like OBJ
just down have the flexibility to allow for us to do research with the
format.  Inventing our own format has the drawback of working on our
own little island.  Roland got really excited by X3D and really took
off with it.  I've been holding back judgement to see how it worked
for him.  Then came Matt into the mix.  I now have two separate people
who keep showing me how great X3D is.  There is still the issue of
complete support for the file format in 3D programs, but these guys
have shown me first hand that there is enough critical mass for this
format to win out.  The final 1-2 punch was: 
<ol>
<li>doing some additional work in OpenInventor and getting frustrated with the format not being as uniform as XML</li>
<li>Matt gave me a tour of X3D and showed me that it has all the benefits of XML and all the good things that I count on in OpenInventor</li>
</ol>
Now if only Coin3D would read X3D.

Matt has been doing some really awesome ground work that I have to
show off a bit.  Back in the day (aka before I got to CCOM), the
VisLab created some great demos with billboard images in the
Portsmouth Harbor.  They took it one step further and tried a running
mosaic along the water, but the results caused them to conclude that
ribbon mosaics just are not the correct solution.  However, Briana's
masters thesis demonstrated that billboards do work.  The alternative
to billboards are CAD models of a harbor objects.  When done right
(aka by Chris and not by me), critical visual features can be really
powerful.  But there are two drawbacks two CAD models:
<ul>
<li>They take a lot of time (and consequently $$$) to create.</li>
<li>They miss many of the surrounding textural details that are not directly on the buildings.</li>
</ul>
After much discussion between Matt, Colin, and myself, Matt went out
for a day with Capt. Ben and the labs nice digital camera.  He took
hundreds of pictures of all the key features along the harbor
entrance.  The images included sets of photos of the same object but
from different directions.  He then worked up a prototype in GeoZui of
an application that switches which photo is display based on the
direction from which the object is viewed.  Many objects (.e.g light
houses) look very different when viewed from different locations.
This first prototype still relies on the old TCL engine in GeoZui, so 
the idea needs a more modern as TCL has pretty much reached the end of
development.

Here is a quick bit of code that Matt sent me from his initial
explorations of X3D.  The idea is to have either embedded JavaScript
and/or Python handle the switching.
