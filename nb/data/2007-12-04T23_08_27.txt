AUTHOR: Kurt
DATE: 12.04.2007 23:08
DESC: I have gps data
-----
BODY:
I got my new GPS today.  I was a little worried about getting it
working, but it turned out to be very easy.  I didn't even need to
setup a bluetooth library.  I was thinking that I needed to write some
python to talk to the device with something like <a
href="http://lightblue.sourceforge.net">lightblue</a>

I put in the battery and hooked the GPS up to a USB port on my laptop.
I set the switch to navigation mode and hen went looking for the
manual.  Here are the instructions it somes with:
<ol>
  <li> Go to System Preferences and click on the Bluetooth icon.</li>
  <li> Click on settings to ensure BT is turned on and discoverable.</li>
  <li> Select the Devices tab.</li>
  <li> Click on set up new device.</li>
  <li> This brings up the Bluetooth Set Up Assistant.</li>
  <li> Follow the instructions on the set up assistant and enter 0000 as passkey.</li>
  <li> The device should be listed in the devices window.</li>
  <li> Locate the BT serial utility application in the utilities folder, double click to open.</li>
  <li> Click on new.</li>
  <li> Assign the port a name, select outgoing, click on select device.</li>
  <li> Select the iBT-GPS and highlight SPP slave in the right hand window.</li>
  <li> Click select button and select the port type RS-232.</li>
  <li> Click OK to finish setup.</li>
</ol>
Here is what I did...  System preferences.  Click on the bluetooth
icon. Select Devices.  Click on setup new device.

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2007-12/i.trek-z1-gps-1.png"/>

Press Passkey Options.

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2007-12/i.trek-z1-gps-2.png"/>

Set to "Use a specific passkey".  enter 0000

<img src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2007-12/i.trek-z1-gps-3.png"/>

And then I was good to go.  A device appeared in /dev!
<pre>
ls -l /dev/tty.iBT-GPS-SPPslave-1 
crw-rw-rw-   1 root  wheel   10,  48 Dec  4 22:51 /dev/tty.iBT-GPS-SPPslave-1
</pre>
