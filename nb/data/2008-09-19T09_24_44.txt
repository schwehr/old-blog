Halpern et al., 2008: <a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/cgi/content/full/319/5865/948">A Global Map of Human Impact on Marine Ecosystems</a> [sciencemag]
<pre>The management and conservation of the world's oceans require
synthesis of spatial data on the distribution and intensity of human
activities and the overlap of their impacts on marine ecosystems. We
developed an ecosystem-specific, multiscale spatial model to
synthesize 17 global data sets of anthropogenic drivers of ecological
change for 20 marine ecosystems. Our analysis indicates that no area
is unaffected by human influence and that a large fraction (41%) is
strongly affected by multiple drivers. However, large areas of
relatively little human impact remain, particularly near the
poles. The analytical process and resulting maps provide flexible
tools for regional and global efforts to allocate conservation
resources; to implement ecosystem-based management; and to inform
marine spatial planning, education, and basic research.</pre>
The data and more detailed figures are available here: <a href="http://www.nceas.ucsb.edu/GlobalMarine/impacts">http://www.nceas.ucsb.edu/GlobalMarine/impacts</a>
