<b>Update:</b> Val pointed me to this client: <A href="http://www.microsoft.com/mac/products/remote-desktop/default.mspx">Remote Desktop Connection Client for Mac 2</a> [microsoft].  Works pretty well.

Gone are the days when every computer I used had x11 and all apps were
x11 based.  Way back when, I could just ssh -X to any computer and
work as if I was on the console.  Now I have to manage Windows, Mac
and Linux computers all over a variety of networks.  Linux is easy.
The rest present challenges.  Here is some of what I've been doing.
Thanks to Val for adding another solution to the mix!  He pointed me
to the Mac OSX 10.5 built in "Screen Sharing".

<a href="http://arstechnica.com/reviews/os/mac-os-x-10-5.ars/12">Mac OS X 10.5 Leopard: the Ars Technica review</a> - Screen sharing

I have been using something like this to connect to Windows from a Mac.  You can specify a percent of your screen or a fixed pixel size.
<pre>
% <b>fink install rdestop</b>
% <b>rdesktop -g 1700x800 192.168.8.182</b>
% <b>rdesktop -g 60% 192.168.8.182</b>
</pre>
<img title="rdesktop talking to a windows xp computer" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-09/rdesktop.png"/>

There are lots of other possible ways including Apple Remote Desktop
(ARD; $$$), VNC (of which there are many clients), <a
href="http://logmein.com">LogMeIn</a> (Is there a good ref on what
they are actually doing?), pc2tv, xwd (xwindows dump), and many
more...  It all depends on the environment, available budget and what
you are trying to do.
