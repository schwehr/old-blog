I used the Speakeasy <a
href="http://www.speakeasy.net/speedtest">speedtest</a> to evaluate my
Fairpoint 768Kbps DSL connection.  I got 756kbps down and 135 kbps up.
Which is pretty good.  I'm running 802.11g locally, which should be
much faster.  For another take, I ran on a server at work:
<pre>iperf -s -p 60001</pre>
Then connected from the behind the DSL connection:
<pre>iperf -c somewhere.unh.edu -p 60001</pre>
The results...
<pre>=------------------------------------------------------------
Client connecting to nrwais2.schwehr.org, TCP port 60001
TCP window size:  129 KByte (default)
=------------------------------------------------------------
[  4] local 192.168.2.100 port 59242 connected with 132.177.xx.xx port 60001
[ ID] Interval       Transfer     Bandwidth
[  4]  0.0-18.3 sec   288 KBytes   129 Kbits/sec
</pre>
It's too much hassle right now to setup the reverse test for the download
speed.

After upgrading to the high speed uption ("3 Mb"), I get 2855 kbps down and 682 kbps up from the speakeasy test.
