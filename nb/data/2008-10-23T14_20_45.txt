I'm getting an N-AIS feed of some vessels.  I just got asked about how
often vessels are being sampled.  Here some quick analysis for one
vessel just off of Boston (MMSI=366956050).  First I wrote a program
to take my XYMT (long lat MMSI UTC_unix_timestamp) and convert it to a
delta time.
<pre><tt><i><font color="#9A1900">#!/usr/bin/env python</font></i>
<!-- -->
prev <font color="#990000">=</font> None
<b><font color="#0000FF">for</font></b> line <b><font color="#0000FF">in</font></b> <b><font color="#000000">file</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font><font color="#FF0000">'one-vessel.xymt'</font><font color="#990000">):</font>
    f <font color="#990000">=</font> line<font color="#990000">.</font><b><font color="#000000">split</font></b><font color="#990000">()</font>
<!-- -->
    val <font color="#990000">=</font> <b><font color="#000000">float</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>f<font color="#990000">[</font><font color="#993399">3</font><font color="#990000">])</font>
    <b><font color="#0000FF">if</font></b> prev <font color="#990000">==</font> None<font color="#990000">:</font>
        prev <font color="#990000">=</font> val
        <b><font color="#0000FF">continue</font></b>
    delta <font color="#990000">=</font> val <font color="#990000">-</font> prev
    prev <font color="#990000">=</font> val
<i>    <b><font color="#0000FF">if</font></b> delta <font color="#990000">&gt;</font> <font color="#993399">200</font><font color="#990000">:</font>
        <b><font color="#0000FF">continue</font></b></i>
    <b><font color="#0000FF">print</font></b> delta
</tt></pre>
I ran the above first without the <i>italic</i> bit above to plot all
times.  This gives spikes where ever the vessel left the search
bounding box or the data logging system was offline.  You will see
most of the points are close to 0.  The channel I'm using is supposed
to decimate reports to one every 60 seconds.  This removes the
negative delta times that I get from the full rate data feeds (due to
USCG clock skews on the site controllers for each receive station).

<img title="When did this vessel leave the area?" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-10/nais-reports-vessel-left.png"/>

Then I reran the code with the <i>italic</i> code that throws out the
times over 200 seconds.  This shows the stability of the 60 second
reporting.  I am not sure where the timestamp is coming from, but it is probably one of:
<ul>
  <li>At the site controller next to the receiver</li>
  <li>At the USCG aggregator</li>
  <li>At my end in the USCG software that receives from the agregator via TCP/IP</li>
</ul>
This plot shows that the time between samples that I log ranges from
60 to 180 seconds.

<img title="Hard to see what the sample rates are" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-10/nais-reports-sample-rate.png"/>

Finally a closeup that gives a sens of how stable the report interval is:

<img title="With a subset of time, we can see the N-AIS reporting rate" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-10/nais-reports-sample-rate-closeup.png"/>

It takes a histogram to see what is going on.  For samples that are
less than 200 seconds apart, there are samples centered around 60
seconds, 120 seconds, and 180 seconds.  Perhaps my TCP/IP assumption is wrong?
I need to run a packet sniffer to check.
