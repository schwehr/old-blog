I've just been checking out the US Maritime Administration (MARAD) <a
href="http://marview.gov">MarView</a> website.  Note:  I am using FireFox
3.0.4 to view the website and it looks like it is designed just for IE.

First, I went to the public front page and registered for an account.

<img title="MarView Front Page" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview.gov.png"/>

Once I got an account, this is the screen that I got:

<img title="MarView" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-home.png"/>

I went to check out the Vessels tab.  There are links to vessel
traffic, which turned out to be a link to AisLive.

<img title="MarView Vessels" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-vessels.png"/>

Here is the Crisis Tracking page.  Note that the formatting will
probably look fine on IE.

<img title="MarView" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-crisis.png"/>

Under data sets, there are a lot of links to public web pages.  For
example, here is what is under Modeling and Simulation.

<img title="MarView Modeling and Simulation" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-modeling.png"/>

I didn't see anything of interest in RSS feeds.  These are all pretty
standard and I'll stick to reading RSS using Vienna on my Mac.

<img title="MarView" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-rss.png"/>

They have a database of locks and lock status that pops up MS Virtual
Earth with a placemark for the lock.  Make sure to zoom out a bit to
see the lock as I think these are based off of the street address assigned to the lock that can sometime be a ways away from the actual lock.

<img title="MarView" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2008-12/marview-locks.png"/>
