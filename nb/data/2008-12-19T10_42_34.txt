BODY:
I didn't do the best job of taking notes yesterday.  Thanks to <a href="http://www.tacoma.washington.edu/ias/about/faculty_detail.cfm?employee_id=1147">Peter Selkin</a> for letting me post his list: <i>Ten Things I Learned at AGU's Virtual Globes Sessions</i>
<ol>
<li>Search for environmental data with SciScope - very limited now, but a great concept: <a href="http://www.sciscope.org/">http://www.sciscope.org/</a></li>
<li>Help students write KML for Google Earth with the KML Interactive Sampler: <a href="http://kml-samples.googlecode.com/svn/trunk/interactive/index.html">http://kml-samples.googlecode.com/svn/trunk/interactive/index.html</a></li>
<li>Capture and modify Google Earth topography in SketchUp (as long as your viewpoint is close to the ground in GE...). Does this mean I might be able to import some topography (e.g. from lidar data) and make a Collada file out of it, then view it in GE? I still haven't got a straight answer.</li>
<li>Explore the oceans, draw profiles, examine oceanographic data with GeoMapApp: <a href="http://www.geomapapp.org/">http://www.geomapapp.org/</a></li>
<li>Identify solar and wind energy potential with 3Tier's FirstLook: <a href="http://firstlook.3tier.com/">http://firstlook.3tier.com/</a></li>
<li>Find coastal landmarks with Kurt's GeoCoastPilot (even though it wasn't what he was presenting!): <a href="http://ccom.unh.edu/vislab/projects/GeoCoastPilot.html">http://ccom.unh.edu/vislab/projects/GeoCoastPilot.html</a></li>
<li>Make thematic maps in KML from public data, or use the Thematic Mapping Engine to make your own: <a href="http://www.thematicmapping.org/">http://www.thematicmapping.org/</a></li>
<li>Put Google Charts in Google Earth balloons or use them as placemark icons: <a href="http://earth.google.com/outreach/kml_entry.html#tA%20Googol%20of%20Heat%20Beneath%20Our%20Feet">http://earth.google.com/outreach/kml_entry.html#tA%20Googol%20of%20Heat%20Beneath%20Our%20Feet</a></li>
<li>Beware of design flaws in your KML that might make people laugh at your lack of forethought: <a href="http://googleearthdesign.blogspot.com/">http://googleearthdesign.blogspot.com/</a> (Rich Treves' example of the UK government's "Do Drugs" pencil was hilarious)</li>
<li>Use NASA WorldWind, formerly PC-only, on any platform (just a preview so far) and make extensions for it because it's open source: <a href="http://worldwind.arc.nasa.gov/">http://worldwind.arc.nasa.gov/</a></li>
</ol>
