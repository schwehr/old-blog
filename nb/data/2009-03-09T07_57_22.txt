Wired has a wiki article of interest:  <a href="http://howto.wired.com/wiki/Open_Up_Government_Data">Open Up Government Data</a>

I've been attacking the small pieces of government data on the fronts
of trying to discover what we have on disks and how do we make
metadata generation easier.  This is a chance to talk about the top
down approach.  Not yet addressed are things like:
<ul>
  <li>How do we give researchers and data processors professional credit for releasing data</li>
  <li>How do we make finding data in NGDC and GeoSpatialOne stop easy?</li>
  <li>What do we do about the current ITAR process that hangs up a lot of data?</li>
  <li>We often run into the problem of finding the metadata to a some data, but never actually find the data</li>
  <li>Can we get 3D computer model release built into the contracting process for custom build government contracts?</li>
  <li>How do we maintain the privacy of citizens, while releasing lots of data?</li>
  <li>Can we make this whole process inside the government cost less, while accomplishing more?</li>
</ul>
Speaking of data... soon we shall be able to ask Steve Wolfram the answer to any question at <a href="http://www.wolframalpha.com/">WolframAlpha</a>.  Will he know the answers any better than AskJeeves?  And on the privacy side, check out <a href="http://epic.org/privacy/cpni/">CPNI
(Customer Proprietary Network Information)</a> for information on opting out of 
Verizon's sharing of your personal information.
