I just tried out the idea of using my N-AIS feed to check network
uptime and it worked much better than I though it would.  For each
day, I counted the unique seconds in the data timestamps and divided
by the number of seconds in a day.  This is not the best way to
measure the uptime, but it is interesting.  The main causes of outages are:
<ol>
  <li>Power outages to our building (we are on the wrong site of the train tracks)</li>
  <li>Loss of network connectivity at CCOM or UNH</li>
  <li>Loss of the network between Groton, CT and UNH</li>
  <li>Three hangs of my Mac desktop that is logging before I upgraded to 10.5</li>
  <li>One outage of 1 hour as I upgraded to mac osx 10.5</li>
</ol>
The last item comes from the fact that I am only allowed to have one
specific machine subscribing to N-AIS, so any computer maintanence
requires data loss.  I have no information for the USCG network
performance, so I can't comment on the impact of any outages there.
We sometimes get big storms here and it has taken as much as a day and
a half to get out power back.  This graph starts at 01-Jan-2008 and
goes through 04-Apr-2009.

<img title="data received - fraction of a day " withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-04/nais-ccom-uptime.png"/>

Overall uptime for the entire datalogging system as seen by CCOM:
<pre>seconds = [int(d.split()[1]) for d in file('sec-per-day.dat')]
len(seconds)
<i>Out: 458  # Days</i>
sum(seconds) / (len(seconds)*24*60*60.)
<i>Out: 0.97799285844250361</i></pre>
The overall system was not working for 10 days worth of time during those 458 days.

Note that this is not a good way to judge any one component of the system.
The timestamps are being added regionally by the uscg and I have no
way of separating out the failure modes from just the timestamped NMEA strings.

Contrast that with one of our field data loggers running Ubuntu 7.10.
<pre>% <b>uptime</b>
 06:58:57 up 408 days, 17:31,  1 user,  load average: 0.00, 0.07, 0.23</pre>
The field machine is at Adams Point in Jackson lab and has a small UPS
that has made it through all the local power hickups.  Plus the machine
logs locally with periodic dumps back to ccom.  Too bad the sensors
attached to it are not as reliable.

NOTE: I found that my script didn't notice entire days with no data.
For 2008-06-{13,14}, there was nothing, so my % uptime is over estimated.
