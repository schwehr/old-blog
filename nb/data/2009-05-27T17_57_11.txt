We have a windows machine that is controlling a device via the serial
port.  On boot, the computer did not want to talk to the serial port.
I couldn't figure out how to list what is owning the serial port
(which is pretty easy on Linux...).  Les and Will showed me what
turned out to be the solution to getting the serial port back.  But, I
still don't know what the caused problem.  Solution: Disable the com
port.  Wait 5 minutes for whatever it was give up and die.  Enable the
com port.

First I kill the app that wasn't working and fired up Hyperterm.

<img title="Hyperterm - no luck" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-05/windows-xp-com1-unavailable.png"/>

Disable the port and let it sit...

<img title="" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-05/windows-xp-disable-com1.png"/>

Enable the port...

<img title="" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-05/windows-xp-enable-com1.png"/>

And now everything works as expected.  Looking at the task manager,
there was no change in processes.

Update 2009-July-08: Thanks to Hector M. for pointing out the solution:
<pre>[T]he problem with the grouchy Windows PC serial port is due to
Windows scanning the serial ports on the PC on startup for serial mice
- notice the alarmed serial mouse present in your screenshot. There is
a Microsoft utility ComDisable (<a href="http://support.microsoft.com/kb/819036">http://support.microsoft.com/kb/819036</a>) 
which can be used to disable the scan on startup for selected ports. </pre>
