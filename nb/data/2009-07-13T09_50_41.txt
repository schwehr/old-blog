In my web page comparison last week, I compared <a
href="http://explore.noaa.gov">http://explore.noaa.gov</a> to <a
href="http://jpl.nasa.gov">http://jpl.nasa.gov</a>.  Turns out that is
the Office of Exploration and Research and what I should have been
looking at <a
href="http://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/">http://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/</a>
which is a lot better.  The difference being that I put NOAA in my
search last week.  Ocean Explorer is a much more interesting web site,
but why are there two and what is the difference?  This other page has
links to YouTube, Twitter and more, but isn't well organized nor why
would I know to go to that web page?  Is it linked from <a
href="http://noaa.gov">http://noaa.gov</a>?  NOAA should definitely put
some energy into making it obvious what cool things different parts of NOAA 
are up to!

<img title="Ocean Explorer home page" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-07/noaa-oceanexplorer-web.png"/>

Clicking on the RSS feeds shows that there is lots there, but
yikes... too much text.

<img title="Ocean Explorer feeds" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2009-07/oe-feeds.png"/>

The page could use some images of people doing exciting things like
surveying 3D bathymetry (yeah, I'm biased), driving survey lauches,
working on shorelines, examing the criters that they have collected,
dredging the depths, driving AUVs and ROVs, etc.  Those are the things
that I get to see while working with NOAA that the public should get
to see right off.
