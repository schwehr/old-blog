BODY:
Lubchenco, the new NOAA administrator, has caused a big flurry around
the phrase "Marine spatial planning."  I head this phrase a good
number of times in the last month and today it's in The Boston Globe:
<a
href="http://www.boston.com/news/science/articles/2009/07/20/noaa_chief_says_new_ocean_uses_creating_conflicts/">NOAA
chief says new ocean uses creating conflicts</a>
<pre>...
"Marine spatial planning" refers to the process of determining which
kind of activity should be allowed in which parts of the oceans,
including which portions of the oceans should have single or multiple
uses, while still protecting fragile marine ecosystems.
<!-- -->
Lubchenco said better planning, with the help of extensive public
hearing and comment, could help minimize conflicts in the future,
particularly as developers seek to place wind farms or tidal energy
projects in deeper waters farther from shore.
...</pre>
A <a
href="http://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Anoaa.gov+"marine+spatial+planning">Google
Search of Marine Spatial Planning (MSP) at NOAA</a> only has 211 hits
so far, but I'm sure that will be increasing over the next few months.
