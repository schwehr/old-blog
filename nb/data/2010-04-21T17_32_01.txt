Check out <a
href="http://www.linux.com/news/software/applications/301412-jon-maddog-halls-picks-for-todays-six-best-oss-projects">Jon
'maddog' Hall's Picks for Today's Six Best OSS Projects</a>.  I just
met John a couple of weeks again when a group of us gave a group
presentation at UNH Manchester (<a
href="http://www.unhm.unh.edu/about-us/news-events/unh-manchester-events/index.php#sidore">Open
Access, Open Content in Education</a> - <a
href="http://www.unhm.unh.edu/media/saul-sidore-lecture-series/OpenAccessPanel-SidoreSeries-Mar-2010.ppsx">PPT</a>).
The talks were long so there wasn't any real room for discussions, but
still it was great to meet the other panel members (and visit UNHM for
the first time).

In Jon's picks, he mentioned <a
href="http://www.openstreetmap.org/">Open Street Map (OSM)</a>, which
is great.  I championed OSM for ERMA a while back and it now has OSM
as an optional background.  Nothing like being unable to mark a bridge
out during a disaster or adding emergency roads where the Google team
is just not allowed.  But better yet, he mentioned <a
href="http://www.sahanafoundation.org/">Sahana</a>, which is open
software for distaster management.  It's PHP and MySQL.  I will have
to check it out!  Looks like it has been around quite a while, but of
course the open source consultants that I've been working with (<a
href="http://www.reprojected.com/geoblog/">hi Aaron</a>) already know
about the project and know some of those involved.
