I don't get a lot of feedback from the field about how ERMA is used.
Usually, all I get comes in when something stops working.  A recent
gCaptain's article, <a
href="http://gcaptain.com/maritime/blog/bps-not-really-top-secret-oil-spill-command-center">BP's
(not really) Top Secret Oil Spill Command Post</a>, used a USCG photo
in a recent post: <a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/deepwaterhorizonresponse/4755585221/">Enhanced Mobile Incident Command Post</a>
<pre>MOBILE, Ala. - Coast Guard personnel operate communication equipment
to disseminate oil spill coordinates to vessels conducting oil
skimming operations in the Gulf of Mexico Friday, July 2, 2010. The
enhanced mobile incident command post's primary function is to provide
sheltered work space, voice and data connectivity as well as command
and control capabilities on a mobile platform. Coast Guard photo by
Petty Officer 3rd Class Walter Shinn.</pre>
I noticed the display on the left looks an aweful lot like ERMA.  I zoomed
in and discovered that it is indeed ERMA.  Just not much in the way of
interesting layers.

<img title="ERMA at the USCG AL Mobile Command Post" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-07/dwh-erma-at-uscg-cmd-AL.png"/>

So, if you are out in the field with ERMA and see it in use, I would
really appreciate you snap a photo and send it my way.  Better yet,
post it yourself, set your sharing info (e.g. one of the <a
href="http://creativecommons.org/choose/">Creative Commons
licenses</a>), and send me a link.  Thanks!
