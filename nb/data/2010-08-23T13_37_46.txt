I've fixed up my quake visualization some, added volcanoes and added
plate boundaries.  I think it's starting to look pretty good.  First, the
volcanoes.  I downloaded the Excel spreadsheet (ahem... not a great way to
publish data) and did a save as CSV.  I then used the python <b>csv</b> module
to read in the volcanoes and spit out KML.  I had made my own icon, but decided
that Google's looks tons better.  The plate boundaries took a little more work.
<pre><b>wget http://serc.carleton.edu/files/eet/seismicwave/plates_quakes_countries.zip
unzip plates_quakes_countries.zip
ogr2ogr -f SQLite plates.sqlite plat_lin.shp
./platesdb2kml.py
sqlite3 plates.sqlite .schema</b></pre>
The trickiest part is that the geometry is stored in the SQLite database as <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-known_text">WKB</a> (Well Known Binary).  The database schema:
<pre><tt><b><font color="#0000FF">CREATE</font></b> <b><font color="#0000FF">TABLE</font></b> geometry_columns <font color="#990000">(</font>
     f_table_name <font color="#009900">VARCHAR</font><font color="#990000">,</font>
     f_geometry_column <font color="#009900">VARCHAR</font><font color="#990000">,</font>
     geometry_type INTEGER<font color="#990000">,</font>
     coord_dimension INTEGER<font color="#990000">,</font>
     srid INTEGER<font color="#990000">,</font>
     geometry_format <font color="#009900">VARCHAR</font>
<font color="#990000">);</font>
<b><font color="#0000FF">CREATE</font></b> <b><font color="#0000FF">TABLE</font></b> <font color="#FF0000">'plat_lin'</font> <font color="#990000">(</font>
     OGC_FID INTEGER <b><font color="#0000FF">PRIMARY</font></b> <b><font color="#0000FF">KEY</font></b><font color="#990000">,</font>
     GEOMETRY <font color="#009900">BLOB</font><font color="#990000">,</font>
     <font color="#FF0000">'margin_typ'</font> <font color="#009900">VARCHAR</font>
<font color="#990000">);</font>
<b><font color="#0000FF">CREATE</font></b> <b><font color="#0000FF">TABLE</font></b> spatial_ref_sys <font color="#990000">(</font>
     srid INTEGER <b><font color="#0000FF">UNIQUE</font></b><font color="#990000">,</font>
     auth_name <font color="#009900">TEXT</font><font color="#990000">,</font>
     auth_srid <font color="#009900">TEXT</font><font color="#990000">,</font>
     srtext <font color="#009900">TEXT</font>
<font color="#990000">);</font></tt></pre>
I used <a href="http://pypi.python.org/pypi/Shapely/">shapely</a> to parse the WKB.  It's a little tricky as some items come back as LineString objects and others as MultiLineStringObjects (with more than one line in them).  I'm not a huge fan of ESRI shape files (shp).
<pre><tt><b><font color="#000080">import</font></b> sqlite3
<b><font color="#000080">from</font></b> shapely <b><font color="#000080">import</font></b> wkb
<b><font color="#000080">import</font></b> shapely
<!-- -->
cx <font color="#990000">=</font> sqlite3<font color="#990000">.</font><b><font color="#000000">connect</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font><font color="#FF0000">'plates.sqlite'</font><font color="#990000">)</font>

<!-- -->

            <i><font color="#9A1900"># multiline</font></i>

                lines<font color="#990000">.</font><b><font color="#000000">append</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>line<font color="#990000">.</font>xy<font color="#990000">)</font>
