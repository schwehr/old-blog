Last Sunday, a bunch of us headed out for a walk in the perfect weather.
We first tried Fox Point in Newington.  Apparently, Newinton does not want to share it's parks with the rest of New Hampshire.  We ignored all the signs and went anyway.

<img title="Fox Point" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/fox-point.jpg"/>

It's pretty, but there isn't that much poit there.  I think I have
finally found the "historic site" that has a sign on 16... the
remnants of the old Piscataqua Bridge.

<img title="Old Piscataqua Bridge site" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/old-piscataqua-bridge.jpg"/>

We headed from Fox Point to Adams Point across Little Bay.  We stopped by JEL to check out the current setup of the tide stations.  Note that the Ohmex TideMet is setup here.

<img title="Ohmex at Jel" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/jel-tide-station1.jpg"/>

I am starting to be strong proponent of ditching stilling wells for
tide stations.  Why are we doing low pass filtering with tide stations
with a physical device?  If we post process the water levels and log
the full rate data, then we can see more the spectrum of energy in the
estuary and we can always use software to produce lower frequency
values.  I would love to hear arguments to the contrary.  One of which
is that, for radar, we need a heated area to have a water surface
without ice.

<img title="JEL tide stations" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/jel-tide-station2.jpg"/>

Here is looking back at Jackson Lab from across the small bay.  The fencing across the area is a part of experiments by Win Watson to use video to track <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_crab">horse shoe crab</a> behavior.

<img title="Horse Shoe run at JEL" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/jel-horseshoe-crab-run.jpg"/>

We ran into these bugs on the walk.  What are they?  If you know what these are, please let me know.

<b>Update 2010-10-11:</b> Springmeyer pointed me to the <a
href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_milkweed_bug">large milkweed
bug</a>, which led me to conclude that these are small milkweed bugs (<a
href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lygaeidae">Lygaeus</a> kalmii).

<img title="Bugs on milk week at Adams Point" withgrayborder="True" src="http://schwehr.org/blog/attachments/2010-10/bugs-at-adams-point.jpg"/>

This is the second time that I've seen something like this.  I ran
into thousands of these back on Sept 11th in Dover on a sidewalk.  I
had thought that the criters in both images were the same, but clearly
they aren't the same.  But both have great red color.
