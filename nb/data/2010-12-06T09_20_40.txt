TITLE: AIS feeds from ships
AUTHOR: Kurt
DATE: 12.06.2010 09:20
DESC: how best to implement

BODY:
Dale gets credit for pointing out that we already have a number of
excellent AIS receive platforms out there... the research and survey vessel
fleet.  When these ships have satellite data connections, we have the
potential for shipping back their AIS receives in real-time or near
real-time.  AIS NMEA messages compress to about 1/4th their original
size, so the data volume might not be too much for some of these
links.  Even if we can't do real-time, we could batch up an hour and
send those logs when the link is available.  Barring satellite
connectivity, we should probably be archiving the data and submitting
it to a national archive to support Coastal and Marine Spatial
Planning (CMSP) efforts.  When vessels hit a shore internet
connection, they can then upload their data to the archive.

The next question is how do we get data back in real-time?  The
options are WiMax/3G, Iridium (probably way too slow/expensive),
satellite links using TCP/IP, scp, secure rsync, ftp, and ???  Would
the USCG AisSource software be able to handle the connectivity
experienced by ships where the link often goes down?  The key thing is
to make sure the data is timestamped properly at receive time with a
clock synced to a GPS.
