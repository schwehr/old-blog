A minor point...

AIS devices are <b>not</b> <a
href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder">transponders</a>.  The
<a
href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder_%28aviation%29">aircraft
units</a> are transponders that get asked for their ID.  AIS devices
transmit under their own control in almost all cases.  In the US, you
will never see the unit be interrogated for a response.  These devices
are technically transceivers.  I know it's a lost cause with radio
vendors using the wrong terminology, but I have to try.

The following messages initiate transponder mode:
<ul>
<li>10 UTC/date inquiry</li>
<li>15 Interogation - request for a particular message</li>
</ul>
Those two are just not used in the US.

If you are looking for a marine transponder, a good example is a <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racon">Racon</a>.

When terminology gets sloppy, things get hard to follow.
