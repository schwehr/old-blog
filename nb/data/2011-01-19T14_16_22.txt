My talk: <a href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/papers/2011-schwehr-amss-dwh-lessons.pptx">2011-schwehr-amss-dwh-lessons.pptx</a>

Here are some lessons learned in my oppinion to try to help
get the discussion going:
<ul>
  <li>Ships of all sizes should have AIS and there must be a way to track which ships are a part of a response</li>
  <li>Have portable AIS systems ready to put on ships (suitcase version)</li>
  <li>Know the bathymetry and be willing to collect your own if necessary.  Have charts, the Coast Pilot and local names</li>
  <li>Have a manual that you get to all of the team.  People coming to the area don't know the local names, physical layout, and available data / resources</li>
  <li>Start using mobile devices to collect data,  position and information.  Avoid handwritten and voice communication to prevent confusion</li>
  <li>Train with actual ships being virtually moved around</li>
  <li>Get necessary data integrated into AOOS, ERMA and other systems before you are in a crisis.  Unlike Deepwater Horizon, initial response time is usually critical</li>
  <li>Train people to work with the data types before a crisis</li>
  <li>PDFs and paper are not good primary delivery devices</li>
  <li>Communication across all the teams makes or breaks the response</li>
  <li>We should have AIS from a couple response vessels forwarded through satellite to supplement normal stations</li>
</ul>
Some are being worked on now, some are not.  What would you add?
