This is pretty interesting news.  Since I started at CCOM, I've been
pushing the idea of cell phone and tablet sized devices along with
being able to track the location on the staff on the bridge of a ship
(e.g. rf id, bluetooth location, <a
href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinect">Kinect</a>, etc).
<a
href="http://www.engadget.com/2011/03/01/ipad-gets-approval-from-faa-to-replace-paper-flight-charts-and-m/">iPad
gets approval from FAA to replace paper flight charts and maps</a>
[engadget].  Thanks to Pam for the link.
<pre>The Federal Aviation Administration is moving with the times, it would
seem, as it has just granted the first approval for the use of iPads
instead of paper charts for informing airline pilots while on
duty. There are already a number of EFB (electronic flight bag)
devices in use, however the iPad is by far the cheapest and most
portable one that's been validated yet. Executive Jet Management, a
charter flight operator, went through three months of testing with the
iPad ...</pre>
And TUAW write <a href="http://www.tuaw.com/2011/02/13/ipad-receives-faa-certification-as-an-electronic-flight-bag">iPad receives FAA certification as an electronic flight bag</a> that says it's the <a href="http://www.jeppesen.com/microsites/jeppesen-mobile-tc/?cid=semjdg04928&kwpg=jeppesen%2bipad">Jeppesen Mobile TC App</a>.
The App is free, <b>BUT</b>: 
<pre>Note: You must have a valid Jeppesen electronic charting serial number
in order to activate new coverages, or update your charts. New
Jeppesen subscription serial numbers are activated within an hour of
purchase.</pre>
