I was just looking at <a
href="http://blogs.plos.org/mfenner/2011/03/07/the-trouble-with-bibliographies/">The
Trouble with Bibliographies</a> by Martin Fenner on PLoS Blogs.  As
usual, there is no mention of capturing the spatial component of
papers.  I've been feeling that while BibTex is currently a strong
standard for bib data, it's very long in the tooth.  It is missing
ways to handle many types of references and I'm never sure if I am 
writing valid BibTex.

I had not heard of <a
href="http://blogs.plos.org/mfenner/2010/09/24/citation-style-language-an-interview-with-rintze-zelle-and-ian-mulvany/">Citation
Style Language</a> (<a href="http://citationstyles.org/">CSL</a>)
before.  While it was created by Bruce D'Arcus, an associate professor
of Geography, I don't see a mention of capturing the spatial or
temporal component of the contents.

<b>Update 2011-Mar-17</b>: Also check out Sean Gillies' post: <a href="http://sgillies.net/blog/1077/beyond-the-pdf-and-scholarly-html">Beyond the PDF and Scholarly HTML</a>
