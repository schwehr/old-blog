BODY:
I am now confident that the patent system is garbage.  Tracking ships
with zoom cameras is now patented with the application being submitted
in 2005.  If you want prior art, look no further than the late 1990's
with the CMU Robotics project <a
href="http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~vsam/">Video Surveillance and
Monitoring</a> and replace the words "human," "car," and "truck" with
the word ship.  In the May 2000 <a
href="http://www.ri.cmu.edu/publication_view.html?pub_id=3325">final
tech report</a>, you will find stuff way more advanced than what is in
the patent.

<a href="http://www.freepatentsonline.com/7889232.html">Patent 7889232</a>: Method and system for surveillance of vessels.  Filed 2005, granted 2011.
<pre>A surveillance system and method for vessels. The system comprises
surveillance means for surveying a waterway; vessel detection means
for determining the presence and location of a vessel in the waterway
based on information from the surveillance means; camera means for
capturing one or more images of the vessel; image processing means for
processing the images captured by the camera means for deriving
surveillance data; wherein the camera means captures the vessel images
based on information from the vessel detection means, the surveillance
data from the image processing means, or both. The images can be used
to classify and identify the vessel by name and category, possibly
also to compare the category with that previously registered for a
vessel of this name. The vessel can be tracked, including by speed and
direction until it leaves the surveyed waterway.</pre>
Dear patent examiners, really?  In 6 years, you couldn't find this prior art?
Object identification and tracking is one of the core areas of computer vision.
Doing this is an obvious thing to try.  The algorithms to make it work reliably (which are <b>not</b> in the patent) can be challenging.
