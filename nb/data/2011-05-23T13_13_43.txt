Today, Slashdot posted <A href="http://news.slashdot.org/story/11/05/23/0335244/Google-Founders-Jets-Caught-On-WSJs-Radar">Google Founders' Jets Caught On WSJ's Radar</a>, which links to the <a href="http://online.wsj.com/article/SB10001424052748704904604576336194116401856.html">WSJ's Air traffic database</a>.  This is much like the analysis that I've been working on for AIS, but aircraft have much less freedom of movement than ships.  See also <a href="http://www.morningstar.co.uk/uk/markets/newsfeeditem.aspx?id=142552112133324">WSJ(5/21) For The Highest Fliers, New Scrutiny</a> in Morningstar with more of the article.

See also: <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Automatic_dependent_surveillance-broadcast">Automatic dependent surveillance-broadcast (ADS-B)</a>

Planes have been trackable for a lot longer than ships, but I've spent
much less time looking at plane, ship, and car/truck tracking
technologies.  And the Amateur Radio crowd has had the <a href="http://www.aprs.org/">Automatic Packet Reporting System (APRS)</a> for a long time.
