And an aside... I liked this post for the shell script rewrite with

<a href="http://www.leancrew.com/all-this/2011/12/erm/">Erm</a> by Dr. Drang.  

He went from this:
<pre><tt><i><font color="#9A1900">#!/bin/tcsh</font></i>
echo -n `pbpaste` <font color="#990000">|</font> perl -e <font color="#FF0000">'while (&lt;&gt;) { s/-//g; print $_; }'</font></tt></pre>
to this (I changed bash to sh and added --noprofile for speed).
<pre><tt><i><font color="#9A1900">#!/bin/sh --noprofile</font></i>
pbpaste <font color="#990000">|</font> tr -dc <font color="#FF0000">'[:digit:]'</font></tt></pre>
I would also say to @macgenie's tweets: <a
href="https://twitter.com/#!/macgenie/status/142327964539949057">first</a>
and <a
href="https://twitter.com/#!/macgenie/status/142347515755364354">second</a>... if
you are going to use tcsh for scripts, you should at least switch to
/bin/csh -f rather than tcsh for everything.  Not a big deal in a one
off, but if later you morph into a frequently called script, that will
reduce overhead.  But why would anyone want to use csh/tcsh?  This
coming from me who was a die hard {t,}csh user from 1990 to 1999 until
I was forced to really start using bash during Mars Polar Lander.
