<pre>Q2: What are the five things that you think that other scholars
should do to help challenge the status quo wrt scholarly publishing?</pre>
So what have I done?  
<ul>
<li><a href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/papers/">My papers are online</a> on my site</li>
<li>I have a paper in progress that is in a public DVCS: <a
href="https://gitorious.org/toils-of-ais/toils-of-ais">Toils of
AIS</a> (Sorry ESR!  I need to work on it ASAP!)</li>
<li>I post a good amount of my research <a href="http://schwehr.org/blog/">on my blog</a></li>
<li>I have <a
href="http://scholar.google.com/citations?user=fXbCGw0AAAAJ">my Google
Scholar Citations</a> and <a
href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/papers/schwehr.html">JabRef
HTML pub list with BibTex entries</a></li>
<li>I'm going to work for Google on Ocean, where I hope to show off
all sorts of peoples' research</li>
<li>I post my source code and class notes online with open source
licenses: <a href="https://github.com/schwehr">schwehr on github</a> and <a
href="https://bitbucket.org/schwehr/">schwehr on BitBucket</a></li>
<li><a href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/rt/">My research
tools class</a> has <a
href="http://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL7E11B34616530F5E">videos</a>
and <a href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/rt/audio/">audio from
lecture</a> under a Creative Commons license</li>
</ul>
That would be my list of 5 (+2 extra).  What are yours?

I think that a reasonable alternative to the current closed journals
would be for me to retain the copyright and allow the journal an
exclusive (e.g. not even my web page) to my paper for 1 year.  After
that, I release my paper under an Creative Commons license.
