I wanted to know where the vdatum zones that I had downloaded from
NOAA are located.  Turned out to be pretty easy to parse the "met"
files that are located in the many subdirectories that come from the
regional zip.  Here is the met file for Maine and New Hampshire:
<pre><b>ls MENHMAgome01_8301</b>
dtl.gtx  MENHMAgome01_8301.bnd  MENHMAgome01_8301.met  mhhw.gtx  mhw.gtx  mllw.gtx  mlw.gtx  mtl.gtx  tss.gtx
<b>cat MENHMAgome01_8301/MENHMAgome01_8301.met</b>
ID=0
source=MENHMAgome01_8301
desc=Maine, New Hampshire, Massachusetts - Gulf of Maine, Version 01
tide_epoch=1983-2001
maxlat=45.568
maxlon=294.4202
minlat=39.89
minlon=288.8
spacinglat=0.0017
spacinglon=0.0017
height=3341
width=3307</pre>
Here is my lazy python parser that just slams everything into a dictionary with
the key coming from the left hand side of the '=' and the contents being what
was on the right.  I try to convert the number to an integer or float by brute
force.
<pre><b><font color="#0000FF">def</font></b> <b><font color="#000000">parse_met</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>filename<font color="#990000">):</font>
  r <font color="#990000">=</font> <font color="#990000">{}</font>
  <b><font color="#0000FF">for</font></b> line <b><font color="#0000FF">in</font></b> <b><font color="#000000">open</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>filename<font color="#990000">):</font>
    line <font color="#990000">=</font> line<font color="#990000">.</font><b><font color="#000000">rstrip</font></b><font color="#990000">()</font>
    <i><font color="#9A1900">#print line</font></i>
    key<font color="#990000">,</font>val <font color="#990000">=</font> line<font color="#990000">.</font><b><font color="#000000">split</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font><font color="#FF0000">'='</font><font color="#990000">)</font>
    <b><font color="#0000FF">try</font></b><font color="#990000">:</font>
      val <font color="#990000">=</font> <b><font color="#000000">int</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>val<font color="#990000">)</font>
    <b><font color="#0000FF">except</font></b><font color="#990000">:</font>
      <b><font color="#0000FF">pass</font></b>
    <b><font color="#0000FF">if</font></b> <b><font color="#0000FF">not</font></b> <b><font color="#000000">isinstance</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>val<font color="#990000">,</font>int<font color="#990000">):</font>
      <b><font color="#0000FF">try</font></b><font color="#990000">:</font>
        val <font color="#990000">=</font> <b><font color="#000000">float</font></b><font color="#990000">(</font>val<font color="#990000">)</font>
      <b><font color="#0000FF">except</font></b><font color="#990000">:</font>
        <b><font color="#0000FF">pass</font></b>
<!-- -->
    r<font color="#990000">[</font>key<font color="#990000">]</font> <font color="#990000">=</font> val
<!-- -->
  <b><font color="#0000FF">return</font></b> r</pre>
I then made a little for loop for each file and used the python '''{var}'''.format(var='my value') style python templating
to produce some simple KML for Google Earth.
