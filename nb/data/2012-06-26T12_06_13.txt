AUTHOR: Kurt

BODY:
Back in 2007, Phil McGillivary and I wrote an IEEE/MTS paper, <a
href="http://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/~schwehr/papers/SchwehrMcGillivaryOceansMTS2007.pdf">Marine
Ship Automatic Identification System (AIS) for Enhanced Coastal
Security Capabilities: An Oil Spill Tracking Application</a>.  Shortly
after, I <a
href="http://schwehr.org/blog/archives/2007-11.html#e2007-11-27T14_39_11.txt">linked</a>
to a <a
href="http://www.marinelink.com/news/article/coast-guard-to-test-oil-from-seven-ships-in/325434.aspx">USCG
note in MarineLink</a> saying that the USCG had used NAIS to figure
out who spilled oil.  Since then, there have been papers talking about combining AIS and satellite imaging to catch dumpers.

Last week, SkyTruth and SpaceQuest published a nice demonstration of
doing exactly that:

<a href="http://blog.skytruth.org/2012/06/bilge-dumping-busted-using-satellite.html">Bilge Dumping? Busted Using Satellite Images and AIS Data</a> [SkyTruth]<br/>
<a href="http://gcaptain.com/bilge-dump-csi-skytruth-ids">Bilge Dump CSI: AIS Helps ID Vessel Responsible for Oil Slick Off Angola</a> [gCaptain]

It's nice to see a concept get one step closer to common practice.
