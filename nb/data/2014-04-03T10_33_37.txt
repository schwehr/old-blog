BODY:
Just did this remotely on a mac.  I had historically left autologin
running.  This was blocking some system admin tasks.  Here is what I
did to kick myself out from the commandline on an ancient install of
Mac OSX 10.6.  10.9 likely has things the same.

<a href="http://www.cnet.com/news/how-to-disable-automatic-log-in-via-the-command-line-in-os-x/">how-to-disable-automatic-log-in-via-the-command-line-in-os-x/</a> and <a href="http://superuser.com/questions/40061/what-is-the-mac-os-x-terminal-command-to-log-out-the-current-user">what-is-the-mac-os-x-terminal-command-to-log-out-the-current-user
</a>
<pre><b>ssh myhost
ps -aux | grep $USER | wc -l</b>
24
<b>defaults read  /Library/Preferences/com.apple.loginwindow autoLoginUser</b>
schwehr
<b>sudo defaults delete /Library/Preferences/com.apple.loginwindow autoLoginUser
sudo osascript -e 'tell application "System Events" to log out'
ps -Ajc | grep loginwindow | awk '{print $2}' | sudo xargs kill
ps aux | grep $USER | wc -l</b>
4</pre>
